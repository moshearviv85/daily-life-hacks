Somebody said "just hit 100 grams" like the grocery store prices protein by the slogan. I priced five days that actually land there. Lentils, peanut butter, and oats are $1.57. Ground beef, cheddar, and bacon are $8.33. Same job, about 5.3 times the money.

The 50 gram version of this question is already on [what 50 grams of protein costs per day](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/). This page doubles the target and stays inside three files: protein-per-dollar-2026.csv, dairy-protein-per-dollar-ranked-2026.csv, and meat-per-dollar-protein-ranked-2026.csv. If a food is in more than one of them, the grams per dollar match. I didn't shop a new store for the exercise.

## How much does 100 grams of protein per day actually cost at the grocery store?

Five grocery templates land near 100 grams. Lentils, peanut butter, and oats cost $1.57. Eggs, Greek yogurt, milk, and oats cost $3.22. Drumsticks, eggs, and cottage cheese cost $2.82. Chicken breast, tuna, and mozzarella cost $3.91. Ground beef, cheddar, and bacon cost $8.33. The [National Academies](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10490/dietary-reference-intakes-for-energy-carbohydrate-fiber-fat-fatty-acids-cholesterol-protein-and-amino-acids) RDA is 0.8 grams per kilogram, a baseline, not this target.

Protein in a line is the CSV's protein per 100 grams times the gram weight. Cost is those protein grams divided by the published grams per dollar. The total is the unrounded sum, then one decimal for protein and the cent for money. A line rounded on its own can miss the total by a tenth of a gram. That's rounding, not a second price. The [methodology](/methodology/) spells out the edible fraction.

## What the RDA is doing on this receipt

The [National Academies RDA](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10490/dietary-reference-intakes-for-energy-carbohydrate-fiber-fat-fatty-acids-cholesterol-protein-and-amino-acids) for protein is 0.8 grams per kilogram per day for adults. At 70 kilograms, that's 56 grams. One hundred grams is well above that baseline for a lot of adults, and below it for a much heavier person. The number on this page is the one the grocery question asked. It isn't a prescription, and it isn't the 50 gram label Daily Value either.

Let the lentils or the drumsticks carry the grams when you can. Keep the tuna can for the night you aren't cooking. The beef day is about 5.3 times the lentil cart, and the chart already shows that. The RDA stays a footnote, not a requirement.
