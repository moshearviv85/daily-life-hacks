The yeast packet is empty and somebody still wants a sandwich. That's a Tuesday problem, not a fermentation project. This sourdough discard sandwich bread uses no added yeast. Baking powder and baking soda lift a 9-by-5-inch loaf, and you pull it when the center hits 195 to 200F.

One loaf, ten slices, from 227 grams of 100% hydration discard. The calorie line is about 202 per slice, from the flour, butter, milk, and egg weights on [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168894/nutrients). It's sandwich bread. It isn't a sourdough boule, and it doesn't need an overnight rise.

## Why this loaf doesn't wait on yeast

Unfed discard is sour. Baking soda uses that acid the same way the [discard pancakes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/) foam the second the soda hits the batter. Baking powder brings its own acid, so the loaf still rises if the jar is milder than you expected. Together they do the lifting. Nothing has to double first.

Bake it once the batter is mixed. A long rest, the kind you'd give a yeast dough, spends the soda on the counter. I did that once with a quick loaf and the middle came out like a damp biscuit. The oven was fine. The clock wasn't.

There's no commercial yeast in the bowl. The starter still carries wild yeast, so "no yeast" here means you don't add a packet and you don't wait for a rise. The [no-yeast discard list](/sourdough-discard-recipes-no-yeast/) is the rest of that shortcut. This page is the sandwich loaf.

## Sourdough discard sandwich bread in grams

The recipe card is the batch. This is the same list, so you can shop from the table.

| Ingredient | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Sourdough discard, 100% hydration | 227 g |
| All-purpose flour | 280 g |
| Whole milk | 150 g |
| Unsalted butter, melted | 50 g |
| Large egg | 1 (50 g) |
| Granulated sugar | 15 g |
| Fine salt | 6 g |
| Baking powder | 9.2 g |
| Baking soda | 2.3 g |

At 100% hydration, those 227 grams of discard are about 113.5 grams of flour and 113.5 grams of water. The [discard gram guide](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/) is the cup chart if another card says cups. Stir the jar flat before you weigh it. A bubbly cup lies.

The 280 grams of all-purpose flour sits on top of the flour already in the jar. [FDC 168894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168894/nutrients) puts a cup of that flour at 125 grams, so weigh the 280.

Fifty grams of butter is a small amount on purpose. I used to pour in half a stick, and the slice then fell apart under a piece of cheese. Fifty grams keeps it tender. It doesn't turn the crumb into a biscuit. [FDC 171287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients) counts one large egg as 50 grams, and that egg is the other piece of structure.

9.2 grams of baking powder is 2 teaspoons on [FDC 172803](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172803/nutrients), which lists 4.6 grams per teaspoon. 2.3 grams of baking soda is half a teaspoon on [FDC 175040](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175040/nutrients). 6 grams of fine salt is 1 teaspoon of table salt on [FDC 173468](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173468/nutrients). If your spoon doesn't match those weights, believe the scale.

15 grams of sugar is there for browning, not for a sweet loaf. If you want cinnamon, that's a different pan: the [no-yeast cinnamon rolls](/sourdough-discard-cinnamon-rolls-no-yeast/).

## A 9-by-5 pan and 195 to 200F

Heat the oven to 350 degrees F before you mix. Line a 9-by-5-inch pan with parchment and grease it lightly. Whisk the dry ingredients, stir in the discard, milk, melted butter, and egg, and stop when the flour disappears. A smooth batter is the goal. A workout isn't.

Spoon it in and smooth the top. Bake 50 to 60 minutes. Start checking at 50. The center should read 195 to 200F, and a toothpick should come out without wet batter. A dark top and a wet middle means more time, not a higher rack. Tent the loaf with foil if the crust is already brown.

Cool 10 minutes in the pan, then a rack. Slice it warm, not steaming. A hot crumb gums the knife and then you blame the recipe. Toast a slice if you're stacking turkey. This isn't the open, chewy crumb of a dough that rose all afternoon. What you're slicing is sourdough discard sandwich bread, and the [baking-powder pizza crust](/easy-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough-no-yeast/) is the thin version of the same idea if the jar is going to dinner instead of lunch.

## What 202 calories actually counts

The number is the raw ingredient weights divided by 10. Discard counts as half flour, because 100% hydration is half flour and half water, and the water has no calories. Flour is [FDC 168894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168894/nutrients). Butter is [FDC 173430](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173430/nutrients). Whole milk is [FDC 171265](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171265/nutrients). One slice is about 202 calories, 5.2 grams of protein, 1.1 grams of fiber, and 408 milligrams of sodium. The nutrition box under the recipe lists every row.

Five grams in the slice isn't the protein decision. Eggs, milk, and beans don't cost the same per usable gram, and that comparison is [plant versus animal protein per dollar](/plant-vs-animal-protein-cost-per-dollar/).

A smear of butter after slicing, oil on the pan, and whatever you put between two slices aren't in that total. The batter should look thick. Wet-looking batter is how this loaf bakes.

## After it cools

Cool it all the way before you wrap it. A warm loaf in a bag steams itself soft. A few days on the counter is a baker's quality guess. [FoodKeeper](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/foodkeeper-app) doesn't publish a homemade quick-bread row, so don't treat that as a federal date. Freeze slices you won't finish. [Food held at 0°F can be kept indefinitely](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts), and freezer times are quality.

Use discard from an established starter. Mold, pink or orange patches, or a rotten smell means the jar is done. Weighing it carefully doesn't fix that. [The FDA advises against tasting raw flour, dough, or batter](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/raw-doughs-raw-deal-and-could-make-you-sick). Bake the center to 195 to 200F, then decide if you like it.

If you only have about 113 grams, don't pretend this pan will shrink. Make the skillet flatbread on the [beginner discard recipes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/) instead. Half a loaf is a smaller pan and a shorter bake, and only if you weigh half of everything.
