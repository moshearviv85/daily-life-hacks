Sunday night, four jars, and a yogurt tub that has to earn its shelf space. I used to call this breakfast and then eat a slice of toast at 10 because the jar was mostly sweetened oats and a wish.

These high-protein overnight oats are the version with the grams written down. One jar is 30.8 grams of protein, 11.2 grams of fiber, and $1.01. No egg. The batch is four jars, so the pot is four times the jar. If you want a hot plate instead of a cold one, the [brown lentil and pearled barley soup](/brown-lentil-and-pearled-barley-soup-high-fiber/) is the other end of the same grocery file.

## What is in one jar of high-protein overnight oats?

A jar is one fourth of the batch. Protein and fiber are the USDA grams for that weight. Cost is the package price divided by the package grams, times the grams in the jar. The jar total is those costs added, then rounded to the cent. Line costs in the chart are rounded first, which is why they look like $1.02.

## Where the $1.01 and the grams come from

Greek yogurt is 10.19 grams of protein per 100 grams and 0 grams of fiber ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients)). The price row is a 32 ounce tub for $3.36, 907 grams, in protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. That row says July 2026.

Rolled oats are 13.15 grams of protein and 10.1 grams of fiber per 100 grams ([FDC 173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients)), a 42 ounce canister for $3.36. Chia is 16.54 grams of protein and 34.4 grams of fiber per 100 grams ([FDC 170554](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170554/nutrients)), a 32 ounce bag for $9.44. Peanut butter is 22.21 grams of protein and 5.0 grams of fiber per 100 grams ([FDC 174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients)), a 40 ounce jar for $4.97.

Chia isn't in the protein file. The 2.48 grams are the USDA protein times 15 grams. The fiber row's 33.1 grams per dollar is the whole bag's rate, and this jar uses 15 grams of it.

Oats, chia, and peanut butter keep the fiber-study price, and those rows leave the month cell blank. I didn't relabel a blank as July. The nutrition box may still say July 2026 because the file name has 2026 in it. The yogurt row is the one that actually says July. The same foods as a shopping list, not a jar, are on the [breakfast staples page](/breakfast-staples-per-dollar/) and the [$1 protein versus fiber chart](/one-dollar-of-protein-vs-one-dollar-of-fiber/).

The published rates are 46.6 grams of protein per dollar for the oats, 27.5 for the yogurt, 50.7 for the peanut butter, and 33.1 grams of fiber per dollar for the chia. The jar doesn't eat a dollar of each.

## Stir it like you mean it

Chia clumps if you tap the spoon twice and call it mixed. I stir until the seeds disappear into the yogurt, then I stir once more because I don't trust the first look. Split it into four jars. Half a cup of cold water in each one. The 2 cups in the recipe are that water, and the water isn't in the calorie count.

Eight hours in the fridge. Overnight is the cook time, which is why the card says 0 minutes on the stove. In the morning the oats should bend, not crunch. If the jar looks like spackle, it needs water, not a lecture. Salt is optional. I use a pinch because plain yogurt and peanut butter can taste oddly sweet without it.

This isn't a chia pudding. A chia pudding is mostly seeds and milk, and the [chia pudding variations](/chia-pudding-variations-for-breakfast/) use a quarter cup of seeds in one jar. Fifteen grams of chia here is a fiber scoop, not the whole breakfast. The yogurt is the body of the jar.

## What the $1.01 doesn't buy

A banana, honey, and the granola you were going to sprinkle on top. I left them off so the card would match the jar I priced. Add them and the $1.01 is no longer the jar.

The [egg-free breakfast chart](/high-protein-breakfast-without-eggs/) prices 30 grams of protein from these foods without building the jar. This page is the jar. Four jars is the Sunday batch. One jar is a quarter of the weight, and the serving button does that division.

I eat them cold, standing at the counter, which is the honest review. They're thick, a little salty if you bothered with the pinch, and they don't need an egg to get past 30 grams. That's the whole pitch.
