That's the whole story in one sentence: eggs are good value, not great value, and everything that beats them lives in the dry goods aisle. Here's the top of the ranking eggs didn't crack:

## What actually beats eggs on protein per dollar?

Sixteen foods, and the theme is obvious: if it's dry and it sits on an unrefrigerated shelf, it probably wins. Every dried legume beats eggs, from red lentils at 56.0 grams per dollar up to brown lentils at 77.7. Whole wheat flour leads the study at 96.0, nearly three times the egg number. We ranked the plant half of the store on its own in [the cheapest plant protein sources](/plant-protein-per-dollar-ranked/), where the dry bags lead and the refrigerated products with "protein" printed on the label finish last. Both spaghettis (53.4 and 42.9), peanut butter (50.7), oats (46.6), dry roasted peanuts (39.8), brown rice (36.6), and pearled barley (36.3) all finish ahead too. White rice, at 29.4, does not. Neither does a loaf of 100% whole wheat bread, at 20.1.

Exactly one meat cracks the list above eggs: bone-in chicken drumsticks at 50.3 grams per dollar, and that's after subtracting the bone. Everything else at the meat counter loses to the egg carton, which you can watch happen cut by cut in [the meat-only protein ranking](/meat-per-dollar-protein-ranked/).

## What do eggs beat?

Almost the entire animal side of the store, where eggs finish second only to bone-in drumsticks in [our cost ranking of 21 animal proteins](/animal-protein-per-dollar-ranked/). Here's how eggs stack up against the foods people usually cross-shop:

| Food | Protein per $1 |
|---|---|
| Eggs (large) | 34.4 g |
| Mozzarella (part-skim) | 30.1 g |
| Whole milk | 28.5 g |
| Chicken thighs (boneless) | 27.7 g |
| Greek yogurt (nonfat) | 27.5 g |
| Chicken breast (boneless) | 24.6 g |
| Canned tuna (in water) | 22.4 g |
| Ground beef (80/20) | 11.3 g |
| Bacon | 9.4 g |

Eggs beat chicken breast (24.6) by roughly 40 percent per dollar. They beat every fish in the sample: canned tuna at 22.4, canned pink salmon at 21.6, frozen tilapia at 20.9, sardines at 20.2. They beat every dairy product, including whole milk at 28.5 and cheddar way down at 18.1. And they triple ground beef, which limps in at 11.3 for the 80/20 and 11.2 for the 93/7. Bacon holds last place among all 49 foods at 9.4 grams per dollar. Whole wheat flour, at 96.0, is about ten times that. Nobody buys bacon for protein, but it's fun to have it in writing.

## So are eggs still worth buying for protein?

Rank 17 undersells the convenience: eggs are the cheapest protein in this sample that asks almost nothing of the cook. A dozen holds about 75 grams of protein for $2.19, call it 18 cents an egg, [about 12.6 grams of protein in the standard two egg breakfast](/how-much-protein-in-two-eggs/), and there's no soaking, no simmering, no cutting board. Skip the pan entirely and it's a different list, one that [peanut butter, canned beans, and the dairy tubs](/no-cook-protein-per-dollar/) run instead. Beans beat eggs on paper; eggs beat beans at breakfast when you're barely awake and negotiating with the coffee maker.

They're also hard to get bored of. A [sheet pan breakfast hash](/sheet-pan-breakfast-hash-with-eggs-and-sweet-potatoes/) feeds a table from one pan, [cauliflower fried rice with eggs](/cauliflower-fried-rice-with-eggs/) turns them into dinner, and a good [egg sandwich](/healthy-egg-sandwich-add-ins-toppings/) is the entire reason some of us get out of bed.

## How do you squeeze more value out of the egg habit?

Pair eggs with the foods ranked above them instead of below them. Eggs over [savory oatmeal](/savory-oatmeal-bowls-with-eggs-and-avocado/) puts the 34.4 next to oats at 46.6, and the egg sandwich on 100% whole wheat bread (20.1 grams of protein per dollar) is still a cheap breakfast. The bread just doesn't beat the eggs on protein per dollar. Then let beans take a couple of dinners a week and the grocery math quietly improves without anyone feeling deprived.

That's the honest verdict: eggs aren't the budget champion the internet says they are, but they're the best protein deal that comes with an off switch for effort.
