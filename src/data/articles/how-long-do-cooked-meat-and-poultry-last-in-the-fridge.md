The drumsticks were the cheap protein. Thirty grams for $0.60. Then Thursday showed up, the container was still on the middle shelf, and I wanted the sale price to count as extra time. It doesn't.

Cooked meat and poultry are 3 to 4 days in a fridge held at 40°F or below. That's the leftovers row on the [FoodSafety.gov cold-storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts). Raw poultry and raw ground meat are 1 to 2 days. The dollar figures next to those clocks come from meat-per-dollar-protein-ranked-2026.csv. The filename stays in the sentence. This page doesn't link the file. Grains and beans have their own windows on [how long cooked grains and beans last](/how-long-do-cooked-grains-and-beans-last-in-the-fridge/).

## How long do cooked meat and poultry last in the fridge?

Cooked chicken, turkey, beef, pork, and meat casseroles last 3 to 4 days at 40°F or below. The freezer quality window for cooked meat or poultry is 2 to 6 months at 0°F. Raw poultry and raw ground meat last 1 to 2 days in the fridge. Raw beef and pork chops and roasts last 3 to 5 days. None of those windows start if the food sat out past 2 hours.

![Range chart of refrigerator days and freezer months for cooked and raw meat beside the cost of 30 grams of protein, drumsticks at $0.60 and ground beef at $2.65](/images/how-long-do-cooked-meat-and-poultry-last-in-the-fridge-chart.jpg)

Chart credit: fridge and freezer bars are the cold-storage chart. The dollar bars are 30 divided by grams of protein per dollar in the meat file, rounded half up to the cent. Not a photograph, and not an AI illustration.

The solid part of a range is the first day or month. The lighter part is the rest of the published range, not a guess at the middle. Poultry pieces sit at 9 months because that's the piece row, a single number, not a range I averaged.

Table: Refrigerator and freezer windows from the FoodSafety.gov cold-storage chart

| Food | Fridge at 40°F or below | Freezer at 0°F, quality | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooked meat or poultry | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 6 months | Leftovers row, cooked meat or poultry |
| Soups and stews with meat | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months | Soups and stews, vegetable or meat added |
| Raw chicken or turkey, whole | 1 to 2 days | 1 year | Fresh poultry, whole |
| Raw chicken or turkey, pieces | 1 to 2 days | 9 months | Fresh poultry, pieces |
| Raw ground meat and ground poultry | 1 to 2 days | 3 to 4 months | Hamburger, ground meats, and ground poultry |
| Raw beef or pork chops and roasts | 3 to 5 days | 4 to 12 months | Fresh beef, veal, lamb, and pork |

The chart says freezer times are for quality. Food held at a steady 0°F can sit longer than the month count and still be safe, and it can also taste like the freezer. The fridge days are the argument.

The [FSIS leftovers page](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) agrees on 3 to 4 fridge days and then says 3 to 4 months in the freezer. The cold-storage chart says 2 to 6 months for the same cooked meat or poultry. I won't pretend those are one sentence. If you want a single freezer number, 3 to 4 months is the leftovers page. If you want the chart's range, it's 2 to 6, and it's about quality.

## The days don't start until the pot is cold

FSIS is blunt about the first two hours. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is above 90°F. The [danger zone](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) is 40°F to 140°F. A pot that sat out past the hour doesn't get to borrow day 3.

A stockpot is the usual failure. The lid looks like a plan and the center stays warm. Split the batch into shallow containers. Hot food can go straight into the fridge. Letting a pot "stop steaming" on the counter is the hour you were supposed to use for cooling, spent on waiting.

Reheat leftovers to 165°F. Sauces and soups get a rolling boil. That's for food that was cooled on time. It isn't a refund for a tray that spent the afternoon next to the stove.

Cook temperatures are a different chart, the [FSIS safe minimums](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart). Poultry, including ground turkey, is 165°F. Ground beef and ground pork are 160°F. A pork roast or a pork chop is 145°F and then a 3 minute rest. I keep those next to the fridge row because a raw chop's 3 to 5 days is not a cooked chop's 3 to 4, and ground turkey doesn't get the ground-beef temperature just because both of them are ground.

Two of those rows are proxies, and I don't want them laundered into a cleaner sentence. ## What I do with the container

Label the lid with the food and the day it was cooked. Not the day I found it. If we'll eat it inside 3 days, it stays in the fridge and I don't litigate day 4. If the week is longer than the row, it goes to the freezer the same night, while it still tastes like the thing I cooked.

A rotisserie bird starts its 3 to 4 days when it leaves the hot case. A raw pack of ground turkey doesn't get the cooked row just because I meant to brown it. The cheap cut is still the cheap cut. It just doesn't get a longer fridge.
