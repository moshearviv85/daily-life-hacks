The peas were still cold. Cold isn't a date. I wanted the bag to grant me the rest of the week because it hadn't turned into soup yet. FoodKeeper doesn't work like that.

Purchased frozen vegetables, the Vegetables row in Food Purchased Frozen, are 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator, and the published tip says that recommendation is for storage after cooking. The freezer line from the date of purchase is 10 to 18 months. The after-thawing cells on that same row are empty. If you wanted a single number for "it thawed, now what," the dataset didn't type one in for vegetables as a group.

## How long do thawed frozen vegetables last in the fridge?

Cook them, and the refrigerator recommendation is 3 to 4 days. Leave them as a thawed, uncooked bag of mixed vegetables, peas, or broccoli, and FoodKeeper doesn't give that product an after-thawing day count. Frozen edamame is the exception in the same category: 1 week after thawing, and 2 years in the freezer from purchase.

The [FoodKeeper app](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/foodkeeper-app) is the public face of that dataset. Product 174 sits with deli and prepared foods. Its subtitle is the cooked-vegetable case: leftovers without meat, fish, poultry, or egg. Same 3 to 4 refrigerator days, and a shorter freezer, 1 to 2 months, because you're freezing a cooked dish.

I keep the clocks apart. A bag that's still frozen is on the 10 to 18 month line, and the tip says to keep it frozen and cook it as directed. A pan I already cooked is on the 3 to 4 day line. Thawed and still uncooked isn't a third number on the vegetables row. The cell is blank.

## The counter is two hours, not a fridge day

FSIS draws that line in [Freezing and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety). Don't leave food outside the refrigerator longer than 2 hours. Above 90°F, that shrinks to 1 hour. The [danger zone](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) is 40°F to 140°F. The fridge you're thawing into has to be at 40°F or below, or the FoodKeeper days never start.

A bag that sat on the counter through dinner doesn't get to rejoin the 3 to 4 day row because the peas still look like peas. Whether you can refreeze a bag that thawed in the refrigerator, and why the counter version is done, is [can you refreeze frozen vegetables after thawing](/can-you-refreeze-frozen-vegetables-after-thawing/). I'm not repeating that yes-or-no here. This page is the clock after the bag is already in the fridge, and the dollars if you miss it.

Cooked meat and poultry use their own 3 to 4 day leftovers row. That's [how long cooked meat and poultry last in the fridge](/how-long-do-cooked-meat-and-poultry-last-in-the-fridge/). A skillet that mixed the broccoli with chicken follows the meat clock, not a hope that the vegetable half lasts longer.

Green beans are $2.18 and 10.8 g of fiber per dollar. They have no protein row, so I don't invent one.

## Cook the thawed bag while the recommendation still applies

If the bag thawed in the refrigerator and you're going to eat it as a vegetable, cook it and start the 3 to 4 days. That's the tip on the purchased-frozen row, word for word: the refrigerator recommendation is for storage after cooking. A pot of plain vegetables then sits on the leftovers row too, 3 to 4 days in the fridge and 1 to 2 months if you freeze the cooked batch.

I write the cook date on the lid, not the day I found the bag sweating on the counter. Past day 4, the cooked pan goes into the freezer the same night, inside that 1 to 2 month leftovers window. A bag that never thawed stays on the 10 to 18 month line.

Edamame can sit a week after thawing because its own row says so. The peas can't borrow that week. A $2.32 bag and a $2.87 bag fail on the same blank cell. The chart only tells you which failure costs more.
