The bag of lentils and the bag of barley were both open, which is how a lot of my dinners start. I wanted a bowl that would still look like dinner at 7, not a garnish project. This brown lentil and pearled barley soup is that pot.

One bowl, and a bowl here is one sixth of the pot, is 16.0 grams of fiber, 18.9 grams of protein, and $0.43 of the foods the price files actually cover. It's under $0.70 before you even argue about the oil. If you want the cold jar instead of the pot, the [high-protein overnight oats](/high-protein-overnight-oats-chia-greek-yogurt/) use the same kind of gram math on breakfast.

## What does one bowl of lentil barley soup contain?

The pot is 300 grams of dry brown lentils, 360 grams of pearled barley, 180 grams of carrots, and 150 grams of onion, plus 1 tablespoon of olive oil and 2 cloves of garlic. Divide by six. Water, salt, and pepper aren't in the nutrient table. Water is water. Salt to taste has no gram weight on this card.

## The four prices, and the two that aren't

Brown lentils are 24.63 grams of protein and 10.7 grams of fiber per 100 grams. The bag is 16 ounces for $1.44, the 77.7 grams of protein per dollar and 33.7 grams of fiber per dollar on the published rows. Pearled barley is 9.91 grams of protein and 15.6 grams of fiber per 100 grams, a 16 ounce bag for $1.24, 36.3 and 57.1 grams per dollar.

Carrots are a 2 pound bag for $1.58, 16.1 grams of fiber per dollar. Onions are $1.09 a pound, 7.1 grams of fiber per dollar. Those prices are in fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. Lentils and barley match protein-per-dollar-2026.csv at the same package price.

## One pot, and the barley needs the time

Onion and carrots first, in the oil, until the onion goes soft. Garlic for a minute. Then the dry lentils, the dry barley, and 10 cups of water. No soak. If you soak the barley and forget to say so, the pot goes thick on a different clock and this fiber math is for the dry weights, not a soaked cup.

Simmer 35 to 40 minutes with the lid ajar. I start checking at 30. The lentils go soft first. Stir so the barley doesn't glue itself to the bottom. If the pot looks like porridge while the barley is still firm, add water. Extra water doesn't change the fiber.

Salt at the end. I undersalt it once every winter and spend the meal wishing I'd tasted it. Pepper the same way. This isn't a curry, and it isn't trying to be one. The [lentil curry](/lentil-curry-high-fiber-vegan-dinner/) is the spiced pot. This one is lentils, barley, onion, and carrot, and it tastes like that.

## Leftovers, and what this pot isn't

Cool it within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if the kitchen is above 90°F. [FSIS leftovers guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) gives leftovers 3 to 4 days at 40°F or below. A mixed pot doesn't get to borrow the longer FoodKeeper window for plain rice. The [cooked grains and beans page](/how-long-do-cooked-grains-and-beans-last-in-the-fridge/) is where those two clocks are set next to each other. I freeze the bowls I won't eat inside 3 to 4 days.

This isn't the split pea pot, and it isn't the mushroom barley risotto. The number I'd remember is the bowl. Sixteen grams of fiber, just under 19 grams of protein, and $0.43 of the foods I could price. The oil is real. It just isn't in the spreadsheet.
