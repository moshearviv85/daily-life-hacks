Someone asked which one has the most fiber: bran flakes, bananas, peas, or chia seeds. I had a banana on the counter and a bag of frozen peas that was not trying out for the cereal aisle. The bowl and the receipt don't pick the same winner.

Chia seeds have the most fiber per 100 g of that four: 34.4 g ([FDC 170554](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170554/nutrients)). Bran flakes are 18.3 g. Frozen green peas are 4.5 g. A raw banana is 2.6 g. Crude wheat bran, the ingredient behind the flakes, is higher still at 42.8 g per 100 g ([FDC 169722](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169722/nutrients)). Rolled oats are the oatmeal in this price file. They lose per 100 g. They win per dollar.

## Which has the most fiber, bran flakes, chia, peas, or a banana?

Chia. Per 100 g, it's chia, then bran flakes, then frozen peas, then the banana. Wheat bran would take the crown, and it isn't priced, so the chart leaves that dollar bar blank.

Three questions, three orders. "What contains the most" is the 100 g column. "What did I eat" is the portion. "What should I buy again" is the dollar.

## A serving is not 100 grams

A cup of wheat bran is 58 g and 24.8 g of fiber. That's a baking scoop, not breakfast. An ounce of chia is 28.35 g and 9.8 g of fiber. Forty-five grams of bran flakes, the day menu's "about 1.5 cups," is 8.2 g. The flakes beat that chia spoon only because 45 g is bigger than an ounce.

A half cup of dry rolled oats is 40.5 g, half of the 81 g USDA cup: 4.1 g of fiber for $0.11. A medium banana is 118 g edible and 3.1 g of fiber. Half a cup of frozen peas is 67 g, half of the 134 g cup, and it's 3.0 g. They look tied in the bowl. Per 100 g, the peas have more fiber. The banana is just bigger.

Bran flakes stay a close proxy. [FDC 173888](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173888/nutrients) is POST Bran Flakes. The priced box was a 17.3 oz store brand at $2.98. No generic bran-flakes record exists. The 30.1 g per dollar sits in the table with that flag. It isn't a clean win.

## The dollar ranking is ruder than the bowl

Rolled oats return 35.8 g of fiber per dollar. Chia returns 33.1 g. Bran flakes return 30.1 g. Frozen peas return 17.6 g. Bananas return 11.6 g. Wheat bran returns nothing here. The file has no row.

The chia ounce costs $0.30 for 9.8 g. You're buying a denser spoon, not a cheaper gram. The half cup of oats is $0.11 for 4.1 g. The [10 gram price list](/cheapest-ways-to-add-10-grams-of-fiber-to-any-meal/) already sets these add-ins next to berries. This page is the cereal-aisle cut, banana and peas included. The banana's $0.26 keeps the peel in the price through the 0.64 edible fraction. The 2.6 g per 100 g is the flesh.

## The protein is a bonus

None of these portions is a high-protein breakfast. The wheat-bran cup has 9.0 g of protein because 58 g of a food with 15.55 g per 100 g is still a pile of bran. The half cup of oats has 5.3 g, from 13.15 g per 100 g on [FDC 173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients). The ounce of chia has 4.7 g, from 16.54 g per 100 g, and chia isn't in the protein-per-dollar file. The flakes have 4.5 g, from the 9.9 g on FDC 173888, also missing from that file. Peas have 3.5 g. The banana has 1.3 g. Eat it because you wanted a banana.

## What you can stir into yogurt

Chia wants a stir and a wait. Leave the ounce dry on the yogurt and you'll chew seeds like they owed you money. The bowl that already does this is the [yogurt parfait with the chia counted in the cup](/high-fiber-yogurt-parfait-for-breakfast/).

Wheat bran wants a soak. The [bran muffins](/high-fiber-bran-muffins-that-taste-good/) mix it with buttermilk and leave it 10 minutes. A cup of crude bran on a finished bowl is sawdust with a trophy. Skip it.

Bran flakes are already cereal. Milk or yogurt is the bowl, not a 10-minute soak. They aren't a stand-in for the crude bran in that batter.

Rolled oats aren't a sprinkle either. A half cup tossed into a smoothie at the last second stays chewy. Cook them, or let them sit. The cooked-cup comparison is [oatmeal versus grits](/oatmeal-vs-grits-fiber-content/).

Frozen peas thaw. They don't belong in the yogurt. The banana gets sliced. No soak. About 3 g of fiber, and you're done.

## Where these numbers come from

Fiber per 100 g and fiber per dollar for the five priced rows are fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. The 45 g flakes, 8.2 g and $0.27, match the day-menu row in fiber-day-cost-2026.csv. Oat and pea protein are protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. Chia protein, banana protein, wheat bran, and the cup and ounce weights come from the USDA cache. Bran flakes protein is the 9.9 g on FDC 173888. The banana cost divides by the 0.64 edible fraction first. Rounded half up. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the files.
