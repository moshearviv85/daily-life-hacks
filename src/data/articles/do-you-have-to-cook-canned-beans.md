No. A 15.5 ounce can of black beans is fully cooked before it ever reaches the shelf, which is why 88 cents buys you about 26.5 grams of protein that you can rinse and eat cold with a fork. Heating canned beans is a flavor decision. It is not a safety one.

This trips people up constantly, and the confusion is fair. Dry beans genuinely will hurt you raw. Canned beans are the same bean, already handled.

## Are canned beans already cooked?

Yes, and the reason is the whole point of a can. Low acid foods like beans are sealed and then heat processed under [the FDA's thermal processing rule for hermetically sealed containers](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-113). That heat step is what lets a can sit in a cupboard for years without a refrigerator anywhere near it.

So when you open a can, you are opening a cooked bean sitting in the water it cooked in. That is also the honest reason a can costs more per gram than a bag. Dry black beans returned 81.0 grams of protein per dollar in our July 2026 pricing. The canned version returned 30.1. You are paying somebody else to do the soaking and then paying to ship the cooking water home. The full spread across every bean we checked both ways is in [canned versus dry beans](/canned-vs-dry-beans-cost/).

## How long should you cook canned beans?

Until they are hot. That's the entire instruction.

Five to ten minutes in a pan over medium heat, or ninety seconds in the microwave with the lid cracked. If they're going into a chili or a soup that's already bubbling, stir them in during the last ten minutes so they don't collapse into paste. Beans that have been simmering for forty minutes in a tomato sauce have not become safer. They've just become mush.

The one time I'd argue for real heat is when you want texture. Beans crisped in a hot pan with oil until the skins split are a different food from beans warmed in their own liquid, and the crisped version is better on almost everything.

## Can you eat canned beans straight from the can?

Yes, and honestly this is one of the more useful facts in a grocery store. In our [no-cook protein ranking](/no-cook-protein-per-dollar/), an 88 cent can of black beans is the cheapest package on the list, and it returns 30.1 grams of protein per dollar. Twenty-six grams of protein, about 53 percent of the FDA's 50 gram Daily Value, with no stove involved.

The fiber number is the one that surprises people. That same can holds roughly 30.3 grams of fiber, past the whole 28 gram Daily Value, which is more than most people get in a day from anything. If you're not used to that, one full can in a sitting can be uncomfortable. Ramp up slowly, and read [how to increase fiber intake without gas](/how-to-increase-fiber-intake-without-gas/) before you commit to a can a day.

One rule that isn't about cooking. The FDA advises throwing out cans that are [swollen, leaking, punctured, deeply rusted, or dented badly enough that they won't stack or open normally](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely). A dinged corner is fine. A can with a bulging lid is not a can you open.

## Why rinse them if they are already cooked?

Taste, mostly. The canning liquid is starchy and faintly metallic, and it will flatten whatever you put the beans into. Rinsing also washes away some of the sodium.

The exception is when you want that starch. A can of chickpeas dumped in with its liquid will thicken a stew, and the same logic applies in [three bean chili](/hearty-vegetarian-chili-with-three-beans-and-corn/) where the sauce benefits from the body. Otherwise, colander, cold water, thirty seconds.

Worth knowing: our gram figures describe the 439 gram net weight of the can as sold, liquid included. Drain and rinse and you'll eat somewhat less than the table says. Read those numbers as the ceiling rather than the plate.

## What to do with the can once it is open

Get it into something. Cold beans go straight into [corn salsa with no cooking at all](/quick-black-bean-and-corn-salsa-no-cooking-required/), or onto a salad, or into a bowl with rice and hot sauce. Chickpeas become [homemade hummus](/high-fiber-hummus-recipe-homemade/) in the time it takes the food processor to stop rattling, or [crispy roasted chickpeas](/crispy-roasted-chickpeas-high-fiber-snack/) if you want the snack version.

Whatever you don't use, move out of the can and into a covered container in the fridge, ideally within two hours of opening it. The can itself is not a storage vessel once the seal is broken. We laid out how long the leftovers actually keep in [how long opened canned beans last](/how-long-do-opened-canned-beans-last/), and what a can is worth against a bag of dry beans in [how much a can of beans costs](/how-much-is-a-can-of-beans/).

A can of beans is the rare pantry item that asks nothing of you. No soak, no simmer, no plan. That's most of why it's worth keeping four of them behind the pasta.
