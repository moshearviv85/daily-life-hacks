I opened the carton and got the little paper flap. No eggs. The breakfast protein target didn't resign. It just sat there, next to a tub of yogurt and a jar of peanut butter, waiting for me to do math instead of scrambling.

A high-protein breakfast without eggs is a grocery problem, not a personality. The eight staples below are already priced in breakfast-staples-per-dollar-2026.csv and protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. Where those eggs would have landed on the same receipt is [what 30 grams of egg protein costs next to the rest of the cart](/eggs-vs-everything-protein-value/).

## How do you hit 30 grams of protein at breakfast without eggs?

Skip the carton. A 170 gram tub of plain nonfat Greek yogurt is 17.3 grams of protein ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients)). Stir in 40 grams of dry oats and 32 grams of peanut butter and the bowl is 29.7 grams, about $0.88. Three more plates below clear the same line. The rates are in breakfast-staples-per-dollar-2026.csv and protein-per-dollar-2026.csv.

That's the whole trick. You aren't hunting a magic [30 gram food](/protein-per-dollar-adjusted-for-quality/). You're stacking ordinary breakfast foods until the grams add up, then reading the price that was already published.

## What do eight egg-free staples cost per 30 grams?

Protein in the serving column is the CSV's protein per 100 grams times the gram weight, rounded to one decimal. Cost for 30 grams is 30 divided by the published grams per dollar, rounded to the cent ([methodology](/methodology/)). I didn't invent a new price.

The 170 gram yogurt tub, the 244 gram cup of milk, the 32 gram peanut butter scoop, and the 26 gram bread slice (52 grams for two) are the household weights already on the [7-day high-protein grocery list](/high-protein-grocery-list-50-grams/). Cottage cheese at 225 grams is the small-curd cup on the [cottage cheese versus Greek yogurt page](/cottage-cheese-vs-greek-yogurt-protein-uses/), which is 25.0 grams of protein at 11.12 per 100 grams ([FDC 172179](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172179/nutrients)). Oats are 40 grams because that's the weight I multiplied, not because a cup has one official number. Almonds are 28 grams. Tofu is 100 grams.

Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whole milk, and extra-firm tofu are July 2026 price rows. Whole wheat bread is the May 2026 BLS average on the same file, and I didn't relabel it as July. Rolled oats, peanut butter, and almonds keep the audited fiber-study price, which has a blank month cell. Five of these foods also sit in breakfast-staples-per-dollar-2026.csv at the same grams per dollar. Bread, tofu, and almonds are only in protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. The [breakfast staples page](/breakfast-staples-per-dollar/) is the morning cut of that shopping list. The [methodology page](/methodology/) is where the edible fraction is spelled out.

Peanut butter at $0.59 for 30 grams looks like the winner until you picture 135 grams of it, which is the portion that actually holds 30 grams ([FDC 174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients)). That's a lot of peanut butter before coffee. The chart is the rate. The plate still has to be a plate.

## Four breakfasts that land near 30 grams

Each line cost is that food's protein grams divided by its published grams per dollar. The plate cost is the sum, then rounded to the cent. Protein is the sum of the unrounded grams, then one decimal.

| Breakfast | What's on it | Protein | Cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Yogurt bowl | 170 g Greek yogurt ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients)), 40 g rolled oats, 32 g peanut butter | 29.7 g | $0.88 |
| Cottage toast | 225 g cottage cheese, 52 g whole wheat bread | 31.5 g | $1.27 |
| Tofu and oats | 200 g extra-firm tofu, 80 g rolled oats | 30.5 g | $1.69 |
| Milk, oats, peanut butter | 244 g whole milk, 60 g rolled oats, 64 g peanut butter | 29.8 g | $0.72 |

The yogurt bowl is the one I'd actually eat on a Tuesday. Yogurt, a scoop of oats, peanut butter stirred in until it looks like something you'd pay $9 for and then regret. Here it's $0.88 and 29.7 grams ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients), [FDC 173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients), [FDC 174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients)).

Cottage toast is the salty one. A 225 gram cup of 4% cottage cheese on two slices is 31.5 grams and $1.27. The bread isn't doing the protein job. It's doing the "this is breakfast" job. Cottage cheese still wins the equal-weight argument on the [cottage cheese versus Greek yogurt comparison](/cottage-cheese-vs-greek-yogurt-protein-uses/). Per dollar, the yogurt tub is the tighter 30 gram buy.

Tofu and oats is the plate that costs the most and asks you to own a skillet. Two hundred grams of extra-firm tofu plus 80 grams of oats is 30.5 grams and $1.69 ([FDC 174290](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174290/nutrients)). Fine if tofu is already how you eat. A bad use of $1.69 if you bought it to feel efficient.

The milk bowl is the cheap one, and it looks like cereal because it's cereal. A 244 gram cup of whole milk, 60 grams of oats, and 64 grams of peanut butter come to 29.8 grams for $0.72 ([FDC 171265](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171265/nutrients)). Sixty grams of oats is the breakfast scoop on the [7-day grocery list](/high-protein-grocery-list-50-grams/). The peanut butter is two of the 32 gram scoops. You'll taste it. That's the point.

## Which plate actually wins on price?

$0.72, $0.88, $1.27, $1.69. Same job, four receipts. The milk bowl wins this set. The tofu plate loses it. Almonds, at $2.03 for 30 grams, didn't even get a combo, because about 142 grams of almonds is a snack bowl with a budget problem, not a breakfast plan ([FDC 170567](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170567/nutrients)).

Eggs aren't in the chart on purpose. Five large eggs are a different page: 250 grams, 31.4 grams of protein, and $0.87 for an exact 30 grams at 34.4 grams per dollar. That's [the egg receipt against yogurt, chicken, and lentils](/eggs-vs-everything-protein-value/). If the carton is full, use it. If it isn't, the $0.72 bowl is already priced.

The full ranking those rates come from is the [protein per dollar study](/protein-per-dollar-adjusted-for-quality/). This page is only the breakfast slice that doesn't need an egg.

## What this chart won't buy

Salt, fruit, the banana you were going to slice on the oats, and whatever oil hits the tofu. The costs above are the protein-carrying foods, the same rule as the day studies. Thirty grams here is a breakfast-sized slice of the day, not a prescription with your name on it. If 30 grams isn't even your number, [how much protein you need per day](/how-much-protein-do-you-need-per-day/) is the page that sorts the daily total.

That's the morning. No carton required, four plates between $0.72 and $1.69, and the peanut butter jar doing more work than the egg marketing ever did.
