Popcorn kernels deliver 57.7 grams of fiber per dollar. Almonds deliver 8.8. That's about 6.6 times more fiber for the same money, using USDA figures and July 2026 prices, which is one of the widest snack-aisle gaps we found. The honest caveat: almonds go in a bag and travel, and popcorn needs a pot and four minutes of your attention.

| | Popcorn kernels | Almonds |
|---|---|---|
| **Fiber per $1** | **57.7 g** | **8.8 g** |
| Fiber per 100 g | 14.5 g | 12.5 g |
| What you buy | 2 lb bag | 16 oz bag |
| Price | $2.28 | $6.47 |
| Cost per 100 g | $0.25 | $1.43 |

*Source: USDA FoodData Central + single-store US prices, July 2026. Full methodology at [/methodology/](/methodology/).*

## How can popcorn be 6.6 times cheaper for fiber?

Look at the fiber-per-100-grams row again. Popcorn 14.5, almonds 12.5. That's a rounding error, not a blowout. The entire gap comes from price.

Almonds cost $1.43 per 100 grams. Popcorn kernels cost 25 cents. You're paying almost six times more per gram of food for something with roughly the same fiber density, and the ranking just reports the consequence.

Run it in bag terms. That $2.28 two pound bag of kernels holds about 132 grams of fiber, which is around 4.7 times the FDA Daily Value of 28 grams. The $6.47 bag of almonds holds about 57 grams. So one bag of popcorn kernels carries more fiber than two bags of almonds, at roughly a sixth of the cost. Nothing about that is a trick of the math.

Popcorn also sits fifth overall in our [fiber per dollar study](/fiber-per-dollar-cheapest-high-fiber-foods/), keeping company with dry beans and whole wheat flour. It's the only thing on that shortlist that you eat out of a bowl in front of a movie.

## When are almonds still the right buy?

When the snack has to survive a glovebox.

This is the part rankings are bad at. Almonds require zero equipment, zero cleanup, and zero planning. They sit in a jacket pocket for a week and come out fine. They bring fat and protein along with the fiber, so a small handful may keep you satisfied longer than the same volume of air-popped corn, which is mostly air by design. If your 3 PM problem is that you're actually hungry rather than bored, almonds solve it better.

They're also just better in things. Chopped almonds on a [no-bake oat and flax energy ball](/no-bake-high-fiber-energy-balls-recipe/) do something popcorn can't. Same for the crunch on top of a bowl of yogurt.

What almonds are not is the cheapest way to buy fiber. Once you know that, you can buy them for the reasons they're actually good at and stop pretending it's a budget move.

## What's the cheapest nut or seed for fiber?

If you want the almond-shaped hole in your pantry filled for less, here's the nuts and seeds bracket:

| Chia seeds | 32 oz bag, $9.44 | 33.1 g |
| Whole flaxseed | 22 oz bag, $5.98 | 28.5 g |
| Dry roasted peanuts | 16 oz jar, $2.78 | 13.7 g |
| Sunflower seed kernels | 16 oz bag, $2.98 | 13.1 g |
| Peanut butter | 40 oz jar, $4.97 | 11.4 g |
| Almonds | 16 oz bag, $6.47 | 8.8 g |

Chia leads at 33.1 grams per dollar, but nobody snacks on chia by the handful. It earns its place stirred into things, like a [chia raspberry jam](/high-fiber-raspberry-jam-recipe-chia/) that turns toast into a fiber delivery system.

For actual snacking, dry roasted peanuts at 13.7 grams per dollar are the quiet answer. Same jar-of-nuts experience, about 1.6 times the fiber per dollar of almonds, and the jar costs $2.78 instead of $6.47. Sunflower seeds at 13.1 are right there too.

## What should the snack shelf actually look like?

Kernels for volume, peanuts for portability, almonds when you specifically want almonds. That's the whole system.
