## Why can't we verify the old 6.6x claim?

The two popcorn inputs describe different forms. USDA's 14.5-gram value is for air-popped popcorn, whose weight reflects moisture loss and expansion during cooking. The store price is for unpopped kernels. A valid cost comparison needs a nutrition record for kernels as sold or a documented popped yield that connects the two.

Almonds cost $1.43 per 100 grams in this sample, while kernels cost 25 cents. Those prices are usable, but applying the air-popped fiber density directly to the kernel bag is not. Price alone cannot resolve the mismatch.

The same limitation blocks the bag-total claim. Multiplying the air-popped value by the unpopped bag weight produces the recorded 132 grams, but it does not verify how much fiber is in that kernel bag. We have removed that result from the answer.

The parent [fiber per dollar study](/fiber-per-dollar-cheapest-high-fiber-foods/) preserves popcorn's published fifth-place observation for transparency and marks it unresolved. It should not be treated as a current verified rank.

## When are almonds still the right buy?

When the snack has to survive a glovebox.

This is the part rankings are bad at. Almonds require zero equipment, zero cleanup, and zero planning. They sit in a jacket pocket for a week and come out fine. They bring fat and protein along with the fiber, so a small handful may keep you satisfied longer than the same volume of air-popped corn, which is mostly air by design. If your 3 PM problem is that you're actually hungry rather than bored, almonds solve it better.

They're also just better in things. Chopped almonds on a [no-bake oat and flax energy ball](/no-bake-high-fiber-energy-balls-recipe/) do something popcorn can't. Same for the crunch on top of a bowl of yogurt.

What almonds are not is the cheapest way to buy fiber. Once you know that, you can buy them for the reasons they're actually good at and stop pretending it's a budget move.

Chia leads at 33.1 grams per dollar, but nobody snacks on chia by the handful. It earns its place stirred into things, like a [chia raspberry jam](/high-fiber-raspberry-jam-recipe-chia/) that turns toast into a fiber delivery system.

For actual snacking, dry roasted peanuts at 13.7 grams per dollar are the quiet answer. Same jar-of-nuts experience, about 1.6 times the fiber per dollar of almonds, and the jar costs $2.78 instead of $6.47. Sunflower seeds at 13.1 are right there too.

Kernels for volume, peanuts for portability, almonds when you specifically want almonds. That's the whole system.
