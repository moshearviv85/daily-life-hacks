I used to treat the BLS average like a receipt. It isn't. It's the U.S. city average for a named commodity, and the Great Value bag in the cart is a different package. Sometimes a different food entirely.

The protein rates are protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. The fiber rates are fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. The later BLS print is bls-nutrition-per-dollar-latest.csv. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the files. What one dollar buys, without splitting the receipt by who priced it, is [what $1 buys in protein versus fiber](/one-dollar-of-protein-vs-one-dollar-of-fiber/).

## When does a Walmart bulk bag beat the BLS average?

Whole wheat flour returns 96.0 grams of protein and 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar ([FDC 168893](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168893/nutrients)). Brown lentils return 77.7 and 33.7 ([FDC 172420](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172420/nutrients)). Green split peas return 73.9 and 71.0 ([FDC 172428](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172428/nutrients)). BLS dry beans return 57.6 to 59.9 grams of protein. BLS whole wheat bread returns 20.1 grams of protein and 9.7 grams of fiber. Walmart Greek yogurt, at 27.5, does not beat BLS whole milk at 28.5.

That's the honest version of "beats." The bulk bag wins when it isn't the same food as the BLS row. It doesn't win just because the label says Great Value.

## These rows are not one shopping trip

Orange is protein. Slate is fiber. The tick says Walmart or BLS because that's the price basis in the file, not because I bought both on the same Tuesday.

## Dry bags versus the national bean price

Lentils and split peas clear the bean range. Chickpeas don't.

BLS navy beans are 59.9 grams of protein and 41.1 grams of fiber per dollar. Black beans are 58.0 and 41.6. Pintos are 57.6 and 41.7. Same July series, APU0000714233, three different USDA records. Walmart brown lentils are 77.7 and 33.7. Split peas are 73.9 and 71.0. Those aren't the same legume as a pinto, and the protein gap is still the gap in the file.

Chickpeas are the Walmart dry bag that doesn't get to brag. 56.7 grams of protein and 33.8 grams of fiber, [FDC 173756](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173756/nutrients). That's under navy beans on protein and under all three bean rows on fiber. A bulk bag isn't a prize. It's a row.

Drumsticks are the meat version of the same warning. 50.3 grams of protein per dollar, July 2026, after the file takes 33 percent off for bone and cartilage. [FDC 172373](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172373/nutrients) lists 0.0 grams of fiber per 100 grams, so the slate bar is a zero. BLS boneless breast is 24.6 grams of protein in this file. That's a cheaper cut versus a boneless cut, not Walmart breast versus the national breast price. The fiber file doesn't price the drumstick. The zero is the USDA record.

## Flour beats bread. Yogurt doesn't beat milk.

The loaf is where the flour number goes to die. Whole wheat flour is 96.0 grams of protein and 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar. The May 2026 BLS whole wheat loaf is 20.1 and 9.7. Same kind of wheat, after somebody else baked it and the average shopper paid $3.52 for 20 ounces. You want the flour number, you turn the oven on.

Yogurt is the row that loses. Plain nonfat Greek yogurt is 27.5 grams of protein per dollar, a 32 ounce tub at $3.36 in July 2026. [FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients) lists 0 grams of fiber per 100 grams. BLS whole milk is 28.5 grams of protein per dollar at $4.31 a gallon, [FDC 171265](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171265/nutrients). The tub doesn't beat the jug. Not on this file.

Eggs are 34.4 grams, a dozen at $2.19. Ground beef is 11.3 grams at $6.89 a pound. Every Walmart dry bag on the chart clears both. That still isn't a Walmart egg price or a Walmart beef price. The file priced those two on the BLS average.

A digestibility score would reshuffle the top. This page doesn't apply one. The scored version is [plant versus animal protein per dollar](/plant-vs-animal-protein-cost-per-dollar/).

## October moved four BLS series. It didn't move the answer.

The later file is October 2026 for the U.S. city average. It isn't a second copy of the July table.

| Ground beef, 80/20 | $6.755 per lb | 11.53 g | 0 g | [FDC 174036](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174036/nutrients) |

White flour isn't whole wheat flour. 87.17 grams of protein is close to the Walmart whole wheat bag's 96.0. 22.85 grams of fiber isn't close to 77.8. I won't call that a same-bag win.

Eggs slipped from 34.4 to 32.48. Milk rose from 28.5 to 29.99, so October milk beats the July yogurt row by a little more. Breast ticked up from 24.6 to 24.90. Beef ticked up from 11.3 to 11.53. The dry bags still sit on top. Regions don't share one October price, which is the point of [the October regional protein prices](/us-regional-grocery-protein-prices-october-2026/).

The latest file has no October U.S. city row for dried beans or whole wheat bread. I didn't borrow May or July and rename it.

If the cart is a batch of chicken instead of a flour bag, the dollar isn't the only clock. Cooked meat and poultry are 3 to 4 fridge days, priced on [how long cooked meat and poultry last](/how-long-do-cooked-meat-and-poultry-last-in-the-fridge/).

Buy the bulk bag when the row is a dry food you'll actually cook. Don't buy it because a national average for a different food made the bag look like a hero.
