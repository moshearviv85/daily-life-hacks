I had the tub, the other tub, and a dozen eggs, and I still tried to pick a winner by staring. The stickers don't speak protein. The files do.

Thirty grams of protein is $0.87 in eggs, $1.09 in plain nonfat Greek yogurt, and $1.14 in 4% cottage cheese. That's 30 divided by the grams-per-dollar figure in dairy-protein-per-dollar-ranked-2026.csv, rounded half up to the cent. The equal-weight spoon is a different page. This one is the receipt, plus what happens when you actually heat the stuff.

## What does 30 grams of protein cost in eggs, Greek yogurt, and cottage cheese?

Eggs win the three-way. They're 34.4 g of protein per dollar, $0.029 per gram. Yogurt is 27.5 g per dollar and $0.036 per gram. Cottage cheese is 26.3 g per dollar and $0.038 per gram. Five cents separates the two tubs at 30 g. Twenty-seven cents separates the eggs from the cottage cheese. That's the whole argument at the register.

A dozen is 600 g edible at $2.19, July 2026 BLS. The yogurt tub is 32 oz at $3.36. Cottage cheese is 24 oz at $2.87. Both tubs are Walmart Great Value. The smaller sticker isn't a cheaper protein.

## Sodium shows up for four of the six, and the cap stays at 1.00

Greek yogurt is the quiet row: 36 mg of sodium per 100 g. Eggs are 142 mg. Cheddar is 653 mg. Part-skim mozzarella is 666 mg. Whole milk and cottage cheese aren't on the sodium sheet. The slate bars are that absence, not a claim that the food is saltless. I won't paste a milligram onto FDC 172179 from memory.

The color line is 140 mg per 100 g, the same ruler as the [dairy and soy protein chart](/cheapest-dairy-and-soy-protein-per-dollar/). Yogurt clears it. Eggs miss it by 2 mg. The two cheeses miss it by a country mile. Mozzarella still buys 30.1 g of protein per dollar, which is $1.00 for 30 g, and then you eat 666 mg of sodium in every 100 g. That's a seasoning you paid for.

DIAAS on the quality sheet is 1.13 for eggs and 1.14 for mozzarella, milk, yogurt, and cottage cheese. The sheet caps the multiplier at 1.00, so the adjusted grams per dollar stay 34.4, 30.1, 28.5, 27.5, and 26.3. The score doesn't buy you extra protein. Cheddar has no DIAAS row. I'm not copying 1.14 onto it because both foods started as milk.

A pound of protein, counted as 454 g because that's the weight on the mozzarella and cheddar package rows, is $13.20 in eggs, $15.08 in mozzarella, $15.93 in whole milk, $16.51 in Greek yogurt, $17.26 in cottage cheese, and $25.08 in cheddar. Milk looks cheap by the gallon until you remember it's 3.15 g of protein per 100 g. You drink your way to that pound. The wider list, past these six, is the [protein per dollar ranking](/protein-per-dollar-adjusted-for-quality/).

## Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and eggs don't take the same heat

Yogurt is the cold sauce and the dollop you stir in after the pan is off the heat. Let it boil and it splits. Grainy curds, watery puddle, and you standing there like the tub betrayed you. It didn't. You boiled it.

Cottage cheese is the thicker savory base, and only after it sees a blender. Dump the curds into a screaming skillet and they weep. Blend them and you've got something you can loosen with a spoon of the pasta water. It's still not yogurt. It's saltier in the mouth even though this page refuses to invent the milligrams.

Eggs are the one that can set. A sauce that needs to thicken from the heat, a scramble, a thin custard poured over toast. They're also the cheap protein of the three, at $0.87 for 30 g. If the recipe's expensive line is the cheese instead, price the batch in the [recipe cost calculator](/tools/recipe-cost-calculator/) before the block is already in the bowl. Mozzarella at $1.00 for 30 g of protein is a bargain that arrives with 666 mg of sodium per 100 g. Cheddar at $1.66 is the expensive salt.

I keep yogurt for the bowl that stays cold, cottage cheese for the thing I blended on purpose, and eggs when I want the protein and the set. The tubs can share a fridge. They don't share a job.

## Where these numbers come from

Grams per dollar come from the ranked dairy file, checked against the protein file on the same USDA record. Thirty grams divided by that figure, half up to the cent. One divided by it, half up to the thousandth, is the per-gram price. The 454 g pound is the weight already on the cheese rows.

Sodium only where the sodium file shares the record. Egg and cheddar sodium prices in that file are the May 2026 BLS series. The protein figures above are July. I didn't mix May's cheddar price, $5.69 a pound, into July's $5.75 and 18.1 g per dollar.

Eggs for the dollar. Yogurt when you want the 36 mg the sheet actually publishes. Cottage cheese when the recipe wants the curd, at $1.14 for the same 30 g, with the sodium cell blank on purpose.
