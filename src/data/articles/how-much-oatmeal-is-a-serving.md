A serving of dry old-fashioned rolled oats is 60 grams, about 2.1 ounces. That scoop carries 7.9 grams of protein, 6.1 grams of fiber, and costs roughly 17 cents. A 42 ounce canister holds 1191 grams, which is close to 20 of those servings for $3.36.

Everything else on this page is what happens when you go bigger, go smaller, or start reading the box.

## Why 60 grams and not whatever the box says?

Because box servings are set by the manufacturer and they don't agree with each other. 60 grams dry is the portion our cost studies use, it produces a normal-looking bowl, and it makes every other number on this page comparable to the rest of the site.

Weigh it once. Genuinely, one time, on whatever kitchen scale you own, and then look at what 60 grams looks like in your usual bowl or your usual scoop. After that you'll never need the scale again, and you'll stop being surprised when a "serving" on a package turns out to be a third of what you actually pour.

The other reason to know the weight is that dry oats and cooked oats look nothing alike. Oats roughly triple in the pot. A 60 gram scoop looks like a snack and finishes like a meal.

## How many servings are in a canister?

About 19.9. A 42 ounce canister is 1191 grams at $3.36, so 60 gram servings work out to just under 17 cents each.

That whole canister is 156.6 grams of protein and 120.3 grams of fiber. Put differently, one $3.36 purchase covers more than three days of the FDA's 50 gram protein Daily Value and more than four days of its 28 gram fiber Daily Value, if you were eating nothing but oats, which would be a bleak experiment.

The per-dollar version: 46.6 grams of protein per dollar and 35.8 grams of fiber per dollar. That ranks oats second among breakfast staples in [the cheapest high-protein breakfast foods](/breakfast-staples-per-dollar/), behind only whole wheat flour.

## How much oatmeal should you eat for breakfast?

Depends on what else is on the table, and this is where most oatmeal advice goes vague, so here's the arithmetic instead.

One 60 gram bowl gives you about 16 percent of the FDA's 50 gram protein [Daily Value](/fiber-protein-daily-values-explained/) and about 22 percent of the 28 gram fiber Daily Value. If your morning ends with you rummaging in a drawer at ten, the fix usually isn't more oats. Doubling to 120 grams only takes protein to 15.8 grams, and you'd be eating a very large amount of porridge to get there.

Adding something denser works better. Two tablespoons of peanut butter add 7.1 grams of protein for 14 cents, and swapping water for a cup of whole milk adds 7.7 grams for 26 cents. That takes a standard bowl to about 22.7 grams for roughly 57 cents. The full menu of add-ons and what each one costs is in [how to get more protein at breakfast](/how-to-get-more-protein-at-breakfast/).

## What does a bowl of oatmeal actually cost?

Seventeen cents for the oats. Thirty-one cents with peanut butter. Forty-three cents if you make it with milk instead of water. Under sixty cents for the loaded version with both.

Compare that to the same bowl bought as a flavoured instant packet or across a counter, and the canister starts to look like one of the better arguments in the grocery store. Our dataset doesn't price instant packets, so I'm not going to put a number on the markup, but you can read the shelf tags yourself and the direction is not subtle.

The rest of the pantry ranked the same way, from oats down to almonds, is in [the cheapest shelf-stable protein](/shelf-stable-pantry-per-dollar/).

## Is a daily bowl of oats a reasonable habit?

I've eaten one most mornings for years, so I'm not neutral. What I can say from the numbers is that a single 60 gram serving covers about a fifth of the FDA fiber Daily Value from one cheap food, which could make hitting the rest of the day's fiber a lot less fiddly.

The thing that kills the habit isn't nutrition, it's monotony. Rotate what goes on top: peanut butter one day, a chopped banana another, a fried egg and hot sauce when you want [something savory](/savory-oatmeal-bowls-with-eggs-and-avocado/). If oats aren't sticking at all, [chia pudding and the other make-ahead options](/make-ahead-breakfast-ideas-without-eggs/) cover similar ground.
