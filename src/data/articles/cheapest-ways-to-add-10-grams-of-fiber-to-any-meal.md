You already cooked dinner. You want another 10 grams of fiber on it, and you don't want that spoonful to cost more than the bowl. I used to grab the berry box for this job. The berry box is a terrible employee. It shows up late, it costs $2 to $4, and it still might not cover the 10 grams unless you eat most of the package.

Cost here is 10 divided by the fiber grams per dollar, rounded half up to the cent. The rates are fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. Pearled barley and rolled oats are the same rates in grains-fiber-per-dollar-ranked-2026.csv, and the script checks that they match before it draws the bars. Grams are 10 grams of fiber divided by the fiber per 100 g, times 100. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the files.

## What does 10 grams of fiber cost from the pantry?

Dry green split peas are $0.14. Pearled barley is $0.18. Dry pinto, black, and navy beans are $0.24. Rolled oats are $0.28. Chia seeds are $0.30. Whole flaxseed is $0.35. Then the price leaves the pantry and the bars get embarrassed.

## A cheap gram isn't a sprinkle

Forty-five grams of dry split peas sounds cute until you remember they're raw. Same for 64.5 g of dry black beans and 64.1 g of pearled barley. Those weights are the priced form, the way the bag is sold. A raw bean on last night's soup is how you chip a tooth and call it meal prep. [Cook the dried beans](/how-to-cook-dried-beans-from-scratch/) first, then the $0.14 and the $0.24 mean something on a plate.

Frozen green peas are already the freezer version of this idea, and 10 grams of fiber wants 222.2 g of them. That's a side dish, $0.57, not a garnish you hide under the rice. The protein and sodium that come with that bag, and with the cans, are on the [canned, frozen, and dry staples chart](/how-much-protein-and-fiber-in-canned-vs-frozen-vs-dry-staples/). This page only prices the fiber.

Avocado looks like a sprinkle and isn't. You need 149.3 g of edible fruit ([FDC 171705](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171705/nutrients)). The row prices one fruit at about 7 oz and $0.95, edible fraction 0.74, so the flesh is 148 g. Ten grams of fiber is that avocado. One fruit. $0.96. Fine if you wanted the avocado. A strange way to buy fiber if you didn't.

## Seeds, oats, and the bowl you were already eating

Chia at 29.1 g and whole flaxseed at 36.6 g are the actual spoons. Chia is $0.30 for the 10 grams. Flax is $0.35. Both are the seed, not a powder in a capsule, and both still count as food you eat. Rolled oats at 99.0 g aren't a spoon on someone else's breakfast. They're the breakfast. $0.28 is a bargain only if you were going to eat the bowl anyway.

Peanut butter at $0.88 looks like it belongs with the seeds. It doesn't. Two hundred grams of peanut butter for 10 grams of fiber is most of a meal of peanut butter. Dry roasted peanuts need 119.0 g, $0.73. Sunflower kernels need 116.3 g, $0.76. Cheap fiber, heavy hand. The nut-and-seed dollar ranking, protein included, is [nuts and seeds per dollar](/cheapest-nuts-and-seeds-for-protein-and-fiber/).

## Berries, priced as fiber

Raspberries need 153.8 g, most of the 6 oz box, and they cost $2.22. Blueberries need 416.7 g. The priced pint is 11 oz, 312 g, so 10 grams of fiber is more than that pint, at $4.00. I still buy berries when I want berries. I don't buy them because the bowl was short 10 grams of fiber. That's the produce aisle doing a job the dry-goods aisle already finished for 14 cents.

The wider fruit and vegetable ranking, peas and avocado included, is [high-fiber fruits and vegetables per dollar](/cheapest-high-fiber-fruits-and-vegetables/). If the 10 grams have to land on tonight's plate and the pot is cold, use the chia or the flax. If you can cook, the split peas are the whole pitch: $0.14, 45 grams dry, and a real side instead of a tax on dessert.
