I used to treat 50 grams of protein like a personality. Then I priced the week. Two rock-bottom days, three mixed days, and two no-cook days, from rows we already published, come to $10.27.

That's 367.5 grams across seven days, about 52.5 grams a day, for one person. The cart is the dry-goods day, the realistic mixed day, and the no-cook day from the [50-gram cost study](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/), bought once instead of admired as three separate menus. It isn't the $19.46 meat-cook week, and it isn't the $92.61 restaurant week. Scale the same days for a bigger household in the [weekly food-cost planner](/tools/grocery-budget-calculator/), or read how those grams hold up once protein quality counts on the [quality-adjusted ranking](/protein-per-dollar-adjusted-for-quality/).

## What goes on a 7-day high-protein grocery list?

Buy this once. Cook the dry goods on two days, eat the mixed plate three times, and keep the no-cook day for the nights the pot stays in the cabinet. Every protein gram and every cent below is a published row in protein-day-cost-2026.csv, which points at protein-per-dollar-2026.csv and a USDA FoodData Central record. Both files are on the [50-gram cost study](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/). Cup language is the day menu's own label, multiplied. I didn't invent a new scoop.

Split peas and pinto beans are doing quiet work. $0.78 for 66.4 grams of protein between them, which is more than the two tuna cans and a lot less money. The tuna still costs $1.96. Buy it for the nights you won't cook. Don't ask it to be the cheap line.

Two rows say proxy because the parent file says so. Peanut butter and canned black beans have a FoodData Central record, and the priced jar or can isn't an exact form match. The note is in protein-per-dollar-2026.csv, the parent file behind the [50-gram cost study](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/). On both of those foods the parent note says the salt or sodium variant shares the same protein grams, so the ambiguity doesn't move this cart.

Pinto beans, eggs, and milk use the July 2026 BLS US average already written on the day file. Whole wheat bread uses the May 2026 BLS average on that same file. I didn't relabel it as July. Greek yogurt and tuna are Walmart listings dated July 2026. Oats, split peas, brown rice, peanut butter, and canned black beans keep a blank month cell and the audit note "fiber study, audited." Add the visible cents and you get $10.27. The [methodology page](/methodology/) is where the price basis and the edible fraction are spelled out.

## What does each day of the list actually eat?

Two copies of the rock-bottom day, three copies of the mixed day, two copies of the no-cook day. I'm not promising you'll crave split peas on Friday if you had them on Monday. I'm saying the math still clears 50 grams when you repeat the cheap plates from the [50-gram cost study](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/).

The day names are mine. The grams and the cents are the [published menus](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/), repeated. Every one of those days is between 51.7 and 53.5 grams. Divide 367.5 by seven and you get 52.5.

The [FDA's Daily Value for protein is 50 grams](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/daily-value-nutrition-and-supplement-facts-labels), on a 2,000-calorie label. It's a labeling reference, not a personal prescription. These portions are the protein-carrying foods sized to land near that line. Real plates are bigger. Salt, oil, and the vegetables next to the beans aren't in the $10.27.

## What do the meat week and the restaurant week cost?

Same file, different weeks. I left both off the shopping list on purpose.

Seven meat-cook days are 7 times the $2.78 day. Each of those days is 2 large eggs (100 g, 12.6 g protein, $0.37, [FDC 171287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients)), 100 g raw chicken breast (22.5 g, $0.92, [FDC 171077](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171077/nutrients)), and 100 g of 80/20 ground beef (17.2 g, $1.49, [FDC 174036](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174036/nutrients)). That's 52.3 grams and $2.78, all BLS US average, July 2026. The week is 366.1 grams and $19.46. Home-cooked meat isn't the expensive version of this problem. How eggs, chicken breast, and ground beef moved in grams per dollar from 2017 through October 2026 is the [protein per dollar trend](/protein-per-dollar-2017-to-2026-bls-trends/).

Seven restaurant days are 7 times the $13.23 day. [Egg McMuffin](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/product/egg-mcmuffin.html) is 17.0 grams at $5.35, [McDouble](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/product/mcdouble.html) is 22.0 grams at $3.99, and [McChicken](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/product/mcchicken.html) is 14.0 grams at $3.89. McDonald's published the grams. The prices are the PriceListo national average from the July 6, 2026 snapshot recorded in protein-day-cost-2026.csv on the [50-gram cost study](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/), a la carte, not a grocery price and not a promise about your local app. The week is 371.0 grams and $92.61.

Same job, different receipt. $10.27, $19.46, or $92.61. The drive-thru can be a night you chose. It doesn't have to be the plan.

## How do you cook the dry half without making it a project?

The split peas want a pot. So do the pintos. If the bag is still in the pantry at 5 p.m., the clock for that simmer is the [dried bean cook-time chart](/how-to-cook-dried-beans-from-scratch/). Oats don't need a speech. Sixty grams, water, breakfast. The brown rice can share the dinner plate with the beans. Eaten the same day, that pair is the reason [beans and rice count as a complete protein](/beans-and-rice-complete-protein-meal/).

The no-cook day is a can opener and a glass. Yogurt, tuna, two slices of bread, milk. That's the $2.05 back pocket, not a personality. The mixed day is eggs in the morning and a peanut-butter sandwich you already know how to make.

## What this list won't buy

Salt, oil, and whatever you want next to the beans. The $10.27 is the protein-carrying foods, the same rule as the day study. It isn't a complete diet, and it isn't a personalized plan. If 50 grams isn't even your number, [how much protein you actually need](/how-much-protein-do-you-need-per-day/) is the page that sorts that out. This one is the cart.

That's the list. One person, seven days, $10.27, and the cheeseburger can stay a choice instead of a strategy.
