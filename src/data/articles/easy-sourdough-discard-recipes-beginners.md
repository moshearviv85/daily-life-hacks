A jar of discard should come with dinner options, not another feeding schedule. Start with pancakes if you want something in 20 minutes, skillet flatbread if you have half a cup, or crackers if you can leave the oven running for about an hour.

These easy sourdough discard recipes for beginners use **100% hydration discard from an established starter**, meaning equal weights of flour and water. Cold discard is fine. It doesn't need to double before you cook.

## Getting the pancakes right

The recipe card makes roughly six small pancakes, depending on how generously you pour. That's breakfast for two, not a towering stack for four. The calorie estimate is about 370 per serving before syrup, toppings, or extra butter for the pan.

Heat the skillet before adding baking soda to the batter. The reaction starts as soon as the soda meets the acidic starter, so this isn't batter to leave on the counter while you hunt for a spatula.

Keep the heat at medium-low and make one small pancake first. If it browns before the middle sets, turn the heat down. A dark crust with a wet center isn't finished just because it looks good from across the kitchen.

For the original two-serving batch, one cup of discard weighs about 227 grams. Butter contributes roughly 200 calories to the whole batch; replacing it with an unmeasured splash of oil changes the estimate. For a different make-ahead breakfast, the [bran muffin recipe](/high-fiber-bran-muffins-that-taste-good/) spells out its batch yield and flour options.

## Skillet flatbread with half a cup

This is a thin, chewy pan bread, closer to a savory pancake than a fluffy pita. It won't form a pocket.

For **one small flatbread**, stir together 1/2 cup (113 g) discard, a pinch of salt, 1 tablespoon finely sliced scallions, and 1 tablespoon all-purpose flour. Let it stand while you heat 1 teaspoon oil in an 8-inch nonstick skillet over medium-low heat.

Spread the batter into a thin circle about 6 inches across. Cook for 4 to 5 minutes, until the top loses its wet shine and the underside is golden. Flip carefully and cook the other side for 3 to 4 minutes. Tear the center to check that no wet batter remains; give it longer over lower heat if needed.

Eat it warm with eggs, hummus, or leftover vegetables. Keep it thin. A thick puddle of starter takes longer to cook through than its browned outside suggests.

## No-roll crackers for a full cup

Allow about **65 minutes plus cooling**, not 20. The brief mixing time and the baking time are different things.

This version follows [King Arthur Baking's no-roll cracker method](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/easiest-sourdough-discard-crackers-recipe). Mix 1 cup (227 g) discard, 4 tablespoons (57 g) melted unsalted butter, 1/2 teaspoon table salt, and 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika.

Spread onto parchment in an even rectangle about 11 by 15 inches on a rimmed baking sheet. Bake at 325 degrees F for 15 minutes. Score into small squares, then continue baking for 35 to 45 minutes, rotating the sheet halfway through. The center needs to be golden and firm. Let the sheet cool completely before breaking it apart.

The middle tells you whether they're done. Brown edges around a soft center mean the layer was uneven or needs longer. Move finished edge pieces off the sheet if necessary while the center crisps.

## Fix the problem before adding more ingredients

| What happened | What to change |
|---|---|
| Pancakes spread too thin | For the original batch, stir in 1 tablespoon flour and cook another small test pancake |
| Pancakes brown while the middle stays wet | Lower the heat and make smaller pancakes |
| Pancakes taste strongly of baking soda | Check the measured quantity; don't add extra soda to get more height |
| Flatbread sticks or tears | Use a nonstick skillet, oil it lightly, and wait until the first side is set before turning |
| Crackers bend after cooling | Bake them longer; next time spread the batter more evenly |
| Everything tastes sharply sour | Start with more recently collected discard from a maintained starter |

A starter with a different flour-to-water ratio changes the batter. Don't keep adding flour because a recipe on another website assumes a thicker jar.

## Keep the discard jar usable

Label the jar with the oldest collection date and refrigerate it covered. Plan to use this batch within a week; adding fresh discard doesn't reset the age of what's already there. Stir before measuring by volume.

Use discard from an established starter, not the mixture from the first days of building a culture. [King Arthur's starter guide](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/sourdough/create) says to throw out starter with mold, pink or orange discoloration, or a putrid smell. Don't try to bake those problems away.

Discard still contains raw flour. [The FDA advises against tasting raw flour, dough, or batter](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/raw-doughs-raw-deal-and-could-make-you-sick). Cook these recipes through, then judge the flavor. The jar doesn't need to be emptied in one heroic baking session.
