I kept buying the berry box and calling it a fiber plan. Then I added the receipt. A week that clears about 30 grams a day, from foods we already priced in the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/), comes to $9.32.

That's 227.3 grams across seven days, about 32.5 grams a day, for one person. The cart is the dry-goods day, the mixed day, and the no-cook day from the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/), bought once instead of admired as three separate menus. This isn't a produce-only basket, and it isn't the $14.42 restaurant day. Poke the same prices in the [fiber per dollar calculator](/tools/fiber-per-dollar-calculator/), or look up one food in the [food value database](/food-value-database/).

## What goes on a 7-day high-fiber grocery list?

Buy this once. Cook the dry goods on three days, eat the mixed plate twice, and keep the no-cook day for the nights the pot stays in the cabinet. Every fiber gram and every cent below is a published row in fiber-day-cost-2026.csv, which points at fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv and a USDA FoodData Central record. Both files are on the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/). Cup language is the day menu's own label, multiplied. I didn't invent a new scoop.

Split peas are the quiet star. $0.48 and 33.3 grams, which is more fiber than the two apples and both bananas combined, and the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/) is where those line items live. The apples still cost $1.02. Buy them if you want the crunch. Don't ask them to carry the week.

Four rows say proxy because the parent audit says so. Peanut butter, canned black beans, frozen broccoli, and bran flakes have a FoodData Central record, and the priced package isn't an exact form match. The note is in fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv, on the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/). Popcorn stayed off the list. That row is still unresolved, and a grocery list shouldn't pretend otherwise.

Pinto beans and bananas use the July 2026 BLS US average already written on the day file. Whole wheat bread uses the May 2026 BLS average on that same file. I didn't relabel it as July. The other line costs are the Walmart prices those day menus already applied. Add the visible cents and you get $9.32. A price-per-100-gram multiplication can disagree by a penny, because the day rows were rounded first. ## What does each day of the list actually eat?

Three copies of the rock-bottom day, two copies of the mixed day, two copies of the no-cook day. I'm not promising you'll crave split peas on Sunday if you had them on Monday. I'm saying the math still clears 30 grams when you repeat the cheap plates from the [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/).

Every one of those days is between 30.8 and 35.0 grams. The [30-gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/) is the source of the fiber column and the running cost.

Those two days stayed off this list on purpose. Berries can be a weekend thing. They don't have to be the plan.

## How do you cook the dry half without making it a project?

The split peas want a pot. That's [split pea soup](/split-pea-soup-recipe-high-fiber/), and it's the highest-fiber line on the receipt for under fifty cents. The pintos are the other pot. If you forgot to soak them this morning, the [same-day quick soak](/how-to-quick-soak-dried-beans-same-day/) is the version that still makes dinner. The longer clock, if you have it, is the [dried bean cook-time chart](/how-to-cook-dried-beans-from-scratch/).

Oats don't need a speech. Half a cup or two thirds, water, breakfast. Barley can share the pot with the rice if a third grain feels like homework. The no-cook day is a can opener, a microwave, and a sandwich. That's the $1.74 back pocket, not a personality.

Those oats and beans also carry protein. Pricing both on one receipt, instead of adding this fiber cart to a separate protein cart, is the [cost of 50 g of protein and 30 g of fiber a day](/cost-of-50g-protein-and-30g-fiber-per-day/).

## What this list will not buy

Salt, oil, and whatever you want next to the beans. The $9.32 is the fiber-carrying foods, the same rule as the day study. It isn't a complete diet, and it isn't a personalized plan. If this week is a jump from a white-bread routine, don't hit 32 grams tomorrow and then blame the beans. Add fiber gradually, and drink water with it. We already wrote that down in [how to increase fiber without the gas](/how-to-increase-fiber-intake-without-gas/) and [the water and fiber rule](/water-and-fiber-the-golden-rule/).

That's the cart. One person, seven days, $9.32, and the raspberries can stay a treat instead of a strategy.
