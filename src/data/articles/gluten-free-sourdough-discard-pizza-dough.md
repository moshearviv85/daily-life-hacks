I used to feel a strange, low-grade guilt every time I poured half my gluten free starter down the drain. It felt like throwing away a science project I had actually managed to keep alive. If you're like me, you started a GF sourdough starter because you wanted better bread, but now you just have a jar in the fridge that demands to be fed like a very needy, very beige pet.

This gluten free sourdough discard pizza dough is the best way to stop the waste. It doesn't require a 12-hour ferment or a degree in chemistry. You get that distinct sourdough tang and a crust that actually bends without snapping in half. Measure carefully, though. The [sourdough discard grams conversion](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/) keeps an extra splash of starter from turning the crust into paste. And if gluten isn't your problem, the regular [no-yeast sourdough discard pizza dough](/easy-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough-no-yeast/) works the same magic.

## How do you make gluten-free sourdough discard pizza dough without a long ferment?

Whisk one teaspoon of sugar into 118.5 grams of warm water with 8 grams of instant yeast. Stir in 200 grams of gluten-free discard and 27 grams of olive oil, then 180 to 204 grams of xanthan-containing gluten-free flour and 6 grams of salt. Rise 60 to 90 minutes. Par-bake 5 minutes, then 7 to 10 minutes with toppings.

Table: Gram weights for gluten-free sourdough discard pizza dough.

| Ingredient | Amount in the recipe | Grams | Source |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Gluten-free sourdough discard | about 1 cup | 200 g | Recipe amount. No FoodData Central food for gluten-free discard. |
| Warm water | 1/2 cup | 118.5 g | Half of the 237 g (8 fl oz) serving, [FDC 173647](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173647/nutrients) |
| Sugar, or honey | 1 tsp | 4.2 g, or 7 g | Sugar teaspoon, [FDC 169655](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169655/nutrients). Honey is one third of the 21 g tablespoon, [FDC 169640](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169640/nutrients), because that record has no teaspoon. |
| Instant yeast | 2 tsp | 8 g | Active-dry teaspoon is 4 g, [FDC 175043](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175043/nutrients). SR Legacy doesn't publish a separate instant-yeast teaspoon. |
| Gluten-free all-purpose flour blend with xanthan gum | 1 1/2 cups | 180 to 204 g | Branded quarter-cups differ. See below. |
| Salt | 1 tsp | 6 g | Table salt teaspoon, [FDC 173468](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173468/nutrients). Flaky sea salt can weigh less per spoon. |
| Olive oil | 2 tbsp | 27 g | 13.5 g per tablespoon, [FDC 171413](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171413/nutrients) |
| Xanthan gum, only if the blend omits it | 1 tsp | 2 g | Lorann label, [FDC 2396335](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2396335/nutrients). Skip it when the blend already includes xanthan. |

The flour range is six quarter-cups. Hy-Vee's gluten-free all-purpose flour lists [34 grams per 1/4 cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2482931/nutrients), which is 204 grams for 1 1/2 cups. Continental Mills lists [30 grams per 1/4 cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2596456/nutrients), which is 180 grams. Use the quarter-cup weight printed on your bag if it isn't 30 or 34 grams. White rice flour is a different food, [158 grams per cup](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169714/nutrients), and it isn't this blend. The 200 gram discard figure is the amount this recipe already printed. There's no USDA record to swap in for a gluten-free starter.

## Is sourdough discard pizza dough safe for celiac diets, and which gluten-free flour and binder ratio crisps best?

Only if that starter was fed gluten-free flour, never wheat. [FDA 21 CFR 101.91](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-101.91) requires under 20 ppm gluten, and wheat flour in the jar is a gluten-containing grain. Psyllium husk on brown rice and tapioca takes more water and a longer par-bake than xanthan gum. The gram table uses USDA FoodData Central [169640](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169640/nutrients), [169655](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169655/nutrients), [169714](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169714/nutrients), and [175043](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175043/nutrients).

A wheat-fed starter and a starter fed only gluten-free flour aren't the same jar. The rule names wheat, rye, and barley as gluten-containing grains, and it says a fermented food has to already qualify before fermentation. Sourdough is fermented. One wheat feeding fails that test before the dough hits the stone. A shared scoop from the wheat bin is the same mistake. Feed the jar on gluten-free flours only.

I don't toss this dough. I press it. The binder is why the slice either bends or snaps.

The blend I weigh when the bag doesn't decide for me is 120 grams of brown rice flour and 80 grams of tapioca starch. That's 200 grams. Hydration is the water divided by those 200 grams, and oil stays out of the percent. 220 divided by 200 is 110%. The 2 gram xanthan line is the teaspoon already in the gram table, [FDC 2396335](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2396335/nutrients). The 5-minute par-bake is the clock in the steps. Wetter doughs take the extra minute or two this page already allows before the sauce goes on.

If that jar is the 100 percent hydration the [sourdough discard calculator](/tools/sourdough-discard-calculator/) assumes, half of those grams are already water. This table is the flour on top of the jar, not a second accounting of it.

That's the crisp. Wheat in the starter, and you don't have a gluten-free crust. Psyllium and more water, and the slice bends. Xanthan and a shorter par-bake, and it snaps if you fold it like a New York slice.

## Why use discard in gluten free pizza?

Gluten free flour blends can sometimes taste a bit flat or dusty. The fermented discard adds a depth of flavor that mimics a slow-risen traditional dough. It also helps with the texture. Since we aren't dealing with gluten, we need every bit of structural help we can get, and the tang is flavor. Sourdough often lands around pH 4.0 to 4.8 ([Lau et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8306212/)), and that acidity isn't an established clinical benefit ([D'Amico et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10399781/)).

Most people think sourdough means waiting all day. By adding a little instant yeast to this gluten free sourdough discard pizza dough, we get the flavor of the old starter with the speed of a standard weeknight meal. It is the ultimate shortcut for people who have better things to do than watch dough not rise. It's the same idea behind my [same-day sourdough pizza dough](/easy-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough-no-yeast/), just adapted for GF flours.

## Handling the sticky dough situation

If you are used to tossing pizza dough in the air like a guy in a 90s commercial, prepare for a reality check. Gluten free dough is sticky. It's more like a thick cake batter that you have to coax into a circle. Don't try to knead this on a floured counter with your bare hands unless you want to spend twenty minutes scrubbing your cuticles.

**The Parchment Trick:** Always roll or press your dough out on parchment paper. It's the only way you'll get it into the oven without it tearing.
**Oiled Hands:** Rub a little olive oil on your palms before pressing the dough out. This prevents the "velcro effect" where the dough sticks to you more than the paper.
**The Par-Bake:** Never put raw toppings on raw gluten free dough. You'll end up with a soggy middle. Giving the crust five minutes in the oven alone ensures it's sturdy enough to hold the weight of your sauce and cheese.

## Choosing the right flour blend

Not all gluten free flours are created equal, and your choice will dictate whether your pizza is a success or a cardboard substitute. Make sure your blend contains xanthan gum. If it doesn't, you'll need to add about a teaspoon yourself, or the crust will crumble the moment you pick it up.

I usually go for a blend that is heavy on rice flour and potato starch for that classic crisp. A blend with too much bean flour might result in a weird aftertaste that even a mountain of pepperoni can't hide. If your starter is made from brown rice flour, it will add a nice nuttiness to the finished gluten free sourdough discard pizza dough.

## Getting the perfect crisp

To get a crust that actually withstands the "fold test," you need heat. A pizza stone is great, but a heavy-duty baking sheet flipped upside down works just as well. Preheat that sucker for at least 30 minutes at 450°F. You want the surface to be screaming hot when the parchment hits it.

If you like a thinner, New York-style crust, press the dough out until it's about a quarter-inch thick. If your kids demand a "hand-tossed" feel, leave it a bit thicker but increase the par-bake time by two minutes. Just don't walk away while it's in there; the line between "perfectly browned" and "charcoal" is thinner than you think.

While you could certainly spend your Saturday obsessing over hydration percentages and ambient room temperature, this recipe isn't about that. It’s about using what you have in the fridge to make a dinner that doesn't taste like a compromise. Toss on whatever cheese is on sale, use the jarred sauce, and enjoy the fact that you didn't have to throw your starter in the trash today.
