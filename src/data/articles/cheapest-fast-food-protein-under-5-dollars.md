You're third in line and the total has to stay under five bucks. That's not a nutrition philosophy. That's the number on the screen before you lose your nerve and add fries.

Ten a la carte items in fastfood-protein-per-dollar-2026.csv are priced at or under $5.00. Protein is the chain's own nutrition file. Price is whatever basis that row recorded, a national average or a store snapshot, and your zip code can move it. The filename stays in the sentence. This page doesn't link the file.

## Which fast-food items under $5 give the most protein per dollar?

Ten rows in fastfood-protein-per-dollar-2026.csv are priced at or under $5.00. Chipotle's High Protein Cup leads at $3.82, with 32 g of protein and 8.4 g per dollar. Wendy's Jr. Bacon Cheeseburger is $2.89 for 18 g, or 6.2 g per dollar. Taco Bell's Cheesy Bean and Rice Burrito is the cheapest row at $1.59 for 9 g.

## The cup still beats the sandwich

The High Protein Cup is chicken without the architecture. No bun doing a cost impression. 32 grams for $3.82 is 8.4 grams per dollar, and nothing else under $5 on this file gets close. Wendy's Jr. Bacon Cheeseburger is the fun second place, 18 grams, $2.89, 6.2 grams per dollar, and it beats the McDouble on the ratio even though the McDouble has more protein. More grams isn't the same contest as more grams per dollar.

The spicy chicken sandwich and the cheesy burrito share 5.7. One is $4.89 and 28 grams. The other is $1.59 and 9 grams. If the goal is the ratio, they're tied. If the goal is leaving with more protein, the sandwich wins and spends most of the five dollars. If the goal is the smallest number on the screen, the burrito wins and you might be hungry. I know which one I order when I'm actually hungry. It isn't the $1.59.

Small chili is the quiet Wendy's row, 19 grams for $3.39, 5.6 grams per dollar. It doesn't look like a protein order. The ratio doesn't care.

## Five dollars is a fence, not a medal

KFC's chicken breast is 7.6 grams per dollar and $5.10. It misses this page by a dime. Dave's Single is $5.49. The full ranking, including those misses, is [fast food protein per dollar](/fast-food-protein-per-dollar-ranked/). This page is only the rows that clear the fence.

Fiber is a different cut. Orders that clear 20 grams of protein and 10 grams of fiber are [fast food meals with both](/fast-food-meals-with-protein-and-fiber/), and several of those bowls sit in the $8 to $14 range on purpose. A cheap burrito and a high-fiber bowl aren't the same question. Don't staple them together and call it a meal plan.

## The 2,300 mg ceiling isn't in this price file

The [Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030](https://www.realfood.gov/documents/DGA.pdf) say people ages 14 and older should consume less than 2,300 mg of sodium a day. Children's ceilings are lower. This CSV doesn't carry sodium, so I won't borrow a milligram from a different menu and pretend it's this burger. Protein per dollar doesn't tell you whether the Jr. Bacon Cheeseburger spent a third of that day or half of it. If salt is the question, read the chain's own nutrition page for the item in your hand. The disclosure column is where that page is named.

## Groceries still lap the window

8.4 grams per dollar is the best number under $5 in this file. Dry black beans, in the grocery file, return 58.0. The can returns 30.1 and still beats the cup. The side-by-side, including the sodium the can adds, is [canned versus dry beans](/canned-vs-dry-beans-cost-and-sodium-per-serving/).
