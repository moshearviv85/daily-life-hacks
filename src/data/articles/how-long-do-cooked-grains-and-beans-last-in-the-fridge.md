The rice went into the fridge on Sunday. On Thursday somebody wants to know if Thursday still counts. I used to answer with whatever number I had heard last, which is how you get three different household policies and one suspicious container.

USDA publishes two clocks, and they don't match. [FoodKeeper](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/foodkeeper-app) gives cooked pasta, rice, and quinoa their own refrigerator windows. The [FSIS leftovers page](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) gives leftovers 3 to 4 days and doesn't break the grains out. Cooked beans never get a row with their name on it. Here's the reconciliation, from the English FoodKeeper file `FMA-Data-v128` and that FSIS page.

## How long do cooked grains and beans last in the fridge?

Cooked pasta is 3 to 5 days, cooked rice is 4 to 6, and cooked quinoa is 6 to 7, in a fridge held at 40°F or below. Cooked beans have no FoodKeeper row, so plain beans use leftovers without meat: 3 to 4 days. FSIS still says leftovers in general last 3 to 4 days. None of those windows start if the pot sat out past 2 hours.

![Range chart of refrigerator days for cooked beans, pasta, rice, and quinoa next to the FSIS 3 to 4 day leftovers rule](/images/how-long-do-cooked-grains-and-beans-last-in-the-fridge-chart.jpg)

The solid part of each bar is the first day of the range. The lighter part is the extra days FoodKeeper adds, not a guess at the middle. Beans sit on top of the FSIS bar because they use the same 3 to 4 days. Quinoa is the one that sticks out past a week, almost.

Table: Refrigerator windows for cooked grains and beans

| Food | Fridge | Freezer, quality | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leftovers, any food | 3 to 4 days | 3 to 4 months | [FSIS leftovers page](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) |
| Cooked beans, no meat | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 2 months | FoodKeeper 174, leftovers without meat, fish, poultry, or egg |
| Cooked beans with meat | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months | FoodKeeper 173, leftovers with meat, fish, poultry, or egg |
| Opened low-acid cans, beans included | 3 to 4 days | pantry row, not this clock | FoodKeeper 372, after opening |
| Opened refried beans | 2 to 3 days | not given on that row | FoodKeeper 499, after opening |
| Cooked pasta | 3 to 5 days | 1 to 2 months | FoodKeeper 177 |
| Cooked rice | 4 to 6 days | 6 months | FoodKeeper 178 |
| Cooked quinoa | 6 to 7 days | 6 to 12 months | FoodKeeper 426, quinoa, cooked |

Freezer months are quality. Food held at a steady 0°F can sit longer than the month count and still be safe, and it can also taste like the freezer. The fridge days are the argument.

## FoodKeeper and FSIS don't share one clock

Start with the awkward part, because it's inside FoodKeeper, not between two rival blogs. Product 174 is leftovers without meat, and the subtitle names cooked vegetables, rice, or potatoes. That row is 3 to 4 days. Product 178 is cooked rice, and that row is 4 to 6 days. Same dataset. Rice is on both. If you only read the leftovers subtitle, Thursday rice is done. If you only read the rice product, you've still got a day or two.

Pasta is the milder version of the same split. Product 177 says 3 to 5 days. The FSIS leftovers page says 3 to 4. A plain tub of pasta can cite the pasta row. Pasta mixed into a meat sauce is a leftover with meat, product 173, and that one goes back to 3 to 4 days. The grain didn't get a bonus because it shared a container with the chicken.

Quinoa is the outlier. Product 426 says 6 to 7 days in the fridge and 6 to 12 months in the freezer for quality. FSIS never names quinoa on the leftovers page. I won't pretend the 6 to 7 day row is a rumor, and I won't pretend the 3 to 4 day leftovers rule forgot how to read. They're answering at different levels. One is a named grain. One is every leftover in the house.

Beans are the row that isn't there. I looked for "cooked beans" in that v128 product sheet and it isn't a product. What you get instead:

* **Plain cooked beans** use product 174, leftovers without meat, 3 to 4 days. The subtitle doesn't say beans. It says vegetables, rice, or potatoes. Beans cooked without meat are the closest fit, and the fridge number matches the FSIS leftovers page anyway.
* **Beans with ham, bacon, or sausage** use product 173, leftovers with meat. Still 3 to 4 days in the fridge. The freezer quality window moves to 2 to 3 months.
* **An opened can of beans** is product 372, low-acid canned goods, and the subtitle lists beans. After opening, 3 to 4 days in the fridge. The count starts when you open the can, not when the can was packed.
* **Opened refried beans** are their own product, 499, and they're shorter: 2 to 3 days after opening. Don't give the refried tub the bean-pot number.

If you want one rule for every container, use 3 to 4 days. If you want the row with the food's name on it, pasta says 3 to 5, rice says 4 to 6, quinoa says 6 to 7, and beans say they were never given a name. The longer rows still assume the fridge stayed at 40°F or below. A warm fridge doesn't get to borrow day 6.

## The days don't count until the pot is cold

FSIS is blunt about the first two hours. Leftovers have to be refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is above 90°F. Food left out longer than that gets thrown out. The [danger zone](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) is 40°F to 140°F, and that's the range where bacteria grow fastest.

A big pot is the usual failure. The center stays warm while the lid looks like a plan. FSIS says to divide large amounts into shallow containers so the food actually reaches 40°F. Hot food can go straight into the refrigerator. You don't earn extra safety by letting a pot of rice "stop steaming" on the counter for an hour first. That's the hour.

Reheat leftovers to 165°F, checked in more than one spot if you used a microwave. Sauces and soups get a rolling boil. That reheating step is for food that was cooled on time. It isn't a refund for a pot that spent the afternoon next to the stove.

## Cooked rice has a second problem, and it isn't the smell

Rice gets the longer FoodKeeper window and the stricter lecture, which feels unfair until you read why people single it out. [FDA's Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/food/foodborne-pathogens/bad-bug-book-second-edition) describes *Bacillus cereus* illness from a toxin that can form in rice and other starchy foods left warm after cooking. A [CDC report on a fried-rice outbreak](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00025744.htm) makes the practical point: spores can survive cooking, and holding the rice at room temperature is when the bacteria multiply.

The rice can look fine. Smelling the container is not a test. That's also why I don't treat day 5 on the rice row as a casual leftover. The 4 to 6 day product assumes the rice got cold. If it sat in the cooker while everyone ate and then talked, the row doesn't apply, and neither does the 3 to 4 day leftovers rule. Both clocks start after the cool, not instead of it.

Grains other than rice aren't magically exempt from a warm kitchen. The Bad Bug Book puts the starchy-food note on more than one pot. Rice is just the food the outbreak writeups keep using. The same shallow-container habit covers barley, quinoa, and pasta. The [rice and beans weeknight](/can-you-eat-rice-and-beans-everyday/) runs into this every time the pot is bigger than dinner.

## What I do with the actual container

Label the lid with the food and the day it was cooked. Not the day you found it. If I know we'll eat it inside 3 days, it stays in the fridge, and I don't have a debate about day 6. If the week is longer than that, it goes to the freezer the same night, while the quality is still the quality I cooked.

That freezer list only works if the bag is findable later. A [simple freezer inventory](/freezer-inventory-simple-system/) is the other half of this page: name, date, and a spot you can see. The fridge is the short clock. The freezer is the pause. A container with no date is just a guess wearing a lid.
