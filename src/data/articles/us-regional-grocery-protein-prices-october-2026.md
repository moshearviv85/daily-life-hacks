A national average is a polite way to hide the West. Boneless chicken breast in the October 2026 file is $3.768 a pound in the Midwest and $4.514 a pound in the West. Same bird, same month, different receipt. If you shop in one of those regions, the US city average is a rumor about somebody else's cart.

The prices and the grams per dollar are the regional rows in bls-nutrition-per-dollar-latest.csv. The series is the [BLS average price program](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/average-prices.htm). Protein per 100 grams is [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/), joined onto each price. The file is named in the sentence. It isn't linked. A quality adjustment, which this page doesn't redo by region, is [plant versus animal protein per dollar](/plant-vs-animal-protein-cost-per-dollar/). The July animal lineup, one national price each, is [animal protein per dollar](/animal-protein-per-dollar-ranked/).

## Which U.S. region has the cheapest grocery protein per dollar in October 2026?

In the October 2026 [BLS rows](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/average-prices.htm), no region wins every staple. Midwest [chicken breast](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171077/nutrients) returns 27.09 grams per dollar at $3.768 a pound, against 22.61 grams at $4.514 in the West. South [ground beef](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174036/nutrients) returns 12.32 grams at $6.320, against 10.68 grams at $7.290 in the West. Northeast [pork chops](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/167839/nutrients) lead at 22.89 grams.

That's the answer I'd give at the meat case. Midwest for the boneless breast. South for the whole bird and for 100 percent ground beef. Northeast for the boneless chops, by a smaller gap. The West is the expensive end of the breast, the beef, and the cheddar rows that are actually October.

The gaps worth quoting are the October-versus-October ones. West chicken breast is $4.514 against the Midwest's $3.768, which is 19.8 percent more per pound, and the grams move the other way, 22.61 against 27.09. West ground beef is $7.290 against the South's $6.320, 15.3 percent more per pound, 10.68 grams against 12.32. West cheddar is $7.849 against the South's $5.521, 42.2 percent more per pound, 13.22 grams against 18.79. Those three are the spread. Pork is the quiet one: the Northeast's $4.271 against the Midwest's $4.622 is 8.2 percent, and every region still lands between 21.15 and 22.89 grams.

I checked the percent against the price strings in the file, not against a rounded dollar. ($4.514 minus $3.768) divided by $3.768 is 19.8 percent. ($7.290 minus $6.320) divided by $6.320 is 15.3 percent. ($7.849 minus $5.521) divided by $5.521 is 42.2 percent. ($4.622 minus $4.271) divided by $4.271 is 8.2 percent. Same arithmetic the [BLS average price](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/average-prices.htm) rows support, and no softer range pasted on top.

## The rows that aren't October

Five cells would lie if you let them vote. Northeast sirloin is 2023-06, at $10.064 and 8.97 grams. Northeast cheddar is 2024-10, at $6.516 and 15.92 grams. Midwest cheddar is 2025-04, at $5.673 and 18.29 grams. Northeast milk is 2024-10, at $4.420 and 27.82 grams. Midwest milk is 2020-04, at $2.574 and 47.78 grams.

That last one is the trap. 47.78 grams per dollar looks like the best protein deal on the page, and it's a gallon from April 2020. The chart omits it. The table keeps it, with the month in the cell, because hiding a row is how a later edit "discovers" a miracle Midwest. Among milk rows that are October 2026, the South is $3.927 and 31.32 grams, and the West is $4.546 and 27.05 grams. That's the milk comparison this month can support.

Sirloin has the same problem in one direction. The October prints put the Midwest at 7.19 grams and $12.555, the South at 6.80 grams and $13.269, and the West at 5.00 grams and $18.036. The Northeast's 8.97 grams would win, and that price is June 2023. I didn't let it win.

## Whole chicken already dropped the bone

The whole-bird price is the cheap number on the sticker, and the grams aren't naive about that. The file's edible fraction is 0.61, 276.7 grams of meat and skin per priced pound, on [FDC 171447](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171447/nutrients). After that cut, October still ranks the South first at 26.87 grams and $1.915 a pound, then the Midwest at 25.72 grams and $2.001, then the Northeast at 23.59 grams and $2.182, then the West at 23.56 grams and $2.184. You're buying a bird. The $1.915 isn't a boneless cutlet.

Set that next to boneless breast in the same region and the bird still holds up. South breast is 25.27 grams at $4.039. South whole chicken is 26.87 grams at $1.915, bone already removed from the protein math. Midwest breast wins the breast row at 27.09, and Midwest whole chicken is 25.72, so the cutlet wins there by a gram and a half. The ranking depends on which package you meant.

A national weekly food budget, without this regional split, is the [USDA Thrifty Food Plan](/usda-thrifty-food-plan-weekly-cost/). It answers a different question, a reference family's week, not which census region paid less for a pound of breast in October.

If you live in the Midwest, the boneless breast is the October win in this file. If you live in the South, the whole bird, the ground beef, and the cheddar rows that are actually this month are the cheap end. If you live in the Northeast, the chops are the small win, and the sirloin, cheddar, and milk prints you'd want are older than October. If you live in the West, the same foods cost more. The aisle doesn't change. The receipt does.
