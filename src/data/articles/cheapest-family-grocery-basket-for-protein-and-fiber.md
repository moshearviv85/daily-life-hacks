The berries looked like the responsible cart. The lentils looked like a pantry dare. I priced both for a family of 4, same job: 200.0 g of protein and 112.0 g of fiber in a day. The cheapest family grocery basket, the staple one, is $5.38. The convenience basket is $14.41.

This isn't four copies of a one-person menu. Protein day 1 in protein-day-cost-2026.csv is 52.7 g and $0.82, so four copies would be 210.8 g and $3.28, and they still wouldn't be this plate. Fiber day 1 in fiber-day-cost-2026.csv is 31.9 g and $0.62, so four copies would be 127.6 g and $2.48. The household target is 50 g of protein and 28 g of fiber for each person. The [50 gram protein day](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/) and the [100 gram day](/how-much-100-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/) stay one-person questions. The [combined 50 g and 30 g page](/cost-of-50g-protein-and-30g-fiber-per-day/) is what happens when you add two menus and buy the oats twice.

## The cheapest family grocery basket is the staple cart

Line cost is grams times the published price per 100 g, then the cent. Protein and fiber are grams times the published amount per 100 g, then the tenth. The day total is the unrounded sum, then that same rounding. Add the rounded fiber tenths and you get 111.9. The total stays 112.0 g. That's rounding, not a second basket.

The protein tenths land on 200.0 g, and the cents land on $5.38. Brown lentils are 77.7 g of protein per dollar. Dry black beans are 58.0 g of protein and 41.6 g of fiber. Flour is 96.0 g of protein and 77.8 g of fiber per dollar, which is why 220 g is $0.30 and still carries 23.5 g of fiber. Oats are 46.6 g of protein and 35.8 g of fiber. Eggs are 34.4 g of protein per dollar, and 250 g is five large eggs if the day file's 100 g is two eggs. Drumsticks are 50.3 g per dollar. Peas are 20.4 g of protein and 17.6 g of fiber. Carrots are 16.1 g of fiber per dollar and cabbage is 14.6, the same produce rows as [cheapest high-fiber fruits and vegetables](/cheapest-high-fiber-fruits-and-vegetables/).

The 180 g drumstick line is edible meat and skin ([FDC 172373](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172373/nutrients)). Edible fraction 0.67 means the bag for that portion is 268.7 g. The $0.65 already divides the bag price by 0.67. Subtract the bone again and the chart lies. Five eggs and 180 g of meat won't look like a feast. The dry goods are doing the quiet work.

## The convenience basket costs $14.41 for the same grams

Same targets. Different foods: chicken breast, 80/20 ground beef, canned black beans, whole wheat bread, and strawberries. Breast is 24.6 g of protein per dollar. Beef is 11.3. Canned black beans are 30.1 g of protein and 34.4 g of fiber, and that row is flagged proxy because the record names black turtle beans and the can is priced undrained. Bread is 20.1 g of protein and 9.7 g of fiber. Strawberries are 2.7 g of fiber per dollar.

1070 g of canned beans is a little under two and a half of the 439 g cans. 420 g of bread is about three quarters of the 567 g loaf. 690 g of strawberries is about one and a half of the 454 g boxes, and the edible fraction is 0.94, so 13.0 g of fiber isn't 690 g of flesh. The protein cell is 0.0 g because strawberries aren't on the protein file. Carrots and cabbage are 0.0 g for the same reason.

Bread is May 2026. Breast, beef, and the berries are July. I'm not pretending the loaf and the chicken came off the same tape. $14.41 is about 2.7 times $5.38 because beef is 11.3 g of protein per dollar and strawberries are 2.7 g of fiber per dollar. I'd still buy the berries when they're the fruit people will eat. I wouldn't call that box the cheap way to 112 g.

The week is seven times the unrounded day, then the cent. Seven times the rounded $5.38 is $37.66, and the chart says $37.63. Seven times $14.41 is $100.87, and the chart says $100.84. Tap the rounded day in seven times and you'll argue with the picture.

Either basket is 1400.0 g of protein and 784.0 g of fiber across the week. You buy seven portions in one trip. Oil and sauce aren't in the number. Eggs, dry black beans, drumsticks, breast, beef, and strawberries are July 2026 rows. Bread is May 2026. Oats, lentils, flour, peas, carrots, cabbage, and the canned beans have a blank month. I didn't stamp July on a blank cell.

The second grocery run is where the cheap cart goes to die. You planned the lentils, then Wednesday wants berries, and the $5.38 day turns into the $14.41 day in pieces. The [week of dinners with fewer grocery runs](/plan-week-of-dinners-fewer-grocery-runs/) is the habit. This page is the receipt.
