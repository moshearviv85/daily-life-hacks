The albacore can has the better font. It also has the mercury number you don't want. The $0.98 chunk light can beside it leads this four-fish list at 22.4 grams of protein per dollar.

I used to grab the white-tuna label because it looked like the upgrade. The [FDA and EPA fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) says the cheap can is the one on the friendlier list. That's rare in a grocery aisle, and it's the whole reason this page exists.

## Which canned or frozen fish gives the most protein per dollar with the lowest mercury?

[Chunk light tuna](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173709/nutrients) leads the four at 22.4 grams of protein per dollar, then canned pink salmon at 21.6, frozen tilapia at 20.9, and sardines in oil at 20.2. [FDA and EPA](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) call all four Best Choices. Their mean mercury runs 0.013 to 0.126 ppm, against 0.350 ppm for canned albacore.

Lowest mercury and most protein per dollar aren't the same fish. Sardines and tilapia sit at 0.013 ppm on the [FDA mercury table](https://www.fda.gov/food/environmental-contaminants-food/mercury-levels-commercial-fish-and-shellfish-1990-2012). Canned light tuna is higher, at 0.126 ppm, and still a Best Choice. Canned albacore is the one at 0.350 ppm, on the Good Choices list. The price file doesn't sell you a mercury ranking. It sells you four rates, and the FDA table sits next to them.

The packages behind those rates: tuna is a 5 ounce can, 113 grams drained, at $0.98. Pink salmon is a 14.75 ounce can at $3.80. Sardines are a 3.75 ounce can, 92 grams drained, at $1.12. Tilapia is a 4 pound bag at $17.47. All four prices are July 2026. Tuna, salmon, and sardines are exact matches. Tilapia is marked proxy, because [FDC 175176](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175176/nutrients) is raw tilapia and the sticker is a frozen bag. The record even publishes a 116 gram fillet. It doesn't describe glaze.

Sodium is the part people skip while they're celebrating the protein. Pink salmon is the salty one of the three cans, at 403 mg per 100 grams ([FDC 173690](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173690/nutrients)). Sardines are 307 mg. Tuna is 247 mg. Tilapia's 52 mg is the raw fillet, and it isn't a row in the sodium file. Don't put that 52 next to the tuna can and declare a winner. One is raw. The others were canned.

## Albacore is the font, not the list

Best Choices means 2 to 3 servings a week in the [FDA and EPA advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish). A serving there is 4 ounces. The drained tuna can is 113 grams, which is about 4 ounces, so one can is about one of those servings and about 22 grams of protein ([FDC 173709](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173709/nutrients)). That's a lunch, not a personality.

Canned albacore is a Good Choice: 1 serving a week, and 0.350 ppm on the same [mercury table](https://www.fda.gov/food/environmental-contaminants-food/mercury-levels-commercial-fish-and-shellfish-1990-2012). I didn't invent a price for it. If the white-tuna can isn't in the grocery file, it doesn't get a grams-per-dollar bar. The chart would look more dramatic, and it would be fake.

The means are 1990 to 2012 commercial samples, not a test of the can in your hand. A different brand can land somewhere else inside that range. The list is still the list. Light tuna, salmon, sardines, and tilapia are the Best Choices side. Albacore isn't.

## What 30 grams costs, and what a whole day costs

Thirty grams of [tuna](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173709/nutrients) protein is $1.34. Salmon is $1.39. Tilapia is $1.44. Sardines are $1.49. The spread across the four is 15 cents. That's nothing next to the gap between a lentil day and a beef day.

If the question is a full day near 100 grams, not one can, the priced templates are on [what 100 grams of protein costs per day](/how-much-100-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/). One of those days uses this same tuna can. The wider animal counter, drumsticks through bacon, is [animal protein per dollar](/animal-protein-per-dollar-ranked/). Drumsticks still beat every fish here, at 50.3 grams per dollar, and they aren't a Best Choices conversation. Different aisle.

I'd buy the chunk light can for the lunch that has to be fish, and I wouldn't pay the albacore font for the privilege. The chart is four bars and a mercury column. The receipt can still talk you into the pretty label. The table is there so you don't have to.
