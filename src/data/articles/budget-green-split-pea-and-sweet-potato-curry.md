A bowl is a quarter of the curry and a quarter of the rice. The grams below are the dry peas and the dry rice, not the cooked volume. Protein and fiber are the USDA figures for those raw weights. The pot doesn't get a cooking-loss discount. The chart is the four priced foods, and it's a fiber chart because the peas are the whole story.

## Why the priced rows are $0.42

The unrounded sum of those four lines is $0.42. Round each one to the cent and you get $0.19, $0.13, $0.07, and $0.04, which add to $0.43. Same bowl. The card uses $0.42.

## Dry peas, not a can

A cup of dry split peas is 196 grams on [FDC 172428](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172428/nutrients). This pot uses 240 grams, a scant 1 1/4 cups. No soak. If it looks like mortar before the peas are soft, add a splash of water. If it still looks like soup at 40 minutes, take the lid off. You want a curry you can set on rice, not a bowl you drink.

The onion is 60 grams, about half a small onion. The sweet potato is 130 grams, one 5 inch root on [FDC 168482](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168482/nutrients), in 1/2 inch cubes. This isn't a soup with a ham bone. The can-versus-bag math for a different dry legume is the [dried beans converter](/tools/dried-beans-to-canned-converter/). These peas stay in the bag until the pot.

## The rice is in the bowl

250 grams of dry brown rice is the batch. A serving is 62.5 grams, 4.71 grams of protein and 2.25 grams of fiber ([FDC 169703](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169703/nutrients)). Leave the rice out and the 19.9 grams and the $0.42 are both wrong. Cook it in a second pot. Split peas and rice argue about water, and the rice loses.

A cup of dry brown rice on that record is 185 grams, so this batch wants 2 3/4 cups of water, not 2. About 40 minutes.
