Seven dinners, priced before you leave the house, come to $3.70 a person. That's the whole point of meal planning on a budget: you find out what the week costs while you can still change it, instead of at the register.

Most meal planning advice is about deciding what to eat. That part's easy. The part that saves money is writing a number next to each decision, and almost nobody does it because they don't know what a plate costs. We priced 165 foods, so here's a week where every line has a figure attached.

| Day | Dinner | Per serving | Running total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Split pea soup | $0.28 | $0.28 |
| Tuesday | Pinto beans and brown rice | $0.32 | $0.60 |
| Wednesday | Black bean tacos with slaw | $0.41 | $1.01 |
| Thursday | Pasta with canned black beans | $0.70 | $1.71 |
| Friday | Drumsticks, rice, frozen peas | $0.88 | $2.59 |
| Saturday | Lentil soup with carrots | $0.58 | $3.17 |
| Sunday | Chickpea curry with rice | $0.53 | $3.70 |

*Source: USDA FoodData Central + US store prices, July 2026. Full methodology at [/methodology/](/methodology/). Costs count only the components we have priced.*

## How do you meal plan on a budget without a spreadsheet?

Three columns on a scrap of paper. Day, dinner, price. That's it, and it takes about six minutes.

The trick is that you only need prices for anchors, not for whole recipes. A 90 gram serving of dry pinto beans is $0.20. A 60 gram serving of dry rice is $0.09 to $0.12. Two eggs are $0.37. A can of tuna is $0.98. Learn eight of those numbers and you can price any dinner in your head, which is a genuinely useful party trick if you go to very boring parties. We costed ten plates that way in [cheap dinner ideas](/cheap-dinner-ideas-cost-per-serving/).

Then work backwards. If you want the week under a certain number, you don't cut portions, you swap anchors. Moving one dinner from ground beef to beans saves more than an entire week of coupon clipping, because beef returns 11.5 grams of protein per dollar against 97.9 for pintos. The full spread is in [protein per dollar](/protein-per-dollar-cheapest-protein-sources/).

## What does a week of budget dinners actually cost?

$3.70 a person for the seven above. Six of them are anchored on a dry legume and one on drumsticks, which is why the total looks implausible.

Scale it and it holds. Four people is roughly $14.80 of dinner ingredients for the week, because grams cost the same per gram regardless of how many bowls you fill. That's the entire mechanism, and it's why big packages favor big households.

Dinner isn't your whole grocery bill, though, and pretending otherwise is how meal plans lose credibility. Breakfast, lunch, coffee, snacks, and staples usually cost more than dinner does. The honest way to use this number is as a floor you can build on, and [what USDA says a thrifty week costs](/usda-thrifty-food-plan-weekly-cost/) gives you the ceiling to check against.

## How is meal planning different from meal prep?

Planning decides. Prep cooks. You can do the first without ever doing the second, and if you only pick one, pick planning, because the money is decided at the shelf.

Prep is what protects the plan from a bad Wednesday. If Wednesday's beans are already cooked and in the fridge, Wednesday's takeout impulse loses. If they aren't, it usually wins. That's the whole argument, and [meal prep for beginners](/meal-prep-for-beginners-complete-system/) has the system, while [how to meal prep on a budget](/how-to-meal-prep-on-a-budget-for-one-person/) covers the solo version.

Overlap your ingredients on purpose and both jobs get easier. Plan two pots that reheat well and you've bought yourself four dinners, which is the math behind [cheap healthy meals with leftovers](/cheap-healthy-meals-with-leftovers/). One bag of dry pintos covers Tuesday and half of Wednesday. One head of cabbage covers the tacos and a side salad. There's a whole method for this in [one theme, five dinners, one grocery list](/one-theme-five-dinners-one-grocery-list/).

## How do you plan for 2, 4, or 6 people?

Multiply the pot, keep the menu. A beans and rice pot at $0.38 a serving is about $1.52 for four and $2.28 for six, so headcount changes the total without changing the plan.

What does change is package math. A four pound bag of pintos at $3.97 is twenty servings, which a single person will still be eating in November and a family of six will finish in five days. Shopping for one means either smaller bags at worse unit prices or a freezer strategy, a problem we worked through in [budget meal ideas for one](/budget-meal-ideas-for-one/).

For bigger households the advice inverts. Buy the big bag, buy the drumsticks in the five pound pack, and let [how to stretch meals for large families](/how-to-stretch-meals-large-families/) handle the rest.

## Where budget meal plans usually fall apart

Wednesday. Not for lack of a plan, but because the plan assumed a version of you that isn't tired.

Build in one lazy night on purpose. Pasta with a can of beans takes twelve minutes and costs $0.70, which is cheaper than the meal you'd otherwise order in a moment of weakness. A plan with no escape hatch is a plan that gets abandoned entirely rather than adjusted.

The other leak is the produce you bought hopefully and threw out guiltily. Food you paid for and binned costs full price and feeds nobody, which is why [reducing food waste at home](/how-to-reduce-food-waste-at-home-easy-tips/) does more for a budget than most shopping tricks. Plan fewer fresh items and give each one two jobs.

Read the totals as a comparison between plans, not a promise about your store.
