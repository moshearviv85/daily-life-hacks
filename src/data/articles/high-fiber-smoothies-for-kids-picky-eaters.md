I swear, my youngest thinks anything green is a monster trying to get him. A little stalk of broccoli? Nope. A pea? not. It's a battlefield, dinner time, every single night. And as a parent, you're just trying to make sure they, you know, grow properly and get all the good stuff their little bodies need, like fiber, which could be really helpful for digestive health and keeping things moving along, if you catch my drift.

One day, I just stared at a bunch of spinach in the fridge. And then at a banana. And then at my kid, who was currently attempting to feed a perfectly good carrot stick to the dog under the table. Lightbulb moment. Smoothies. But not just any smoothie; a truly deceptive, delicious smoothie where the veggies vanish. We need high fiber smoothies for kids picky eaters. This is the way.

## Why Fiber is, Like, a Huge Deal for Little Humans

Okay, fiber. It's not just for grown-ups reading cereal boxes. The National Academies Adequate Intake is [19 grams a day at ages 1 to 3 and 25 grams at ages 4 to 8](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/9fb9fae6-337c-4b7c-9821-2c81d1f65ad0). I'm not going to claim a smoothie hits that. A sudden jump can also mean gas, because gut bacteria ferment the extra fiber ([Eswaran et al., 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3705355/)). So finding a way to add a few grams without a fight, well, that's the parenting move. (Smoothies are just the opening move; these high-fiber dinners for picky kids carry the same strategy all the way to the dinner table.)

And veggies, like spinach? A cup of raw spinach is only about 30 grams, which is under 1 gram of fiber at [2.2 grams per 100 grams](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168462/nutrients). Spinach is here because the flavor hides. The fiber is in the fruit and the seeds. The 126-gram banana serving is about 3 grams of fiber at [2.6 grams per 100 grams](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173944/nutrients). But how do you get a kid who'd rather eat dirt (and sometimes does, let's be honest) to consume a leafy green? Blend it till it's unrecognizable and tastes like a milkshake. Genius.

## The Stealthy Smoothie Strategy: Tips for Veggie Disappearance

Before we get to the recipes, a few pro-tips from someone who has battled many a tiny food critic.

### Choose Your Weapon (Ingredients, I Mean)

**Frozen Fruit's Your Bestie:** Seriously. It makes the smoothie thick, cold, and eliminates the need for ice (which can water down the flavor). Berries, especially strawberries and raspberries, have a strong flavor that masks greens really well. Bananas are excellent for creaminess and sweetness. Frozen spinach and cauliflower blend in the same way, and they're usually the cheaper buy, which you can see in this comparison of what frozen and fresh vegetables cost per gram of fiber.

**A Liquid Base That Works:** Almond milk, oat milk, or even regular dairy milk if that's your jam. Sometimes a little fruit juice with no added sugar can help, but don't overdo it. Water works too, but it's less creamy.

**The Green of Choice:** Spinach is king here. It has a mild flavor and blends down to nothing. Kale is a bit stronger, so maybe save that for when they're hooked. You can really pile it in without much notice.

### Blend It Like You Mean It

A good blender is key. You want no flecks of green. None. Zip. If you see green bits, blend more. Keep blending. Your kid will spot it. They have radar for that stuff.

### Presentation Matters (Sort Of)

Use fun cups, maybe a silly straw. If it looks exciting, they might be more inclined to try it. But don't make a big deal about what's *in* it. Just present it like it's the most normal, delicious thing ever. "Here's your pink drink!"

## Recipe 1: The OG Strawberry-Banana Spinach Smash

This is where it all began for us. A true classic. It's sweet, it's pink, and it's packed with fiber.

**Ingredients:**
* 1 cup (packed) fresh spinach (seriously, pack it in there)
* 1 ripe banana (the riper, the sweeter)
* 1 cup frozen strawberries
* ½ cup milk (almond, oat, or dairy)
* Optional: 1-2 tablespoons plain yogurt for extra creaminess
* Optional: a tiny dash of vanilla extract (really helps with the flavor profile)

**Instructions:**
1. Toss the spinach and milk into your blender first. Blend until the spinach is gone. No green bits. This is.
2. Add the banana, frozen strawberries, and any optional ingredients.
3. Blend again until super smooth and creamy. If it's too thick, add a tiny bit more milk, a tablespoon at a time, until it reaches your desired consistency.
4. Pour and serve immediately. Watch them slurp it down.

## Recipe 2: Berry Blast Banana Bliss (Still Hides Spinach!)

So, they liked the first one? Great! Let's add a little more berry goodness. This one has a deeper color, making it even harder to detect the sneaky green stuff.

**Ingredients:**
* 1 cup (packed) fresh spinach
* 1 ripe banana
* ½ cup frozen strawberries
* ½ cup frozen mixed berries (blueberries, raspberries, whatever you have)
* ¾ cup milk (or slightly more, depending on your desired thickness)
* Optional: 1 tablespoon chia seeds (a hidden fiber boost!)

**Instructions:**
1. Same drill: spinach and milk into the blender. Blend until smooth, no green.
2. Add the banana, both types of frozen berries, and chia seeds if you're using them.
3. Blend, blend, blend until everything is creamy and vibrant. Adjust milk for thickness.
4. Serve it up. They'll be none the wiser. My kid calls this the "purple potion."

That spoon of chia is doing more work than another handful of berries. Dried chia has [34.4 grams of fiber per 100 grams](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170554/nutrients), about 4 grams in a 12-gram spoon. A cup of strawberry halves is [about 3 grams](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/167762/nutrients) (152 grams at 2.0 grams per 100 grams). Where those foods land on price is the [fiber per dollar ranking](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/).

## Recipe 3: Cocoa Banana with a Spoon of Chia

This is the chocolate one from the questions above, with the portions the fiber number actually uses. No spinach. If you want it pink, you're in the wrong glass.

**Ingredients:**
* 1 ripe banana (126 grams, the USDA serving this page already uses)
* 1 tablespoon unsweetened cocoa powder (5.4 grams)
* 1 tablespoon chia seeds (12 grams)
* 50 grams avocado (the USDA NLEA serving, not the whole fruit)
* 1/2 cup milk (whole milk in the fiber math; almond or oat if that's the carton you own)

**Instructions:**
1. Blend the milk, cocoa, and chia first so the powder doesn't sit on the lid.
2. Add the banana and avocado. Blend until the spoon can't find a chia clump.
3. Taste it. Ripe banana is the sweetener. If it's still stern, add another slice of banana, not a pour of syrup, and know the fiber number moves with it.
4. Pour it and hand it over. Water on the side if this is a bigger fiber jump than yesterday's glass.
