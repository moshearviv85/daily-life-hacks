The can is $0.88 and the bag looks like a project. I've stood in that aisle with a weeknight already half over, doing the math badly and buying the can anyway. The math isn't actually hard. It's just rude.

A serving here is 20 grams of protein. Cost is 20 divided by the grams-per-dollar rate, rounded to the cent. The rates are canned-vs-dry-beans-cost-2026.csv. Fiber per dollar is the matching row in beans-double-win-fiber-protein-2026.csv. Sodium for the dry rows and for canned kidney beans is sodium-per-dollar-2026.csv. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the files.

## How much extra do canned beans cost per serving, and how much sodium do they add vs. dry beans?

Twenty grams of protein costs $0.34 from dry black beans and $0.66 from the can, about 1.9 times the money. Chickpeas go from $0.35 dry to $0.91 canned, about 2.6 times. Sodium jumps from 24 mg to 278 mg per 100 g for chickpeas, about 12 times, and from 5 mg to 384 mg for black beans, about 77 times.

## The 1.9 times and the 2.6 times

Divide, don't vibe. Dry black beans at 58.0 grams per dollar, divided by the can at 30.1, is about 1.9. Dry chickpeas at 56.7, divided by the can at 22.0, is about 2.6. That's the price penalty per gram of protein. A $0.88 can still looks cheaper than a $3.38 two-pound bag. The can is cheaper as an object. It isn't cheaper as protein.

Pinto at 57.6 against canned kidney at 23.4 is about 2.5, and I'm not going to pretend those are the same legume. The file simply doesn't have a dry kidney price. Navy sits next to them at 59.9 grams per dollar and 5 mg of sodium, still a dry bag, still a different bean. Lentils and split peas don't have a canned twin in this file at all. They lead the dollar column because nobody packed them in water and charged you for the water.

## Sodium is the part the can doesn't print on the sale tag

Five milligrams versus 384. That's the black bean pair, per 100 g, and 384 divided by 5 is 76.8, about 77 times. Chickpeas are the polite end: 278 divided by 24 is 11.6, about 12 times. Canned kidney at 296 mg against dry pinto at 12 mg is about 25 times. Against the dry beans that sit at 5 mg, 296 is about 59 times, which is the "about 60" people quote when they skip the black bean can. The black bean can is wider than that band. I kept it in the chart.

[FDA 21 CFR 101.61](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-101.61) is the sodium-claim rule. Less than 5 mg per reference amount is the sodium-free line. 35 mg or less is very low sodium, and 140 mg or less is low sodium, when the reference amount is over 30 g. The rule judges the reference amount, not a flat 100 g, so a 100 g column isn't a verdict on the label. It's still the only unit these files share. Every dry row here is 5 to 24 mg per 100 g. Every can is 278 to 384 mg per 100 g, already past 140 mg before you scale the scoop. The wider low-sodium grocery list, same milligrams, is [low-sodium foods by the dollar](/low-sodium-budget-foods-ranked/).

Drain and rinse and some of that sodium goes down the sink. These USDA records are the can as packed, liquid included. I don't have a rinsed number I'm willing to invent. The measured rinse, and the pantry foods under 140 mg per 100 g, are in [how to cut sodium in canned beans](/how-to-cut-sodium-in-canned-beans-and-budget-meals/).

## The can is still dinner when the bag is a rock

I buy the can on the nights the soak didn't happen. That's not a moral failure. It's a $0.32 to $0.56 premium on 20 grams of protein, plus a sodium column that doesn't care about your schedule.
