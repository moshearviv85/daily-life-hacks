What to do with 100 grams of sourdough discard is a small-batter question. Weigh the jar, then pick a recipe that already uses about that many grams. If you're hunting recipes that use a lot of sourdough discard, skip the one-cup pancake and start at 500 grams.

The [easy sourdough discard recipes for beginners](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/) are the batches this site actually writes down: a 113 gram flatbread, and pancakes, crackers, and a pizza crust at 227 grams. Heavier amounts later on this page are someone else's printed weight, with a link, not a guess from this kitchen.

## Find your amount first

Put the bowl on a scale, press tare, and pour. Cups lie when the starter is full of bubbles. I'd rather trust the grams than scrape a sticky cup over the sink.

| US volume on this site | Approximate weight |
|---|---:|
| 1/4 cup | 57 g |
| 1/2 cup | 113 g |
| 1 cup | 227 g |

Those three lines come from this site's [discard conversion guide](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/), which starts from [King Arthur Baking's 227 gram cup](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/sourdough-starter-recipe). For 100% hydration discard, half the weight is flour and half is water. So 100 grams is 50 grams of each.

Other writers print cup labels that don't match that chart. Kneaded That calls 100 grams a third of a cup. This site's third of a cup is about 76 grams. Breadtopia calls 200 grams three quarters of a cup. This site's three quarters of a cup is about 170 grams. Living Bread Baker calls 500 grams one and three quarters cups, which would be about 397 grams on our chart. Use their grams. Leave their cups on their pages.

## What to do with 100 grams of sourdough discard

Fifty grams and 60 grams are real questions, and this site doesn't have a cooked recipe at either weight. Sixty grams is only a few grams over the 57 gram quarter cup. That's still a small spoonful next to the 113 gram flatbread, not a second recipe.

At 100 grams you're close. Audrey Grubb's [sourdough lemon muffins](https://kneadedthat.com/sourdough-lemon-muffins/) print **100 grams** of discard, with a total time of about 35 minutes on her card. She also lists that 100 grams as a third of a cup. Weigh it.

If you want to stay with a recipe from this site, 100 grams is a little under the 113 gram flatbread. The measuring guide's scaling idea is the amount you have divided by the amount the recipe wants. Here that's about 0.88. The flatbread has no egg to divide, which is the usual headache. It still isn't a tested 100 gram card, so don't expect the 6 inch round to come out identical.

## If you have about 113 to 227 grams

This is the range the site's own recipes were written for.

| Recipe | Discard | Time to allow |
|---|---:|---|
| [Skillet flatbread](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/#skillet-flatbread-with-half-a-cup) | 113 g | 10 to 15 minutes |
| [Pancakes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/#recipe) | 227 g | 20 minutes |
| [No-roll crackers](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/#no-roll-crackers-for-a-full-cup) | 227 g | About 65 minutes, plus cooling |
| [Thin pizza crust](/easy-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough-no-yeast/) | 227 g | About 40 minutes |

The cracker method on that page follows [King Arthur's no-roll crackers](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/easiest-sourdough-discard-crackers-recipe). The pizza crust uses baking powder, not a yeast rise. Half a cup, 113 grams, is the flatbread. A full cup, 227 grams, is the other three. If the jar says 150 grams, the measuring guide already walks through that case against a 227 gram recipe. The multiplier is about 0.66. Scale the other ingredients too. Don't just pour less discard into the same bowl of flour.

Melissa Johnson at Breadtopia prints **200 grams** for a half-sheet batch of [spread crackers](https://breadtopia.com/easy-delicious-sourdough-discard-crackers/), with 15 minutes of prep and 25 minutes of baking on the card. That's the nearest published card to a slightly short cup.

## If you have 300 to 500 grams or more

Now you're past one of our batches. Two pancake batches, or two cracker sheets, are 454 grams. That's arithmetic from the 227 gram cards, not a new recipe. The oven time doesn't magically become one tray. You still bake them one after another.

Breadtopia shows the same cracker batter scaled to **300 grams** for a larger sheet, and she prints the baker's percentages so the oil, salt, and herbs move with the discard. Jenny Prior's [sourdough discard crepes](https://livingbreadbaker.com/sourdoughdiscardcrepes/) print **500 grams** for 12 large crepes, and **250 grams** for a 6 crepe batch. She says the batter is ready in less than 10 minutes. Cooking is still a minute or two per crepe after that. The title's "10 minutes" is the mixing, not the whole stack.

## If you have 1,000 grams or more

Rebekah Parr's [sourdough discard waffles](https://thatsourdoughgal.com/fast-easy-sourdough-discard-waffles/) print **1,000 grams** of discard, and the recipe card lists 10 minutes of prep and 20 minutes of cooking. She says to scale it in **250 gram** steps, and she wants discard from an established starter that's around 10 days old or less for that recipe. Living Bread Baker also prints a **1,000 gram** crepe batch, scaled to 24 crepes. A kilogram is a waffle iron or a crepe pan. It isn't four heroic skillets of our pancake card unless you actually want to cook four batches.

## Match the recipe to the clock, not just the jar

The jar and the clock have to agree. Ten minutes and a hungry table point at the 113 gram flatbread. Twenty minutes points at the pancakes. Forty minutes points at the baking-powder pizza crust or Breadtopia's cracker card. An hour plus cooling is the cracker sheet on this site. Crepes are fast to mix and slow if you're flipping twelve of them one at a time.

If the recipe is asking the starter itself to lift a loaf, you're in a different problem. That's [discard versus an active starter](/sourdough-discard-vs-active-starter/), not a bigger pancake.

## When the jar is too old or too young to use

Grams don't fix a jar you shouldn't bake with. Discard from a starter that isn't established yet doesn't belong in any row of this table. [When a new starter's discard is usable](/can-you-use-sourdough-discard-from-a-new-starter/) is a rise test, not a day on the calendar.

A heavy jar that's been sitting unlabeled is the other miss. [How long discard lasts in the fridge](/how-long-does-sourdough-discard-last/), and when to freeze it, is the clock for the collection date. Mold, pink or orange streaks, or a rotten smell end the plan no matter what the scale says.
