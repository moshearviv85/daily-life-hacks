Yes, and within two hours. USDA's [cold food storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) lists pizza under leftovers with 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator, which settles the underlying question: USDA counts pizza as a perishable food, not a shelf-stable one. The two hour clock comes from [FSIS leftovers guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety), which says leftovers must be refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking or of coming off a warming appliance.

I know the counter-argument, because I used to make it. The cheese is cooked, the crust is dry, it's basically bread. USDA does not agree, and it publishes a number.

| Leftover pizza | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerator, at 40F or below | 3 to 4 days | [USDA FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) |
| Freezer, at 0F or below | 1 to 2 months (quality only) | USDA FoodSafety.gov |
| Maximum time at room temperature | 2 hours | [USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) |
| Maximum time above 90F | 1 hour | USDA FSIS |
| Reheat to | 165F | USDA FSIS |

*Source: USDA FoodSafety.gov cold food storage chart, reviewed September 2023, and USDA FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety.*

## What actually happens to pizza on the counter?

It sits in the danger zone. USDA's [danger zone guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) puts that range between 40F and 140F, and says bacteria grow most rapidly there, "doubling in number in as little as 20 minutes."

A pizza that comes out of the oven at 200F does not jump to room temperature. It coasts down through that whole band and then parks in the middle of it. Six hours in a cardboard box is not six hours of a cooked food resting. It's several hours of ideal growing conditions.

FSIS's rule is unambiguous: throw away all perishable foods left at room temperature for more than 2 hours, and 1 hour if it's above 90F.

The FDA makes the same point about restaurant food in its [refrigerator guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety), and its version is the one that sticks with me: "If you can't get home within 2 hours after eating out, don't request a doggie bag."

## But it looks fine and smells fine

That's the part that catches people. FSIS's [food product dating guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating) explains that spoilage bacteria and the bacteria that cause illness are two different populations. Spoilage bacteria make food taste bad and smell off. The ones that make you sick often do not.

[Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-safety/refrigerator-storage) says it plainly: disease-causing bacteria can grow without changing the odor, color, or texture of the food.

So the smell test is not a test. It catches one category of problem and misses the other.

## What is the right way to store it?

Not in the box. The box is a poor cold conductor, it takes up half a shelf, and FSIS's cooling advice is to divide food into shallow containers so it cools fast, since improper cooling of cooked food is one of the most common causes of foodborne illness.

* **Stack slices with parchment or wrap between them** in a shallow covered container. FSIS says to cover leftovers or seal them in storage containers to keep bacteria out and stop the food picking up fridge odors.
* **Date the lid.** Day three is dinner, day six is not.
* **Freeze what you won't eat by day two.** FSIS says to freeze leftovers within 3 to 4 days, so freezing early is where the quality actually is.

## How should you reheat it?

To 165F, checked with a thermometer. FSIS says to reheat leftovers to that temperature, covering food so it heats all the way through, and to cover and rotate food in the microwave and check the temperature in several places because microwaves leave cold spots.

You can also go straight from frozen. FSIS says it's safe to reheat frozen leftovers without thawing, in the oven or microwave, and that it simply takes longer.
