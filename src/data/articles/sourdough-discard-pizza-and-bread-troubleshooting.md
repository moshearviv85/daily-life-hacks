The crust looked finished from the doorway and then gummed the knife. I blamed the oven. The jar was the one that lied. Sourdough discard pizza and no-yeast bread troubleshooting starts with the scoop: a stiff 50% jar isn't the pourable 100% discard those cards were written for, and the difference is flour you can weigh.

The dollars below are the whole wheat flour row in fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. The gram split is the same formula as the [hydration conversion chart](/sourdough-discard-hydration-conversion-chart/): flour is the discard weight divided by (1 + h), and h is the hydration percent divided by 100. Water is the rest of the scoop. I rounded the printed grams to a tenth. The cents use the unrounded flour. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the file.

## What a 50% scoop does to a 100% card

**100% is half and half.** A 227 g scoop, the cup on the [no-yeast pizza dough](/easy-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough-no-yeast/) and the [no-yeast sandwich bread](/sourdough-discard-sandwich-bread-no-yeast/), is 113.5 g of flour and 113.5 g of water.

**50% is two-thirds flour.** The same 227 g holds 151.3 g of flour and 75.7 g of water. That's 37.8 g more flour and 37.8 g less water than the pourable scoop. A 200 g scoop, the jar weight on the [gluten-free discard pizza](/gluten-free-sourdough-discard-pizza-dough/), is 100.0 g and 100.0 g at 100%, or 133.3 g of flour and 66.7 g of water at 50%. The stiff gap there is 33.3 g.

Pour the stiff jar into a card written for 100% and you've already changed the dough before you touch the flour bin. The pizza card still adds 180 g of flour and 75 g of water on top of a 227 g scoop. Those card grams assume the jar was half water. A 50% jar spent that water budget inside the scoop.

## The flour, the water, and what the bag cost

The bag is whole wheat flour, [FDC 168893](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168893/nutrients): $3.12 for 5 lb, 2268 g, 10.7 g of fiber and 13.21 g of protein per 100 g. Cost is unrounded flour grams times $3.12, divided by 2268, then half up to the cent. Fiber and protein in the table are that same unrounded flour times the per-100 g rate, then half up to a tenth. A 100 g scoop prints 13.2 g of protein because 13.21 g rounds there.

A starter you feed with all-purpose isn't this bag. A gluten-free starter isn't either. The gram split still applies. The receipt doesn't transfer.

## Gummy centers and pale crusts

A gummy pizza center is a thick round, wet toppings, or the stiff jar. The 50% scoop shorted the water and loaded the flour, so the middle never dried. Roll the 12-inch circle the card asks for. Bake at 450F for 12 to 15 minutes, until the bottom is browned and the center has no wet dough. Thick or wet toppings take the long end. A rim that looks ready, and a center that isn't, is how you serve paste.

Pale is a cold pan. The pizza card heats the sheet for at least 20 minutes at 450F. A room-temperature sheet gives you a blonde bottom. The sandwich loaf is the other oven: 350F, 50 to 60 minutes, center at 195 to 200F. A dark top and a wet middle means more time. Tent the loaf with foil if the crust is already brown.

The gluten-free crust par-bakes 5 minutes at 450F with no sauce, then 7 to 10 minutes with toppings. That card uses instant yeast. It isn't the no-yeast dough. A wheat-fed jar doesn't become gluten-free because you wanted it to. If that middle stays soft, follow the par-bake on its own page.

## Snap-back

The dough shrinks when you lift the pin. On these cards that's extra flour, or a rest you skipped. The stiff 227 g scoop is already 37.8 g ahead on flour. Another dusting on a sticky counter is flour the card didn't write. The pizza card kneads about a minute, then rests 10 minutes. That rest is the dough relaxing. Skip it and the gluten stays tight.

If the scoop was 50% and the card wanted 100%, fix the grams before you fight the pin. Pull 37.8 g of flour off a 227 g batch, or 33.3 g off a 200 g batch, and put that water weight back. Then follow the card.

## Acidity and the soda already in the loaf

The sandwich loaf uses 2.3 g of baking soda, half a teaspoon on [FDC 175040](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175040/nutrients), plus 9.2 g of baking powder, against 227 g of 100% discard. The soda is there because the jar is sour. A second half teaspoon on top of that 2.3 g tastes like the box.

The pizza card doesn't use soda. It uses 4.6 g of baking powder, one teaspoon on [FDC 172803](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172803/nutrients). If the pizza tastes sharply sour, that's the discard you poured. Feed the starter if you wanted a milder jar. Don't invent a soda dose for a card that left soda off.

I didn't price baking powder, yeast, or soda. Those tins aren't in the price file. The $0.14 to $0.21 is flour you already own. The lift is whatever the card wrote.

Weigh the scoop. Match 50% or 100% before you roll. Then let the card's clock finish the center. A pale rim is a cold pan. A soap taste is extra soda. The jar was never the oven.
