The jar is full, it's Saturday, and someone in the house has decided cinnamon rolls are the plan. A yeasted batch wants a rise you didn't start last night. These sourdough discard cinnamon rolls use no added yeast. Baking powder and baking soda do the lifting, and the pan goes in the oven as soon as the dough is wet.

This batch is 8 rolls from 240 grams of 100% hydration discard. The calorie line is about 313 per roll, from the flour, butter, and sugar weights on [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168894/nutrients). It's a sweet breakfast. It isn't a fiber project.

## Why the baking soda won't wait

Unfed discard is sour. That acid is what the baking soda uses. The same thing happens with the [discard pancakes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/): the batter foams the second the soda hits it. These rolls are that reaction in a log of dough.

Bake them right away, because the soda starts working as soon as the dough is wet. A 20-minute rest, the kind you'd give a yeast dough so it relaxes, spends the lift on the counter. You get a dense spiral and a pan that never really rose. I learned that the slow way, with a tray that looked like cinnamon cookies wearing a sugar coat.

There's no commercial yeast in the bowl. If you came here for sourdough discard cinnamon rolls and the yeast packet is empty, you're in the right kitchen. The starter still carries wild yeast. "No yeast" here means you don't add a packet and you don't wait for a rise. The [no-yeast discard list](/sourdough-discard-recipes-no-yeast/) keeps Lisa Bass's quick rolls as a method comparison. Her card is a different weight and a hotter oven. This one is the batch written down here.

| Ground cinnamon | 4 g |

At 100% hydration, those 240 grams of discard are about 120 grams of flour and 120 grams of water. The [discard gram guide](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/) is the cup chart if another card says cups. Stir the jar flat before you weigh it. Bubbles make a cup lie.

The extra 260 grams of all-purpose flour sits on top of the flour already in the jar. Whole milk at 120 grams, plus 45 grams of melted butter in the dough, keeps it soft enough to roll. A gallon of that milk isn't one national price. The October 2026 census-region cut is [US regional grocery protein prices](/us-regional-grocery-protein-prices-october-2026/). If the dough fights you and cracks, it was probably too dry, not too weak. Wet your hands. Don't dump in another handful of flour or the centers bake up tough.

If the jar isn't 100% hydration, stop and use the [discard gram guide](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/). A thicker starter is more flour than water, and this dough doesn't have spare liquid to hide that.

## Filling, the pan, and 350 degrees F

Soften 40 grams of butter so it spreads instead of tearing the dough. 80 grams of brown sugar and 4 grams of cinnamon is a thin, even coat, not a sand dune of sugar in the middle of the log. Leave a bare strip along one long edge so the seam sticks.

An 8-inch square pan holds 8 rolls. They can touch. They shouldn't be stacked. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F before you mix, because you aren't waiting on a rise. Bake 22 to 26 minutes. The tops should be golden and the center of a middle roll should be set. A wet, shiny middle means more time, not a higher rack.

I skip the glaze on this card. A powdered-sugar icing is delicious and it isn't in the 313 calorie line. If you add one, you're eating a different roll than the nutrition box describes.

## What 313 calories actually counts

The number is the raw ingredient weights divided by 8. Discard counts as half flour, because 100% hydration is half flour and half water, and the water has no calories. Flour is [FDC 168894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168894/nutrients). Butter is [FDC 173430](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173430/nutrients). Brown sugar is [FDC 168833](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168833/nutrients). The nutrition box under the recipe lists every row.

Toppings you invent later, extra butter for the pan, and a glaze aren't in that total. Neither is the little flour you use so the dough doesn't glue itself to the counter.

## Leftovers, and the jar you shouldn't bake

Cool the pan 10 minutes, then pull the rolls apart. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours, or one hour if the kitchen is above 90 degrees F. Use them within three to four days and reheat to 165 degrees F, following [USDA leftover guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety). A few minutes in a toaster oven brings the edges back. The microwave makes them steamy and then tough. I wouldn't.

Use discard from an established starter. Mold, pink or orange patches, or a rotten smell means the jar is done. Weighing it carefully doesn't fix that. If you only have about 113 grams, make the skillet flatbread on the [beginner discard recipes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/) instead of pretending this pan will shrink itself. Half a batch is a smaller pan and a shorter bake, and only if you weigh half of everything.
