A serving is a quarter of the pan. Protein and fiber are the USDA grams for that raw weight. Nothing here gets a cooking-loss discount. The chart is the four foods that carry the protein. ## Why the can and the thighs are doing different jobs

The thighs are the protein. A 142.5 gram thigh is the "thigh without skin" weight on [FDC 173627](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173627/nutrients). That's meat only, not a bone-in thigh you weigh with the bone still on.

The can is the fiber. One drained can is 254 grams on [FDC 173801](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173801/nutrients). Skip it and you still have a chicken pan. You don't have this 9.2 grams.

The 22.0 grams of protein per dollar on that canned-chickpeas row is the undrained can. The drained serving has 4.47 grams of protein, and the can still cost $0.98. I didn't paste 22.0 onto the beans you eat.

## The roast, and the broccoli problem

Heat is 425 degrees F. Potatoes and chickpeas start the roast. Dry the chickpeas first. Wet beans steam, and steamed chickpeas are just beige. One layer. If you pile them, you made a casserole by accident.

Broccoli and garlic wait 20 minutes. Broccoli that rides the whole roast turns into sad trees. Garlic that rides the whole roast tastes like the oven. The chicken should read 165 degrees F in the thickest part. Sweet potato cubes are 3/4 inch. Bigger than that, and you're negotiating with a microwave.

## Leftovers, without a new price

The $1.79 is the raw grocery list split four ways. Leftovers don't get cheaper. Cool the pan within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if the kitchen is above 90 degrees F, and eat it within 3 to 4 days. Reheat the chicken to 165 degrees F. The chickpeas won't be crisp tomorrow. They'll still be the fiber.
