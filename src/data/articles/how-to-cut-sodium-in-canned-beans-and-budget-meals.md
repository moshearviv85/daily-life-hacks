Sodium for the foods that have a row is sodium-per-dollar-2026.csv, 56 foods. Canned black beans and canned chickpeas aren't in that file. ## How to cut sodium in canned beans and budget pantry meals

Cook the dry bag when you have the evening. Open the regular can when you don't, then drain it and rinse it, and don't pretend the rinsed plate is the dry bean or a low-sodium label. Five of the fifteen foods here clear 140 mg per 100 g. The other ten don't.

The price gap for the same cans, protein per dollar, is [canned versus dry beans](/canned-vs-dry-beans-cost-and-sodium-per-serving/). This page is the sink, the 140 mg line, and the other pantry foods that blow past it.

## Fifteen foods against the 140 mg line

Orange is over 140 mg per 100 g. Slate is 140 or under. The dashed line is 140 mg on this chart, so a loaf and a bag of almonds share one ruler. [21 CFR 101.61](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-101.61) sets the low-sodium claim at 140 mg or less per reference amount, not per 100 g. A 100 g column isn't a verdict on the label. It's the only unit these files share.

## What draining and rinsing actually measured

Two labs. I'm not averaging them.

[Duyff, Mount, and Jones, 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15428052.2011.582405) measured five canned bean varieties. A 2-minute drain cut the mean from 503 mg to 321 mg per half-cup label serving, 36 percent. Then about 3.5 liters of lukewarm water, about 72°F, for 10 seconds, and another 2-minute drain. That rinse landed at 295 mg, 41 percent. The Bean Institute reports those same averages. The smaller brand-by-brand check is on [low-sodium foods by the dollar](/low-sodium-budget-foods-ranked/).

That half-cup mean is their cans. It isn't FDC 175188. I won't multiply 384 by 0.59 and call it dinner.

The USDA ARS figure is vegetables, not beans. [Haytowitz, 2011](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1096/fasebj.25.1_supplement.609.3), Nutrient Data Lab, rinsed corn, peas, and green beans with the same 3.5 liters and a 2-minute drain. Peas went from 261 mg per 100 g to 244 after draining, 5 percent, and to 231 after rinsing, 7 percent. Green beans lost nothing on the drain. The rinse took them from 231 to 214, 7 percent. Corn went from 205 to 185 on the drain, 10 percent, and to 162 after the rinse, 12 percent. Vitamin C dropped too: 10 percent in the green beans, 21 percent in the corn, 6 percent in the peas.

None of those vegetables are rows in the sodium file. I didn't paste 12 percent onto a kidney bean.

## Cook the bag when the evening is free

Dry black beans are 5 mg per 100 g. The regular black bean can is 384. About 77 times, and the can is still undrained. Drain it and you leave the 384. You don't arrive at the 5.

A 100 g scoop of dry beans isn't the cooked can. The files only share the 100 g unit, so that's the unit in the table. The dollar version, including the 1.9 times and 2.6 times protein gap, is the [cost and sodium page](/canned-vs-dry-beans-cost-and-sodium-per-serving/). Protein and fiber without this salt chart is [canned versus dried beans](/canned-beans-vs-dried-beans-nutrition/). The soak and the simmer are [how to cook dried beans](/how-to-cook-dried-beans-from-scratch/).

If the can is what's open, drain it, then a short lukewarm rinse. Your label is still your label.

## Bread, tuna, and the foods already under the line

The loaf is the loud one. Whole wheat bread is 455 mg per 100 g, a 567 g loaf in the sodium file. The whole loaf is 2,580 mg, rounded half up. 455 times 567, divided by 100. That's the loaf, not a slice. The file doesn't give me a slice.

The [Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030](https://www.realfood.gov/documents/DGA.pdf), say people ages 14 and older should consume less than 2,300 mg of sodium a day. Children's ceilings are lower. The whole loaf clears that adult ceiling before anyone cuts it.

Canned tuna is 247 mg per 100 g, drained solids, and the can in the file is 113 g. That's 279 mg in the can, 247 times 113, divided by 100, rounded half up. Rinsing a bean can and rinsing already-drained tuna aren't the same job.

Canned kidney beans are 296 mg per 100 g and the can is 439 g. On that math the undrained can is 1,299 mg. Dry black beans are a 907 g bag at 5 mg per 100 g, 45 mg in the bag. Different beans. Dry weight isn't cooked weight. One bag is 45 mg. One kidney can is 1,299 mg before you pour anything off.

Drumsticks are 106 mg, under the line, and the price note says 33 percent of the bag is bone. The 106 is meat and skin. I didn't shave it into a boneless number. Chicken breast is 45. Greek yogurt is 36. Almonds are 1. Frozen edamame is 6. Sunflower kernels are 9.

The wider protein screen, same 140 mg line, is [low-sodium high-protein foods](/low-sodium-high-protein-foods-per-dollar/). Dry bags, frozen vegetables, and cans, tuna included, are [canned, frozen, and dry staples](/how-much-protein-and-fiber-in-canned-vs-frozen-vs-dry-staples/).

Tonight, if the bag is still a rock, drain the can and rinse it. If the evening is free, cook the dry beans. The loaf is the one I'd look at twice. 2,580 mg in 567 grams is a lot of bread.
