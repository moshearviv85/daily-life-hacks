In our July 2026 US price study of 53 grocery foods, chia seeds led at 34.4 grams of fiber per 100 grams. Whole flaxseed followed at 27.3 grams and dry green split peas at 22.2. The 18.3-gram bran-flakes row uses a brand-specific USDA record as a proxy for a differently branded priced cereal. This is a bounded grocery comparison, not a claim about every food sold worldwide.

This is the ranking by fiber content, not by price. We have [a separate ranking by cost](/fiber-per-dollar-cheapest-high-fiber-foods/), and the two lists disagree in useful ways. Here's the content version.

## Why do seeds and dry beans dominate this list?

Because they're dry. That's most of it, and any list that doesn't say so is selling you something.

Fiber per 100 grams rewards food that has already had the water taken out. Dry split peas measure 22.2 grams per 100. Green cabbage measures 2.5. That looks like a nine-to-one blowout, and on the plate it isn't, because those peas will drink up a serious amount of water before anybody eats them and the cabbage arrives ready to go.

The ranking still points in a useful direction. In this 53-food sample, the first fresh fruit appears 24th, after seeds, dry legumes, grains, nuts, and dried fruit. Just don't read "100 grams" as "one serving." A 60 gram scoop of dry rolled oats delivers 6.1 grams of fiber, which is a real breakfast number and about 22 percent of the Daily Value.

## Which fruits and vegetables led our 53-food study?

Produce plays a different game, and the honest winners are smaller than the headline foods.

Avocado led our fresh list at 6.7 grams per 100 grams. Raspberries came in at 6.5, blackberries at 5.3, chopped kale at 4.1, brussels sprouts at 3.8. At the bottom, russet potatoes managed 1.3 and yellow onions 1.7. Blueberries, which get talked about like a superfood, finished the whole 53-food list near the floor at 2.4 grams per 100 grams.

None of this makes produce pointless. It makes it a different tool. Berries and vegetables come with water and volume, which is exactly what beans don't, and eating a lot of them is easy in a way that eating a lot of dry bran is not. The full fresh breakdown is in [the cheapest high-fiber vegetables and fruits](/produce-fiber-per-dollar-ranked/), and if you want the fruit-only version, [the best high fiber fruits](/best-high-fiber-fruits-for-weight-loss-list/) covers it.

## Which foods combined fiber and low price in this study?

Here's where the content ranking and the cost ranking part ways, and it matters.

In this 53-food study, chia seeds lead on fiber content and then land 12th on fiber per dollar, because a 32 ounce bag ran $9.44. Almonds have 12.5 grams per 100 grams and finish 36th on cost, at 8.8 grams of fiber per dollar. Within the same study, whole wheat flour ranks first on cost at 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar from a $3.12 five pound bag, despite sitting mid-pack on content at 10.7 grams per 100 grams.

Dry green split peas place near the top of both rankings in this study: 22.2 grams per 100 grams and 71.0 grams per dollar, from a $1.42 bag. That's the closest thing to a free lunch on the shelf, and it becomes [split pea soup](/split-pea-soup-recipe-high-fiber/) with almost no technique required. The popcorn row cannot currently support an equivalent claim as named: its 14.5 value matches USDA's air-popped record, not unpopped kernels, while the price came from kernels. We leave that row unresolved rather than quietly treating the two forms as interchangeable.

## How do you actually eat these?

Nobody eats bran by the spoonful twice, so the trick is hiding the heavyweights inside food you already like.

The bran-flakes row at 18.3 grams per 100 grams needs no cooking, but it is a proxy: that exact value comes from a POST Bran Flakes USDA record while the price came from Great Value. Chia seeds work best as a texture ingredient: stirred into [savory breakfast bowls](/savory-chia-seed-recipes-breakfast/) or thickened into [a chia raspberry jam](/high-fiber-raspberry-jam-recipe-chia/) that quietly stacks two of the top foods on this list into one spoonful. Dry beans go where dry beans always go, which is [a pot on Sunday](/how-to-cook-dried-beans-from-scratch/) and then everywhere else all week.

One thing worth saying before you go rearrange your pantry. If you're currently eating very little fiber, hitting 28 grams tomorrow tends to go badly. Build up over a couple of weeks and drink more water while you do it. We covered both in [how to increase fiber intake without gas](/how-to-increase-fiber-intake-without-gas/) and [the water and fiber rule](/water-and-fiber-the-golden-rule/).

The list above is a shopping guide, not a scoring system. Pick two or three things from the top half of this 53-food list that you'd genuinely put in your mouth, and the daily number sorts itself out. Where all of this fits into a week of real meals is laid out in [how to eat more fiber on a budget](/how-to-eat-more-fiber-on-a-budget-complete-guide/).
