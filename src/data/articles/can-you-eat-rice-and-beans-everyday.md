Yes, people do it, and the numbers behind it are better than the reputation. A dinner built from 90 grams of dry pinto beans and 60 grams of dry brown rice came to 23.8 grams of protein for about 32 cents in our audited day costing. That is roughly 48 percent of the FDA's 50 gram protein Daily Value from one very cheap plate.

The honest part of the answer is the part about what we can't see.

## What does a day of rice and beans actually cost?

Cents, which is the whole reason the question gets asked in the first place.

In our day costing work, the bean and rice portion of one dinner ran 90 grams of dry pinto beans at $0.20 for 19.3 grams of protein, plus 60 grams of dry brown rice at $0.12 for 4.5 grams. Thirty two cents, 23.8 grams of protein. Run the same plate for fiber and 60 grams of pinto beans plus 50 grams of brown rice gave 11.1 grams of fiber for 23 cents.

Those are ingredient costs, not a full day of eating, and they assume you cook from the bag. If you want the portion math per person laid out properly, we did that in [how much rice and beans per person per day](/how-much-rice-and-beans-per-person-per-day/).

## Is rice and beans good for you?

On the two things we measure, it holds up. Dry pinto beans topped our entire 49 food [protein per dollar ranking](/protein-per-dollar-cheapest-protein-sources/) at 97.9 grams per dollar and also delivered 70.8 grams of fiber per dollar, which is a combination almost nothing else in a grocery store manages. We wrote that double up in [beans win twice](/beans-double-win-fiber-protein/).

Now the limit, and it's a real one. Our 22 datasets cover protein, fiber, and price. That's it. No vitamins, no minerals, no iron figures, no calorie column anywhere. So when somebody asks whether you can live on rice and beans, we can tell you the protein and fiber math works and we cannot tell you anything about the rest. If micronutrients are your question, [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) is the source, not us.

I'd rather say that plainly than write a confident paragraph about nutritional completeness out of data we don't hold.

## Do rice and beans make a complete protein?

Legumes and grains complement each other's amino acid profiles. That's the careful version of the claim, and it's the one worth repeating instead of the folklore about combining them in the same bite.

The money version is more interesting. Split a dollar evenly between dry pinto beans and dry brown rice and it bought 67.2 grams of protein in our pairing study. Split it between pinto beans and whole wheat flour instead and it bought 97.0 grams, the best pair we tested. Rice is the traditional partner. It is not the cheapest one. The full ranking of 20 pairs is in [the cheapest complete protein pairs](/cheapest-complete-protein-pairs/), and the recipe version is [beans and rice as a complete protein meal](/beans-and-rice-complete-protein-meal/).

## What rice and beans will not do on its own

Cover everything, and stay interesting.

The variety problem is the one that actually ends these experiments. Nobody quits rice and beans because of a nutrient. They quit because it's Thursday and they've eaten the same bowl eleven times. That's a cooking problem, and it's solvable with acid and heat: lime, vinegar, hot sauce, pickled onions, whatever wakes the bowl up.

The other thing worth flagging is the fiber ramp. Eating this every day is a large jump in fiber for most people, and going from a low intake to 25 or 30 grams overnight is uncomfortable. Build up across a couple of weeks instead, and read [how to increase fiber intake without gas](/how-to-increase-fiber-intake-without-gas/) before you commit to a bowl a night.

Adding produce doesn't blow up the budget either. Frozen green peas returned 17.6 grams of fiber per dollar and frozen green beans 11.2, both from our [produce fiber ranking](/produce-fiber-per-dollar-ranked/). A bag of frozen vegetables stirred into the pot costs almost nothing and changes the plate.

## How to eat it every day without hating it

Change one variable at a time. The beans, the acid, or the shape.

Swap pinto for black beans and the whole bowl reads differently for the same money at 81.0 grams of protein per dollar. Turn it into [a burrito bowl for meal prep](/high-fiber-burrito-bowl-meal-prep/). Push the leftovers into [tacos](/easy-black-bean-tacos-weeknight-dinner/). Fry yesterday's rice hard in a pan and it stops being leftovers at all, which is most of the point of [using up leftover rice](/how-to-use-leftover-rice-creative-ideas/).

Cook once, eat three times. That's the only version of this that survives a real week, and the [budget playbook](/eat-healthy-on-a-budget-complete-playbook/) is built around the same idea.

Rice and beans every day is a genuinely good deal and a genuinely narrow menu. Treated as the base of the plate rather than the whole plate, it holds up fine. If you want the bean side of it done properly, start with [what beans to use for red beans and rice](/what-beans-for-red-beans-and-rice/) and [how long a cooked pot can safely sit out](/how-long-can-rice-and-beans-sit-out/).
