I added the two cheap days and felt clever for about four seconds. Rock-bottom protein, $0.82. Rock-bottom fiber, $0.62. Together, $1.44. Then I looked at the foods. Oats, split peas, pinto beans, brown rice. They're on both receipts. Adding the days doesn't buy a second nutrient. It buys the same bags twice.

The protein menus live in protein-day-cost-2026.csv. The fiber menus live in fiber-day-cost-2026.csv. Both files already price five tiers. This page is the part neither file does alone: one receipt that has to clear about 50 g of protein and about 30 g of fiber. The [50 gram protein day](/what-50-grams-of-protein-costs-per-day/) and the [7-day fiber list](/high-fiber-grocery-list-30-grams/) stay the source menus. The [label Daily Values](/fiber-protein-daily-values-explained/) are a different question from these shopping targets.

## How much does it cost per day to hit both 50 g of protein and 30 g of fiber?

**$0.82, if you keep the rock-bottom protein portions and count the fiber those foods already have.** That's 52.7 g of protein and 37.13 g of fiber. One pile of dry goods. No second trip for the fiber.

The 37.13 isn't a new lab pull. Oats, peanut butter, and the gram weights that match a fiber row are copied. Split peas, pintos, and rice are the fiber row times the extra grams:

* Rolled oats, 60 g: 7.9 g protein, 6.1 g fiber, $0.17. Same grams in both files. [FDC 173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients).
* Green split peas, 60 g: 13.9 g protein and $0.19 on the protein row. The fiber row is 50 g, 11.1 g, $0.16, so 60 g is 11.1 × 60/50 = 13.32 g. [FDC 172428](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172428/nutrients).
* Pinto beans, 90 g: 19.3 g protein and $0.20. The fiber row is 60 g, 9.3 g, $0.13, so 90 g is 9.3 × 90/60 = 13.95 g. [FDC 175199](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175199/nutrients).
* Brown rice, 60 g: 4.5 g protein and $0.12. The fiber row is 50 g, 1.8 g, $0.10, so 60 g is 1.8 × 60/50 = 2.16 g. [FDC 169703](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169703/nutrients).
* Peanut butter, 32 g: 7.1 g protein, 1.6 g fiber, $0.14. Same grams in both files. [FDC 174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients).

6.1 + 13.32 + 13.95 + 2.16 + 1.6 = 37.13 g of fiber. The protein column is the published 52.7 g. The cost is the published $0.82. The fiber day's pearled barley, 23 g and 3.6 g of fiber, stays on the fiber menu. This plate doesn't need it.

These are the nutrient-carrying foods, not a whole day of calories. Salt, oil, and whatever you put next to the beans aren't in the $0.82. Same rule as the two source files.

## Five tiers, and why you shouldn't add them

Each pair below is two published days with the same tier name. The last column is what you get if you pay both receipts. Day 3 doesn't even share a menu. The protein file's Day 3 is a meat-eater cook day. The fiber file's Day 3 is a fresh-produce day.

![Grouped bar chart of five daily tiers, comparing the cost of a 50 gram protein day with a 30 gram fiber day, from 62 cents to $14.42](/images/cost-of-50g-protein-and-30g-fiber-per-day-chart.jpg)

| Tier | Protein day | Protein | Fiber day | Fiber | Both receipts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rock-bottom dry goods | $0.82 | 52.7 g | $0.62 | 31.9 g | $1.44 |
| No-cook convenience | $2.05 | 53.5 g | $1.74 | 30.8 g | $3.79 |
| Day 3, different baskets | $2.78 | 52.3 g | $4.18 | 31.4 g | $6.96 |
| Restaurant day | $13.23 | 53.0 g | $14.42 | 31.0 g | $27.65 |
| Realistic mixed | $1.51 | 51.7 g | $1.99 | 35.0 g | $3.50 |

The rock-bottom fiber day is cheaper than the protein day and still misses the trick. Its portions are smaller, and it spends $0.06 on barley to finish 31.9 g. Scale the overlapping foods up to the protein portions and the fiber is already 37.13 g. You paid for it when you paid $0.82.

No-cook is the pair that mostly isn't the same food. The protein day is yogurt, tuna, bread, and milk, 53.5 g and $2.05. The fiber day is bran flakes, bread, peanut butter, prunes, chickpeas, and frozen peas, 30.8 g and $1.74. Bread shows up twice. Almost everything else doesn't. Adding them to $3.79 is closer to two real days than the dry-goods sum is.

Day 3 is the one I'd refuse to graph as a single shopper. Eggs, chicken breast, and ground beef are $2.78 and 52.3 g of protein. Raspberries, a banana, an avocado, carrots, and a sweet potato are $4.18 and 31.4 g of fiber. A meat day plus a produce day is $6.96. That's a contrast, not a combined plate.

The restaurant bars are the tall ones, and they aren't the same afternoon. McDonald's for the protein day is $13.23 and 53.0 g. Starbucks, Wendy's, and Taco Bell for the fiber day are $14.42 and 31.0 g. Together, $27.65. That's two takeout days. If the drive-thru is the plan, at least don't pretend it's one errand.

The realistic mixed days are the ones a person might actually repeat. Protein: eggs, peanut butter, bread, black beans, rice, milk, $1.51 and 51.7 g. Fiber: oats, a banana, bread, peanut butter, an apple, black beans, frozen broccoli, $1.99 and 35.0 g. The naive sum is $3.50. Bread, peanut butter, and the half can of black beans are doing double duty in that sum.

Across a year, the rock-bottom mistake gets loud. $0.82 × 365 is $299.30. $1.44 × 365 is $525.60. The $226.30 gap is the fiber-day receipt, bought again, for fiber the protein-day foods already hold. The [7-day protein list](/high-protein-grocery-list-50-grams/) is the week that shops the protein days once. This page is the reason the fiber week doesn't have to be a second cart of the same oats.

## One receipt with the seven foods

The dry-goods five already clear both targets. A normal day still has two more foods on it: 2 large eggs and half a can of black beans. Each food is on the receipt once. Nothing is entered twice.

| Food | Amount | Protein | Fiber | Cost | FDC |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Rolled oats | 60 g dry | 7.9 g | 6.1 g | $0.17 | [173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients) |
| Green split peas | 60 g dry | 13.9 g | 13.32 g | $0.19 | [172428](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172428/nutrients) |
| Pinto beans | 90 g dry | 19.3 g | 13.95 g | $0.20 | [175199](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175199/nutrients) |
| Brown rice | 60 g dry | 4.5 g | 2.16 g | $0.12 | [169703](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169703/nutrients) |
| Peanut butter | 32 g | 7.1 g | 1.6 g | $0.14 | [174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients) |
| 2 large eggs | 100 g | 12.6 g | no fiber row | $0.37 | [171287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients) |
| Canned black beans | 220 g, half can | 13.3 g | 15.2 g | $0.44 | [175188](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/175188/nutrients) |
| Receipt | | 78.6 g | 52.33 g | $1.63 | |

Eggs are in the protein file and not in the fiber file, so this table doesn't assign them a fiber gram. The 52.33 g is the other six foods: 37.13 from the dry goods plus 15.2 from the half can. Black beans are the one line where both files already use 220 g, and both price it at $0.44. Protein on that line is 13.3 g. Fiber is 15.2 g. Same can, two columns.

78.6 g and 52.33 g are not a tight 50 and 30. They're what happens when you keep every food on the list after the dry goods have already done the job. Drop the eggs and the can and you're back to $0.82, 52.7 g of protein, and 37.13 g of fiber. Keep them if you want breakfast and a can in the same day. The beans and the rice, eaten the same day, are also the pair behind [beans and rice as a complete protein](/beans-and-rice-complete-protein-meal/). Split peas still do the rude amount of work on both nutrients, which is the whole point of [scoring beans on protein and fiber together](/beans-double-win-fiber-protein/).

A can instead of the dry pintos is the convenience tax, not a different nutrient. The half can is $0.44 for 13.3 g of protein. The 90 g of dry pintos are $0.20 for 19.3 g. If the can is what gets beans onto the plate, [dried versus canned](/canned-beans-vs-dried-beans-nutrition/) is the longer argument. This receipt just refuses to charge you for both.

## What I'd actually buy

Oats, a bag of split peas, dry pintos, brown rice, a jar of peanut butter. That's the $0.82 day, and it already clears both numbers. Eggs and one can of black beans turn it into the $1.63 day if you want them. I wouldn't also buy the $0.62 fiber day, the $4.18 produce day, or a second restaurant.

The prices aren't frozen. Eggs, chicken, and beef move for years at a time in the [BLS protein-per-dollar trend from 2017 to October 2026](/protein-per-dollar-2017-to-2026-bls-trends/). The day files are the menus. That series is the reason a July snapshot isn't a decade.

One receipt. The oats don't need to be purchased twice to count twice.
