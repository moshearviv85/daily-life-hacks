The snack drawer had a bar in it that cost more than the popcorn I didn't buy. That's the annoying part. The cheap fiber is a bag on the grocery shelf. It just doesn't come with a wrapper speech.

The rates below are high-fiber-snacks-per-dollar-2026.csv, checked against fiber-per-dollar-2026.csv. Every food prints the same grams per dollar in both files. The filenames stay in the sentence. This page doesn't link the files. The protein version of this drawer, including the yogurt that has no fiber bar, is [cheapest portable high-protein snacks](/cheapest-high-protein-snacks-and-portable-foods/).

## Which high-fiber snacks are cheapest per dollar?

Popcorn kernels. 51.3 grams of fiber per dollar, a 2 lb bag at $2.28. Carrots are next, and they're the first row with a USDA id: 16.1 grams per dollar, a 2 lb bag at $1.58 ([FDC 170393](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170393/nutrients)). Dry roasted peanuts are 13.7, a 16 oz jar at $2.78 ([FDC 174262](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174262/nutrients)).

Five grams of fiber from the popcorn row costs $0.10. From the carrots, $0.31. From the peanuts, $0.36. That's 5 divided by the grams-per-dollar rate, rounded half up to the cent. Five grams is the chart unit. On a 28 gram Daily Value, high fiber is 5.6 grams per labeled serving. The bar is a nickel short of that line, and the line still belongs to the package, not to my handful.

## The popcorn row is a calculation

Both files print 51.3. The fiber file says the row isn't tied to a published record. There's no SR Legacy or Foundation entry for unpopped kernels in that note. The 12.9 grams per 100 grams is derived, not quoted, from air-popped popcorn at 14.5 grams per 100 grams ([FDC 167959](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/167959/nutrients)), using the moisture gap between kernels and popped corn. The file's sensitivity band is 12.8 to 13.0 grams per 100 grams. Rank holds across that band. It still isn't a USDA kernel assay.

If you want the bowl instead of the kernel math, the topping list is [high fiber popcorn toppings](/high-fiber-popcorn-toppings-healthy/). The popped bowl against the chip bag is [chips versus popcorn](/popcorn-vs-potato-chips-fiber-comparison/). Same snack, different question. One is the price of a gram. The other is what you put on it.

## Nuts, fruit, and the apple that loses

Peanuts win the nut rows. Sunflower kernels are close, at 13.1 grams per dollar, a 16 oz bag at $2.98 ([FDC 170562](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170562/nutrients)). Five grams cost $0.38. Almonds are 8.8 grams per dollar, a 16 oz bag at $6.47 ([FDC 170567](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170567/nutrients)). Five grams cost $0.57. Buy almonds because you want almonds. Don't ask them to be the plan.

The fiber file calls the peanut and peanut butter rows proxies. The price record doesn't name the salt variant. Salted and unsalted records match on fiber, 8.4 grams per 100 grams for the peanuts and 5.0 for the peanut butter. They don't match on sodium. This page stops at the fiber rate. Protein, and the rest of the nut shelf, is [cheapest nuts and seeds](/cheapest-nuts-and-seeds-for-protein-and-fiber/).

Peanut butter is 11.4 grams per dollar, a 40 oz jar at $4.97 ([FDC 174266](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174266/nutrients)). Five grams cost $0.44. That's a spoon, not a project. Bananas are 11.6, 65 cents a pound ([FDC 173944](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173944/nutrients)), $0.43 for five grams. Prunes are 8.1, a 16 oz bag at $3.98 ([FDC 168162](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168162/nutrients)), $0.62. Gala apples are 7.3, $1.28 a pound ([FDC 168204](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168204/nutrients)), $0.68. Raisins are last, 6.3 grams per dollar, a 20 oz canister at $3.34 ([FDC 2758980](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2758980/nutrients)), $0.79 for five grams.

The apple is last among the fruit and it still isn't a bad snack. It's a bad fiber-per-dollar snack. Different complaint.

## What I'd actually throw in the bag

Popcorn kernels if I'm popping them. Carrots if I want something I can eat in the car without a pot. Peanuts if the bag has to live in a coat pocket. The apple is for when I want an apple, and I'll pay the $0.68 for five grams without pretending it's the bargain.

Roasted chickpeas aren't in this file. This ranking is the grocery bag, not a sheet pan.
