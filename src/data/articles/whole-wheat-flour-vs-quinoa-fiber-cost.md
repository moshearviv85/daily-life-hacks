Whole wheat flour delivers 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar. Dry quinoa delivers 10.6. That's about 7.3 times more fiber for the same money, based on USDA figures and July 2026 prices, and it makes flour the single best fiber value in our whole ranking. The honest caveat is enormous, though: flour isn't food until you bake it, and quinoa is dinner in 15 minutes.

| Fiber per 100 g | 10.7 g | 7.0 g |
| What you buy | 5 lb bag | 2 lb bag |
| Price | $3.12 | $5.97 |

## Why is whole wheat flour 7.3 times cheaper for fiber?

Because both halves of the fraction go the wrong way for quinoa.

Start with price. A five pound bag of whole wheat flour ran $3.12, which is about 14 cents per 100 grams. The two pound bag of quinoa ran $5.97, about 66 cents per 100 grams. That's roughly 4.8 times more expensive before anyone mentions nutrition.

Then the density. Whole wheat flour carries 10.7 grams of fiber per 100 grams because it's the entire wheat berry, bran and germ still in the bag. Quinoa carries 7.0. Multiply a 4.8 times price penalty by a lower fiber count and you land at 7.3 times, which is exactly where the [fiber per dollar study](/fiber-per-dollar-cheapest-high-fiber-foods/) puts them: flour at number one out of 53 foods, quinoa at number 32.

Quinoa's reputation was built when it was the interesting new grain on the shelf. The price never really came back down.

## When is quinoa still the right buy?

When you need a cooked grain in 15 minutes and you're not turning on an oven.

This is the part the ranking can't see. Flour at 77.8 grams per dollar is theoretical until you've mixed dough, waited for it to rise, and heated a 400 degree oven. Quinoa goes in a pot with water and comes out done, which is why it works as a base for a [make-ahead quinoa lunch salad](/high-fiber-quinoa-salad-for-lunch-prep/) or the filling in [stuffed portobello mushrooms](/stuffed-portobello-mushrooms-quinoa-spinach-feta/). Flour cannot do either job. Not even a little.

Quinoa also carries 14.12 grams of protein per 100 grams in our [protein per dollar study](/protein-per-dollar-cheapest-protein-sources/), slightly ahead of whole wheat flour at 13.21, and it supplies a fuller amino acid profile on its own than most grains manage. Grains and legumes tend to complement each other on that front, so a bowl of rice and beans gets to a similar place, but quinoa gets there without a partner. Add that it's naturally gluten free and you have three real reasons to keep buying it.

Just buy it for those reasons. Not because someone told you it was the high fiber grain.

## What's the cheapest grain you can actually cook like quinoa?

Barley. This is the useful part of the whole comparison.

Pearled barley delivers 57.1 grams of fiber per dollar from a $1.24 bag, which is about 5.4 times what quinoa manages, and it does the same jobs. It goes chewy instead of fluffy, which is arguably better in a [hands-off mushroom barley risotto](/creamy-mushroom-barley-risotto-hands-off/). Bulgur at 24.4 is even faster, since it mostly just soaks, and it's the whole point of a [proper tabbouleh](/tabbouleh-salad-high-fiber-bulgur/).

Swap quinoa for barley in a grain bowl and almost nobody at the table notices. Your receipt does.

## Does this mean you should bake your own bread?

If you like baking, the math is on your side. Flour at 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar against a $1.97 loaf of store bought 100% whole wheat at 17.3 is about a 4.5 times spread, and that's before you consider that homemade bread tastes like something.

Start where the failure rate is lowest. A [simple sandwich loaf](/easy-sandwich-bread-recipe-beginners/) is mostly waiting, and [bran muffins that don't taste like penance](/high-fiber-bran-muffins-that-taste-good/) are a weeknight-sized commitment. If baking isn't your thing, buy barley and move on with your life.
