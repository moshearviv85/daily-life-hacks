If the bag thawed in the refrigerator, you can refreeze it. If it thawed on the counter for more than two hours, you can't. That's the whole rule, and it comes straight from USDA's [freezing and food safety guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety), which draws the line by where the food sat rather than by how it looks or smells.

I know the temptation. The peas look fine. They're still cold. Cold is not the standard.

## Can you refreeze vegetables that thawed in the fridge?

Yes, and FSIS says so in one sentence: once food is thawed in the refrigerator, it is safe to refreeze it without cooking, although there may be a loss of quality due to the moisture lost through thawing.

Notice what's doing the work there. The refrigerator held the vegetables below the temperature where bacteria get busy the whole time they were thawing. Nothing had a chance to multiply, so nothing changed about the safety picture.

The quality picture did change, and we'll get to why.

## How long can frozen vegetables sit out?

Two hours. One hour if it's above 90F, which covers a hot kitchen, a car, or a picnic table.

FSIS states the limit directly in the refreezing section: do not refreeze any foods left outside the refrigerator longer than 2 hours, and 1 hour in temperatures above 90F. The [danger zone guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) explains why the window is that tight. Bacteria grow most rapidly between 40F and 140F, "doubling in number in as little as 20 minutes."

Twenty minutes. That's a school pickup. This is why the grocery bag that sat in the trunk while you ran one more errand is a real question and not a paranoid one.

## What if the power went out?

Then you're grading the freezer, not the clock. FSIS says a full freezer will usually keep about 2 days with the door kept shut, and a half-full freezer about a day.

When power returns, the test is physical: if food is partly frozen, still has ice crystals, or is as cold as if it were in a refrigerator at 40F, it's safe to refreeze or use. Discard foods that have been warmer than 40F for more than 2 hours, and discard anything contaminated by raw meat juices.

FSIS also warns against the move everyone reaches for in winter, which is setting food outside in the snow. Sun exposure can thaw frozen food even in cold air, the outdoor temperature swings hour to hour, and animals get involved. Dry ice or a friend's freezer is the recommendation instead.

## Why do refrozen vegetables taste worse?

Because of what the ice does on the way in and out.

FSIS explains that rapid freezing keeps ice crystals small, while slow freezing creates large, disruptive crystals that damage cells during thawing and cause food to drip and lose juiciness. Commercial freezing is fast. Your home freezer, working on a bag that's already thawed and full of liquid water, is not.

So a refrozen bag of green beans is safe and softer. That's a soup ingredient now, not a sheet pan ingredient. Push it into a stew, a [rice and beans skillet](/quick-20-minute-high-fiber-meals-for-busy-days/), or anything with a sauce, and nobody will file a complaint.

If you'd rather not deal with the question at all, cook the thawed vegetables and freeze the finished dish. FSIS says after cooking raw foods which were previously frozen, it is safe to freeze the cooked foods, which is the whole logic behind [freezer meal prep](/freezer-meal-prep-ideas-for-beginners/).
