Crackers are what the jar is for when you don't want another pancake. These sourdough discard crackers are a rolled dough, not a smear of starter on a sheet pan. You chill it, you roll it thin, and the oven dries it out.

The batch is **227 grams of discard, 57 grams of butter, and 120 grams of all-purpose flour**, plus salt. King Arthur's [sourdough crackers](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/sourdough-crackers-recipe) print the same discard and the same butter with **113 grams of golden wheat flour**. Their [ingredient chart](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/ingredient-weight-chart) puts a cup of all-purpose at 120 grams. That's the swap on this card. Don't use both flours.

## Why this dough sits in the fridge

Mix the flour and salt with the butter before the discard goes in. Fat coats the flour, and the cracker stays shorter. A food processor does this without a workout. Pulse flour, salt, and cold-ish butter to a sandy meal, then add the discard and stop when it clumps.

The dough should be smooth and cohesive, not a batter. If it glues itself to your hands, you rushed the butter or the jar was thin. A teaspoon of flour on the bench is the fix, not another half cup.

Divide it in half. Smaller pieces roll more evenly, and even is the whole game. Wrap and chill about 30 minutes, until the slab is firm. King Arthur's card allows a couple of hours. Overnight is how you forget it behind the mustard. Thirty minutes is the plan.

Their card totals **1 hour 25 minutes**. Hands-on about 25 minutes, the chill, then 20 to 25 minutes of baking. The calorie figure on this page is an ingredient estimate for one eighth of the batch, about 157 calories, from the flour in the discard, the 120 grams of added flour, and the butter. It isn't a lab panel. A serving is a small plate, not one square. They yield about 100 crackers from the batch.

## Roll it thinner than your patience wants

Heat the oven to **350 degrees F** while the second piece waits in the fridge. Flour the parchment, the pin, and the dough. Lightly. A snowbank of flour bakes into a pale, dusty cracker.

**1/16 inch** is the snap, the Wheat Thin end of the scale. King Arthur's cracker post says **1/8 inch** still crunches, more like a sturdy wheat cracker, and those thicker squares need about **30 to 35 minutes** so the center dries. I roll to the thin one. A thick middle with brown edges is the failure mode, and it's annoying because the pan looks done from the doorway.

Ragged edges are fine. Cut squares about 1 1/4 inch and leave them touching. They separate as they bake. Dock each one with a fork if you want them flat. Skip the fork and a few will puff. That's not ruined. It's just a tiny pita you didn't order.

Rotate the sheets halfway, front to back and top to bottom. Pull them when the edges are browning and the center piece breaks clean. Cool on a rack. They crisp as they cool. Judging them hot is how you box a soft cracker and blame the recipe tomorrow.

Two rubber bands stacked on each end of the pin are King Arthur's trick for a consistent 1/16 inch gap. Fancy, and it works. A bench scraper to lift a stuck corner also works, and you already own that.

## Five ways to season the same dough

Keep the 227 grams, the 57 grams, and the 120 grams. Change the 2 tablespoons of dried herbs, or skip them. Salt the top either way.

- **Rosemary.** The one on their card. Dried rosemary, crushed a bit so it isn't a pine needle in every bite.
- **Black pepper.** Coarse pepper in the dough and a little on top. This is the one I make when the cheese plate is actually just cheddar from the drawer.
- **Sesame.** A spoon of seeds pressed into the oiled surface so they stay put. Into the dough is fine too, if you want them throughout.
- **Smoked paprika.** About 1 teaspoon in the dough, not a heaped cloud. The no-roll sheet on the [beginner page](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/#no-roll-crackers-for-a-full-cup) uses 1/2 teaspoon for a flourless batter. This dough can take a touch more because of the flour.
- **Cinnamon sugar.** Skip the herbs and the savory salt shower. A light mix of cinnamon and sugar on the oil. Watch it. Sugar browns faster than plain dough, so check at 18 minutes.

Everything-bagel seasoning is a poor fit for a long bake. It burns. Save it for something that leaves the oven sooner.

## When the tin should close

Cool them all the way. Then a tin or a jar. [FoodKeeper](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/foodkeeper-app) doesn't give homemade crackers a week, so don't treat that as a federal date. Freeze what you won't finish. [Food at 0°F can be kept indefinitely](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts). I freeze half, because an open tin on the counter is a suggestion, not a storage plan.

A bendy center means more oven, not a different recipe. Spread the done edges off the sheet and put the middle back in. Next time, roll more evenly.

Use discard from a starter that already rises after feeding. Don't bake a jar with mold, pink or orange streaks, or a rotten smell, and don't taste the raw dough.
