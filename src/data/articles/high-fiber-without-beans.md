I had the fiber speech ready, and then the other person at the table said beans were "a lot." That's the polite version. The pot stays in the cabinet, the lentils look like a dare, and the 30 gram day I keep quoting is built on split peas. Fine. The price file doesn't only contain legumes.

The [30 gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/) prices five ways to land near 30 grams, and the cheap ones lean on beans. This page uses that same file and keeps only the foods that aren't legumes. Thirty-eight rows survive. One day built from seven of them comes to 30.1 grams for $2.31.

## How do you get 30 grams of fiber a day without beans?

Seventy grams of dry rolled oats, two slices of whole wheat bread, half an avocado, one apple, an ounce of almonds, 30 grams of dry pearled barley, and 100 grams of frozen broccoli. That's the day. It adds to 30.1 grams of fiber and $2.31, using only non-legume rows. Beans, lentils, split peas, peanuts, and edamame never get a turn.

The grams below are as purchased. Where the file has an edible fraction under 1, the fiber line multiplies by that fraction, same as a banana with the peel still on. Costs are the row's price per 100 grams, rounded to the cent. Fiber is rounded to a tenth. The day total is the sum of those rounded lines, not a second pass at the raw math.

## The cheap end is flour, barley, and oats

Once the legumes are gone, the top of the list is still a dry-goods aisle. It isn't a berry aisle.

![Horizontal bar chart ranking 13 non-legume foods by grams of fiber per dollar, from whole wheat flour at 77.8 to blackberries at 4.0](/images/high-fiber-without-beans-chart.jpg)

Whole wheat flour returns 77.8 grams of fiber per dollar. Pearled barley returns 57.1. Popcorn kernels return 51.3. Rolled oats return 35.8. Chia seeds return 33.1. Then the chart falls off a cliff. Avocado is 10.4, whole wheat bread is 9.7, frozen broccoli is 9.5, almonds are 8.8, pears are 8.0, apples are 7.3, raspberries are 4.5, and blackberries are 4.0.

Flour wins and then stares at you, because nobody eats flour with a spoon. The day below eats the bread, which is the same wheat after somebody else did the baking and charged for it. Bread is 9.7 grams per dollar. The bag of flour is 77.8. That's the trade. You want the flour number, you turn the oven on.

Popcorn needs a footnote before anyone quotes it at a party. The row is unresolved. USDA publishes no record for unpopped kernels, so the 12.9 grams per 100 grams is derived from air-popped popcorn, [FDC 167959](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/167959/nutrients), using the moisture gap between kernels and popped corn. Thirty grams of kernels, about a bowl once it pops, is 3.9 grams of fiber and 8 cents on that row. I left it off the sample day. An unresolved row can sit in the ranking. It shouldn't pretend to be dinner.

## A bean-free day that lands on 30.1 grams

Three meals and a snack. Every line is a row in the file. Nothing here is a new price check.

Table: Bean-free sample day from non-legume rows

| Meal | Food | Amount | Fiber | Cost | FDC |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Breakfast | Old-fashioned rolled oats | 70 g dry | 7.1 g | $0.20 | [173904](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173904/nutrients) |
| Lunch | 100% whole wheat bread | 52 g, 2 slices | 3.1 g | $0.32 | [172688](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172688/nutrients) |
| Lunch | Avocado | 100 g, half of a 200 g fruit | 5.0 g | $0.48 | [171705](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171705/nutrients) |
| Snack | Apples (gala) | 180 g as purchased | 3.7 g | $0.51 | [168204](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168204/nutrients) |
| Snack | Almonds | 28 g, 1 ounce | 3.5 g | $0.40 | [170567](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170567/nutrients) |
| Dinner | Pearled barley (dry) | 30 g dry | 4.7 g | $0.08 | [170284](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170284/nutrients) |
| Dinner | Frozen broccoli florets | 100 g | 3.0 g | $0.32 | [169968](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169968/nutrients) |
| Day | | | 30.1 g | $2.31 | |

Breakfast is the oats, 7.1 grams, 20 cents. Lunch is the bread plus half an avocado, 8.1 grams and 80 cents. The snack is the apple and the almonds, 7.2 grams and 91 cents. Dinner is a small scoop of barley and a handful of frozen broccoli, 7.7 grams and 40 cents. Add the rounded lines and you get 30.1 grams and $2.31. I checked the addition twice because a penny error in this series becomes a quote.

A few of those portions need the file's own rules, or the grams lie.

The apple is 180 grams as purchased, and the row's edible fraction is 0.9, so the 3.7 grams don't include the core. The avocado is 100 grams of a fruit the file prices at 200 grams and 95 cents, with an edible fraction of 0.74, so the 5.0 grams are the flesh, not the pit. Frozen broccoli is a proxy. [FDC 169968](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169968/nutrients) is chopped frozen broccoli, unprepared, and the bag we priced was florets. Both frozen unprepared cuts in that note publish 3.0 grams per 100 grams, which is why the fiber number doesn't move and the row still says proxy.

The bread price is the one I don't want you to mix up with an older menu. Two slices, 52 grams, cost $0.32 here because the row is $0.621 per 100 grams, the May 2026 BLS average for whole wheat bread. An older day in the [30 gram cost study](/what-30-grams-of-fiber-costs-per-day/) still shows 18 cents for two slices. This page multiplies the row that's in the file now. I didn't relabel May as July.

If you do eat beans, the [7-day high-fiber grocery list](/high-fiber-grocery-list-30-grams/) is the version with split peas and pintos, and it's cheaper for a reason. Split peas do an almost rude amount of work. This page is the version for the week the peas stay in the bag.

One comparison, because the berry box is how this project usually goes wrong. A 6 ounce box of raspberries is the package in the file, 170 grams, [FDC 167755](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/167755/nutrients): 11.1 grams of fiber and $2.48. That's more than the entire $2.31 day, for about a third of the fiber. Buy the berries because you want them. Don't ask them to be the plan.

Chia can still join if you like the texture. About 12 grams, a level tablespoon, is 4.1 grams of fiber and 12 cents from the chia row, [FDC 170554](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170554/nutrients). It isn't in the 30.1. Add it and the day is a different day. Some people call that spoon breakfast. Some people call it wallpaper paste. Both can be right.

Five rows carry a BLS date, and I won't smear July across the rest. Bananas, russet potatoes, and strawberries are the July 2026 US average. Oranges are June 2026. Whole wheat bread is May 2026. The other prices are the Walmart lines already in the file, and those lines don't have their own month stamp. Your receipt can disagree. That's the deal with a national snapshot.

Whole wheat spaghetti at 35.4 grams per dollar is the quiet one. It's dry pasta, [FDC 169738](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169738/nutrients), and it outranks every fruit on the board. If the day above bores you, a bowl of that pasta with the broccoli is still on the non-legume list. I just didn't need a second menu to show that 30 grams doesn't require a bean.

## What I'd buy on the week the beans stay put

Oats, a bag of barley, a loaf of whole wheat bread, one avocado, a couple of apples, a small bag of almonds, and the frozen broccoli you already own. That's the $2.31 day, and it's a day, not a personality. If your current week is low on fiber, add this slowly and drink water with it. The 30.1 is a shopping sum from a price file. It isn't an instruction about your gut.

The berries can wait for a weekend. The flour can wait for a baking day. The beans can wait until somebody at the table changes their mind. The oats don't need a vote.
