I bought the broccoli crowns. Then I bought them again, because the first bunch had become a science experiment in the crisper. The freezer bag was $2.87 and it was still a bag.

Five frozen vegetables are on the fiber sheet. [Frozen green peas](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170016/nutrients) lead at 17.6 g of fiber per dollar, and the same bag is 20.4 g of protein per dollar. [Frozen broccoli florets](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169968/nutrients) are 9.5 g of fiber per dollar. [Fresh broccoli crowns](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170379/nutrients) are 6.1 g. That's 55.7% more fiber per dollar in the bag than in the crown, on these two rows.

Five bags, plus the fresh crown so the broccoli comparison isn't a slogan. Orange is fiber per dollar. Slate is protein per dollar, and it only shows up where the protein sheet has the food. The dashed line is the fresh crown, at 6.1 g.

## Frozen broccoli versus the crown you meant to eat

The bag is $2.87 for 32 ounces. The crown is $1.94 a pound. Frozen florets use [FDC 169968](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169968/nutrients), broccoli frozen, chopped, unprepared, at 3.0 g of fiber per 100 g. USDA doesn't publish a florets cut. The spears record prints the same 3.0 g, so the cut style isn't the trick. The row stays a proxy because the label says florets and the record says chopped.

The crown is a proxy too. [FDC 170379](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170379/nutrients) is raw broccoli, a whole-head composite, at 2.6 g per 100 g and 6.1 g per dollar. Divide the gap: 9.5 minus 6.1, divided by 6.1, is 55.7%. The bag wins the receipt. It doesn't become a different vegetable.

The rest of the produce case is a different argument. Carrots and cabbage walk past most of the freezer on the [fruit and vegetable fiber ranking](/cheapest-high-fiber-fruits-and-vegetables/). This page is only the five bags, with the crown standing there so the broccoli claim has a number next to it.

## Peas do both jobs. Edamame's fiber number doesn't.

Peas are the one bag I'd buy if I could only keep one. A 32 ounce bag at $2.32, 4.5 g of fiber per 100 g, 5.22 g of protein per 100 g, 108 mg of sodium, exact match on [FDC 170016](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170016/nutrients). Seventeen point six grams of fiber per dollar and 20.4 g of protein per dollar. That's the double.

Edamame is the awkward one. The protein sheet is clean: 18.0 g of protein per dollar, 11.22 g per 100 g, 6 mg of sodium, a 12 ounce bag at $2.12, [FDC 168410](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168410/nutrients). The fiber sheet prints 8.3 g per dollar and then tells you not to trust the record. The 5.2 g per 100 g behind that 8.3 is the cooked edamame, FDC 168411. The bag on the shelf is unprepared. FDC 168410 lists 4.8 g of fiber per 100 g. Divide 4.8 by the sheet's $0.624 per 100 g and you get 7.7 g of fiber per dollar. I left 8.3 in the table because that's the printed figure. I didn't hang FDC 168410 on it.

## Green beans and spinach are fiber bags

Frozen green beans are 10.8 g of fiber per dollar from a 32 ounce bag at $2.18, 2.6 g per 100 g on [FDC 169962](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169962/nutrients). No protein row. No sodium row. The raw fresh bean is a different record, and this page doesn't borrow it. The bag is the frozen one.

Frozen chopped spinach is 8.4 g of fiber per dollar from a 12 ounce bag at $1.18, 2.9 g per 100 g, 74 mg of sodium, [FDC 169287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169287/nutrients). Also no protein row. It's a cheap little box of fiber. It isn't a protein strategy, and the sodium isn't nothing.

## How long the bag keeps

[FoodKeeper](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/foodkeeper-app) gives purchased frozen vegetables 8 months in the freezer for quality, then 3 to 4 days in the fridge after thawing. [Food held at 0°F can be kept indefinitely](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts), and the month count is quality, not a date the bag becomes unsafe. A bag that sat out is a different problem. The refreeze rules live on [can you refreeze frozen vegetables after thawing](/can-you-refreeze-frozen-vegetables-after-thawing/).

Buy the peas if you want one bag that scores on both fiber and protein. Buy the frozen broccoli if the crown keeps dying in the drawer, and stop apologizing to the produce section. Leave the edamame fiber bar marked unresolved until the sheet is.
