How long does sourdough discard last in the fridge? Plan on one week from the oldest date on the jar, then bake it or freeze it. That week is the planning window already used on the [beginner discard recipes](/easy-sourdough-discard-recipes-beginners/). It isn't a safety limit, and it isn't a date until which every jar is fine.

A jar without a date is just a beige mystery.

## How long does sourdough discard last in the fridge?

Use it within a week, or freeze measured portions if you won't. King Arthur says a fridge jar lasts several weeks. Other writers say about a week, or two. When they disagree, this site keeps the shorter planning window and refuses to invent a "safe until" date of its own.

Young discard, from a starter that isn't established, isn't a storage question at all. [Don't bake with that jar yet](/can-you-use-sourdough-discard-from-a-new-starter/). The week starts after you have a starter that rises predictably after feeding.

## Why published answers range from one week to several weeks

They aren't measuring the same thing. Some are talking about quality. Some use the word "safely" for a number no lab report on this page backs up. Here's what each one actually prints.

Plan the fridge jar for one week from the oldest discard in it. That sits at the short end of the table. It doesn't turn King Arthur's "several weeks" into a promise, and it doesn't turn day eight into a hazard by itself. Past that week, freeze it or bake it if it still looks and smells like discard. Mold, pink, orange, or a rotten smell overrides the date.

The counter isn't a fifth column. King Arthur says leave it out only if you're using it that day. Pantry Mama says it gets very sour after a few days on the counter. sourdoughstarter.com says a day, maybe two, and calls 48 hours borderline. If you aren't baking today, put the lid on and refrigerate it.

Those fridge and freezer set-points for the rest of the kitchen live in [food storage temperature rules](/food-storage-temperature-rules/). That page is about leftovers and groceries. It isn't a discard chart, and USDA's leftover times don't transfer to this jar.

## Label the jar: a date system that actually works

Write the oldest collection date on tape and stick it on the jar. Nordic Ware and sourdoughstarter.com both say to label the date. The beginner recipes add the rule that matters when you keep topping the jar up. Adding fresh discard doesn't reset what's already in there.

Pantry Mama says combining days can lengthen how long she keeps a jar, and she still wants it inside a week or two. sourdoughstarter.com says add fresh discard only if the older batch is still inside its window. A spoonful from this morning doesn't launder last month's jar. The oldest date runs the week.

That label is what a [simple freezer inventory](/freezer-inventory-simple-system/) is for once a portion is frozen. A block with no date is how the back of the freezer becomes a museum.

## Signs to throw it out

The overlap is the rule. Mold, pink or orange, or a rotten smell means throw it out. King Arthur's create guide uses that list for starter, and the discard article repeats pink or orange. Nordic Ware says an unpleasant odor, mold, or unusual color. Pantry Mama says mold or pink or orange.

Hooch, the dark liquid on top, isn't that list. King Arthur says stir the black liquid back in. sourdoughstarter.com says hooch means hungry, not ruined, and that a slimy texture is the thing to toss. Acetone is the split. If the jar looks wrong, throw it out. If it only smells sharply hungry and you're still inside the week, that's a flavor issue, covered below.

## Can you freeze sourdough discard?

Yes, if this week isn't when you'll bake. Freeze portions of **57 grams, 113 grams, or 227 grams**. Those are the quarter cup, half cup, and cup on this site's [conversion guide](/how-to-measure-sourdough-discard-grams/), so a frozen block matches the flatbread, the pancakes, the crackers, or the pizza crust instead of becoming a beige ice brick you have to chisel.

Nordic Ware says to freeze it in measured amounts for up to 2 to 3 months, then thaw it in the refrigerator overnight or at room temperature. sourdoughstarter.com says up to 3 months, with headspace in the container because discard expands, and they mention ice-cube portions of about 2 tablespoons if you want smaller pieces. King Arthur's discard article doesn't publish a freezer duration, so this page doesn't invent one for them.

## Thawing and using frozen discard

Thaw it overnight in the fridge. That's the step Nordic Ware and sourdoughstarter.com both describe. Nordic Ware also allows thawing at room temperature. sourdoughstarter.com says not to leave it out that way, not to microwave it, to use it within 3 days of thawing, and not to refreeze it. The overnight fridge thaw keeps you inside both instructions. Stir before you weigh it. Cold discard separates, and the liquid on top isn't the whole block.

## Which recipe suits older discard versus fresher discard

Inside the week, fresher discard is the milder one. Nordic Ware says discard less than a week old is mild enough for sweet or savory, and that older discard tastes stronger and suits savory bakes like crackers or flatbreads. Pantry Mama makes the same split. Fresh for sweet recipes, older for savory, and she still wants that older jar inside two weeks.

On this site, that points at pancakes when the jar is recent, and at the skillet flatbread or the cracker sheet when it's been sitting. The [amount planner](/what-to-make-with-sourdough-discard-by-amount/) is the gram ladder for those batches. That Sourdough Gal's 1,000 gram waffles are her own note, not this rule. She wants that discard around 10 days old or less.

Sour and "gone bad" aren't the same sentence. A sharper jar can still be discard. A moldy one can't. And discard still isn't a ripe starter.
