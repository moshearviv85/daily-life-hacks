I have done the second-store lap. Store one for the loss-leader chicken, store two because the eggs looked friendlier in the ad, store three because I was already in the car and the sunk-cost fairy was driving. The receipt was a little smaller. The afternoon was gone.

Splitting grocery trips is worth the gas only when the basket you actually buy is cheaper by more than the extra miles. At the [IRS business mileage rate](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates) for July through December 2026, that rate is 76 cents a mile. Ten extra round-trip miles cost $7.60. Fifteen cost $11.40. A $35 run has to be about 22 percent cheaper before those 10 miles pay for themselves.

## What does an extra grocery mile actually cost?

The pump is not the whole mile. [AAA's 2026 Your Driving Costs fact sheet](https://newsroom.aaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/AAA_YDC-Fact-Sheet_2026.pdf) puts fuel at 17.3 cents a mile for a new car driven 15,000 miles a year. Regular gas in that study averaged $4.152 a gallon over the 12 months ending May 2026. Maintenance, repair, and tires are another 11.7 cents, so fuel plus upkeep is 29 cents a mile. The full ownership average at that mileage is 86 cents, and that one includes depreciation, insurance, and finance. I wouldn't charge a grocery detour for the insurance you pay if the car sits in the driveway. I would charge the miles for fuel, upkeep, and the IRS wear rate.

The IRS figure is the clean one for "what did this detour cost." It was 72.5 cents from January through June 2026, then 76 cents from July through December. Today sits in the second half, so the chart uses 76 cents. The first-half rate is the smaller bar you don't get to pick just because it makes the trip look smarter.

| Extra round-trip miles | AAA fuel, 17.3 cents | AAA fuel plus upkeep, 29 cents | IRS business, 76 cents |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | $0.35 | $0.58 | $1.52 |
| 5 | $0.87 | $1.45 | $3.80 |
| 10 | $1.73 | $2.90 | $7.60 |
| 15 | $2.60 | $4.35 | $11.40 |

Two miles is a rounding error if you tell the story in gas alone. It's $1.52 once the IRS rate is the one on the form. I still wouldn't drive two extra miles for a 40-cent yogurt. I would drive them for a basket that's actually $4 cheaper.

## When is splitting grocery trips worth the gas?

Same miles, three basket sizes. The percent is the IRS cost divided by the basket, rounded half up to a tenth. This is the gap the second store has to beat on the same foods, not on a cart that quietly swapped salmon for beans.

| Basket | 2 miles, $1.52 | 5 miles, $3.80 | 10 miles, $7.60 | 15 miles, $11.40 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $35 | 4.3 percent | 10.9 percent | 21.7 percent | 32.6 percent |
| $75 | 2.0 percent | 5.1 percent | 10.1 percent | 15.2 percent |
| $150 | 1.0 percent | 2.5 percent | 5.1 percent | 7.6 percent |

A $35 Tuesday shop dies fast. Ten extra miles eat about 22 percent of it. You're not finding a 22 percent gap on the same milk, bread, and bananas unless one store is having a genuine tantrum of a sale. A $150 stock-up can survive 10 miles if the identical list is at least $7.60 cheaper. Fifteen miles asks for $11.40. That's a real sale, not a vibe.

Count only the miles the second store added. If the cheap store is on the way home from work, the detour might be two miles, not fifteen. Charging the calculator for a commute you were already making is how a sensible errand gets framed for a crime it didn't commit. The flip side is the "while I'm out" third store. That one is almost never on the way.

## A real price gap, from the files

protein-per-dollar-2026.csv prices boneless chicken breast at $4.16 a pound and bone-in drumsticks at $5.46 for a 5 pound bag. The bag is $1.09 a purchased pound. Two purchased pounds of breast are $8.32. Two purchased pounds of drumsticks are $2.18. The gap is $6.14.

That $6.14 clears a 5-mile IRS detour ($3.80) and loses to a 10-mile one ($7.60). And it isn't even a same-store coupon. The breast line is the July 2026 BLS U.S. average. The drumstick bag is a July 2026 Walmart price. If both are already in the store you're standing in, you don't need the car at all. You need the other cooler. The [Walmart versus BLS protein and fiber page](/how-much-fiber-and-protein-per-dollar-at-walmart-vs-bls/) is the place those two price bases get compared on purpose.

The national tape moved less than the dashed line. In bls-nutrition-per-dollar-monthly.csv, U.S. city average, one pound of breast, one pound of 80/20 ground beef, and one dozen eggs were $13.23 in July 2026 and $13.17 in October. Six cents. Two extra miles at 76 cents are $1.52, about 25 times that wiggle. I'm not driving across town to catch a nickel the national average already printed.

## Fuel only is the argument that flatters the trip

Seventeen cents a mile feels like permission. Ten miles of AAA fuel is $1.73. Ten miles of fuel plus upkeep is $2.90. Ten miles at the IRS rate is $7.60. People quote the first number because it's the one on the gas-station sign. The tires and the resale value don't print a receipt at the pump, and they still show up.

I use 76 cents when the question is whether the extra store paid. The 86-cent full ownership figure is the wrong stick for a detour. It spreads insurance and a car payment across miles you were going to drive anyway. Tolls and parking sit on top of every rate. A $3 garage under the "cheap" store just ate a 2-mile trip before you touched a cart.

## What I do instead of a second parking lot

One list, one store, the cheap calories first. [How to make grocery shopping cheaper](/how-to-make-grocery-shopping-cheaper/) is the aisle version of that, and it's explicitly not a four-store scavenger hunt. The weekly yardstick, if you want to know whether the total is actually high, is [what the USDA thrifty week costs](/usda-thrifty-food-plan-weekly-cost/). The cart that clears a family's protein and fiber without a convenience tax is the [cheapest family grocery basket](/cheapest-family-grocery-basket-for-protein-and-fiber/).

If a second store is truly on the way, and the same list is cheaper by more than 76 cents times the extra miles, go. Write the gap down once. If you can't name a number bigger than the miles, you're sightseeing with a shopping cart.
