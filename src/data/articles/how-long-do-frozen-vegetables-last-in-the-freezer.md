Frozen vegetables held constantly at 0F stay safe indefinitely, according to USDA's [FoodSafety.gov cold food storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts). Quality is the thing with a clock on it, and that clock runs somewhere between 8 and 12 months depending on which agency you ask. The bag from two winters ago probably won't hurt you. It might taste like the freezer.

That distinction matters more than people realize. Safety and quality are two different questions, and the bag doesn't tell you which one you're asking.

## How long do frozen vegetables last in the freezer for good quality?

The sources don't fully agree, which is worth knowing before you throw out a perfectly fine bag of peas.

| Source | Frozen vegetable storage time | What the time means |
|---|---|---|
| [USDA FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) | Indefinite at 0F | Safety |
| [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/general-freezing-information/how-long-can-i-store-frozen-foods/) | 8 to 12 months | Quality |
| [NDSU Extension](https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/extension/publications/freezing-vegetables) | Within 12 months | Quality |
| [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-safety/freezer-storage) | 8 months | Quality |

*Sources: as linked. All quality figures assume a freezer held at 0F or below.*

The spread is 8 months to a year, and that's not sloppiness. It's the honest range. USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service is blunt about the underlying rule in its [freezing and food safety guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety): "Food stored constantly at 0 °F will always be safe. Only the quality suffers with lengthy freezer storage."

So pick the conservative number if you care about texture. Pick the safety rule if you're standing over the trash can with a bag of green beans and a guilty conscience.

## Does the temperature actually matter that much?

It's the whole ballgame. Every one of those storage times assumes 0F, and most home freezers are not audited by anybody.

FSIS makes a specific carve-out for the freezer compartment in a refrigerator: if it can't hold zero degrees, or if the door gets opened all day long, use it for short-term storage and eat that food sooner. Illinois Extension recommends putting a thermometer between the frozen packages, waiting 5 to 8 hours, and adjusting the control if the reading isn't 0 to 2F.

A freezer thermometer costs less than one bag of frozen edamame. I put one in mine after a summer where everything tasted faintly like ice, and the reading was 12F. That explained a lot.

## Is freezer burn dangerous?

No, and this is the single most useful thing to know about a freezer. FSIS states that freezer burn "does not make food unsafe, merely dry in spots," describes it as grayish-brown leathery patches caused by air touching the surface, and says to cut the burned portions away either before or after cooking.

Heavily burned food may not be worth serving on its own. FSIS suggests that food that's past its best but not spoiled can go into soups or stews, which is exactly where a sad bag of mixed vegetables belongs anyway. Nobody has ever noticed a slightly tired carrot in a pot of chili.

If a bag develops an off or rancid odor, that's the signal to throw it out. FSIS says to check the odor first when a food isn't on the storage chart.

## What about a power outage?

FSIS gives real numbers here: a full freezer will usually keep about 2 days if the door stays shut, and a half-full freezer about a day. Grouping the packages close together helps them hold the cold.

When the power comes back, the test is temperature, not time. Food that's still partly frozen, still carries ice crystals, or is as cold as a refrigerator at 40F is safe to refreeze or use. Anything that's been warmer than 40F for more than 2 hours gets discarded. Buy the bag you'll actually finish inside a year, and don't buy a second one because it was on sale.
