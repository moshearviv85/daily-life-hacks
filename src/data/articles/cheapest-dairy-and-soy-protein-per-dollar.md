The dairy case wants you to believe a block of mozzarella and a tub of yogurt are the same idea in different plastic. They aren't. One of them is a salt lick that happens to contain protein. The other is the protein with the salt mostly left out.

Eggs lead this nine-food list at 34.4 g of protein per dollar ([FDC 171287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients)). Part-skim mozzarella is close behind at 30.1 g, then carries 666 mg of sodium per 100 g ([FDC 171244](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171244/nutrients)). Plain nonfat Greek yogurt is 27.5 g per dollar and 36 mg of sodium ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients)). That gap, not the sticker on the tub, is the shopping decision.

## Which dairy and soy foods give the best protein per dollar without excess sodium?

Eggs win the dollar and miss the sodium line by 2 mg. Greek yogurt is the best dairy row that clears 140 mg per 100 g. Among the soy foods, frozen edamame leads at 18.0 g per dollar and 6 mg of sodium. The chart colors a row orange when the sodium file shows 140 mg or less, deep orange when it's over, and slate when that file has no row at all.

![Bar chart of protein grams per dollar for nine dairy and soy foods, from eggs at 34.4 grams to tempeh at 13.2 grams, colored by sodium per 100 grams](/images/cheapest-dairy-and-soy-protein-per-dollar-chart.jpg)

The six dairy rows are dairy-protein-per-dollar-ranked-2026.csv. Edamame, tofu, and tempeh are the matching rows in protein-per-dollar-2026.csv. Sodium is sodium-per-dollar-2026.csv, joined on the USDA record. DIAAS is protein-quality-per-dollar-2026.csv, and only where that file actually has the food. Those files live in the site's public data folder. Nothing here links out to them.

[21 CFR 101.61](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-D/section-101.61) is where FDA sets a low-sodium label at 140 mg or less per reference amount customarily consumed. Meal and main-dish products use 140 mg per 100 g. This table uses 140 mg per 100 g so a block of cheese and a block of tofu share one ruler. A real package label can land on the other side of the line when the serving isn't 100 g. That's a grocery screen, not a diet you were assigned.

## Eggs win the dollar, then the sodium line gets picky

A dozen large eggs, counted as 600 g edible, was $2.19 in the July 2026 BLS average. That's 34.4 g of protein per dollar and 12.56 g per 100 g ([FDC 171287](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients)). The sodium sheet lists the same record at 142 mg per 100 g. Two milligrams over a round number doesn't make an egg a salt bomb. It does keep the egg off the orange bars. The sodium file's egg price is the May 2026 BLS series. The protein figure above is July. The milligrams are the USDA record, not the month on the receipt.

Whole milk is the next dairy row people already have in the door: 28.5 g of protein per dollar, 3.15 g per 100 g, a gallon at $4.31 in the July 2026 BLS average ([FDC 171265](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171265/nutrients)). Cottage cheese is 26.3 g per dollar, 11.12 g per 100 g, a 24 oz tub at $2.87 ([FDC 172179](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/172179/nutrients)). Neither food is in sodium-per-dollar-2026.csv. I'm not going to borrow a milligram from memory and call it a source. The quality file does score both, at a milk-protein DIAAS of 1.14. If you want the spoon-versus-spoon version of yogurt and cottage cheese, it's [cottage cheese versus Greek yogurt](/cottage-cheese-vs-greek-yogurt-protein-uses/).

Greek yogurt is the dairy row I'd actually point at when sodium is the constraint. Plain, nonfat, 10.19 g of protein per 100 g, 27.5 g per dollar, 36 mg of sodium, 32 oz at $3.36 ([FDC 170894](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/170894/nutrients)). It gives up 6.9 g per dollar against the eggs. It gives up the salt problem. That's a trade I'll take on a Tuesday.

## Mozzarella and cheddar are the salt tax

Mozzarella looks like a win until you read the sodium column out loud. Low-moisture part-skim is 23.75 g of protein per 100 g and 30.1 g per dollar, a 16 oz block at $3.58 ([FDC 171244](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171244/nutrients)). The sodium sheet says 666 mg per 100 g. Cheddar is 22.87 g of protein per 100 g and 18.1 g per dollar at $5.75 per pound in the July 2026 BLS average ([FDC 173414](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/173414/nutrients)). Sodium on that record is 653 mg per 100 g. The sodium file prices cheddar with the May series, at $5.69 a pound. This page keeps the July protein figure and the USDA milligrams. It doesn't mix May's price into July's grams per dollar.

Set those cheeses next to the quiet rows and the cut is the whole story. Greek yogurt's 36 mg is 630 mg under mozzarella's 666 mg, which is 94.6% less. Tofu's 4 mg is 662 mg under that same 666, which is 99.4% less ([FDC 174290](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174290/nutrients)). Against cheddar's 653 mg, yogurt's 36 mg is 94.5% less and tofu's 4 mg is 99.4% less. Edamame at 6 mg and tempeh at 9 mg sit inside that same 94.5% to 99.4% span. The cheese didn't become evil. It became a seasoning you pay for by the block.

The quality file scores mozzarella at a DIAAS of 1.14, a milk-protein proxy from Mathai and colleagues in 2017 and FAO 2013. It doesn't have a DIAAS row for cheddar. I won't copy 1.14 onto the cheddar block just because both foods started as milk. If the recipe's expensive line is the cheese, price the batch in the [recipe cost calculator](/tools/recipe-cost-calculator/) before the block is already shredded into the bowl.

## Soy is the quiet end of the chart

Frozen shelled edamame is the soy food that still competes with cheddar on protein per dollar: 18.0 g, against cheddar's 18.1 g. The sodium is 6 mg per 100 g, protein is 11.22 g per 100 g, and the bag in the price file is 12 oz at $2.12 ([FDC 168410](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168410/nutrients)). It's frozen, so a dead freezer is a different problem. The record says unprepared. Don't tell yourself it's a shelf-stable snack. The quality file has no DIAAS for it. The sodium file does, and 6 mg is the number.

Extra-firm tofu is the low-sodium extreme and a modest dollar. A 14 oz block at $2.92 works out to 13.6 g of protein per dollar, 9.98 g per 100 g, and 4 mg of sodium ([FDC 174290](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174290/nutrients)). DIAAS on the quality sheet is 0.90, from Herreman and colleagues in 2020, cited there as soy. The sheet multiplies through and lands on 12.2 g per dollar adjusted. That's the honest tofu number if you care about the score. The 13.6 g is the one that matches the other rows on this chart, before quality gets a vote.

Tempeh is denser in the package and weaker per dollar. [FDC 174272](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/174272/nutrients) is 20.29 g of protein per 100 g, more than double the tofu's 9.98 g, and 13.2 g per dollar because an 8 oz pack was $3.48. Sodium is 9 mg per 100 g. No DIAAS row. I'd buy it because I want tempeh, not because it beat a dozen eggs at the register.

The wider list, including foods that aren't dairy or soy, is the [protein per dollar ranking](/protein-per-dollar-adjusted-for-quality/). The 25 foods that already clear 140 mg per 100 g are [low-sodium high-protein foods per dollar](/low-sodium-high-protein-foods-per-dollar/). Greek yogurt, edamame, and tofu are on that list. Eggs and the two cheeses aren't.

## Where these numbers come from

Nine foods. Six from the dairy file, three soy rows from the protein file, sodium only where the sodium file shares the USDA record, DIAAS only where the quality file shares it. Eggs at 1.13 and the milk-protein scores at 1.14 are capped at 1.00 when that file multiplies, so their adjusted grams per dollar don't rise above the raw figure. Tofu's 0.90 does change the number, down to 12.2 g per dollar. Cheddar, edamame, and tempeh stay blank on purpose.

Prices are the July 2026 basket already in the dairy and protein files: BLS for eggs, milk, and cheddar, Walmart for the rest. The sodium milligrams are USDA FoodData Central for the form named on the row. Edible portion and the BLS series are written up on the [methodology page](/methodology/). A dollar of mozzarella buys a lot of protein and a lot of salt. A dollar of yogurt buys a little less protein and almost none of that salt. Pick the one your actual Tuesday can live with.
