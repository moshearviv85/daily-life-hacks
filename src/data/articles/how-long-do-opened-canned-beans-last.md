Three to four days in the fridge, in a covered container, not in the can. That is the window [FoodSafety.gov gives for refrigerated leftovers](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts), and an opened can of beans becomes a leftover the second the lid comes off.

Half a can is not a big loss. It's also not nothing, and half cans die in the back of the fridge constantly.

## How long do opened canned beans last in the fridge?

Three to four days, same as any cooked leftover. Get them in there fast. The FDA's [two hour rule](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely) covers this: foods that need refrigeration shouldn't sit at room temperature longer than two hours, or one hour if the room is above 90 degrees F. That clock started when you opened the can, not when you finished dinner.

Keep the fridge at or below 40 degrees F while you're at it. The FDA is blunt that this is the single most useful thing you can do about spoilage bacteria, and a cheap appliance thermometer will tell you more about your fridge than the dial on the back wall will.

If you're at the point where half cans keep dying, the real fix is buying differently. Dry beans cook in the amount you decide, which is part of why they returned 81.0 grams of protein per dollar against 30.1 for the canned version in [our canned versus dry comparison](/canned-vs-dry-beans-cost/).

## Can you store canned beans in the can?

Move them. It's not that the can turns dangerous, it's that an open can is a bad container: no seal, exposed metal at the cut edge, and a surface layer of beans drying out in the air. Leftovers stored that way pick up a faint tinny flavor within a day.

Any covered container works. Keep the liquid with them if you're not eating them right away, since it stops the top layer going leathery. Rinse later, when they actually go into food.

The other honest reason to decant: you can see them. Beans in a clear container get used. Beans in a jagged aluminum can behind the milk get discovered next Thursday.

## How long are canned beans good after the best by date?

Longer than most people assume, and the FDA says so directly. A use by date is the manufacturer's call on best flavor or quality, and it is not a food safety date. Past that date a can may shift in taste, color, texture, or nutrient content while still being wholesome.

What actually matters is the can. Swelling, leaks, punctures, deep rust, or a dent bad enough that the can won't stack or open normally are all reasons to throw it out rather than risk it. A scuffed label and a two year old date stamp are not.

This is the quiet argument for keeping cans around at all. Canned black beans were the cheapest package in our [no-cook protein ranking](/no-cook-protein-per-dollar/) at 88 cents, and they hold that value for years in a cupboard, which almost nothing else in the store does. Dry beans hold even longer, and we went through that in [how long dried beans last](/how-long-do-dried-beans-last/).

## How do you know when to throw them out?

Trust the obvious signals and don't get clever. Sour smell, slime, mold, fizzing, or a lid that pops when you open it. Any of those and it goes.

The harder case is the can you're not sure about. The FDA is worth quoting on this one: food can make you sick even when it doesn't look, smell, or taste spoiled, because the bacteria that cause illness are different from the ones that make food go bad. That's the whole argument for putting a date on the container instead of relying on your nose four days later. A strip of masking tape and a marker costs nothing.

## What to do with half a can instead

Use it the same night. That's the only storage strategy that always works.

Half a can of black beans is about 13.2 grams of protein for 44 cents, which is enough to turn a plate of rice into an actual meal. The whole can only cost 88 cents to begin with, and we broke down what that buys in [how much a can of beans costs](/how-much-is-a-can-of-beans/). Push it into [weeknight tacos](/easy-black-bean-tacos-weeknight-dinner/), fold it into eggs, or drop it cold onto a salad. Chickpea leftovers go into [homemade hummus](/high-fiber-hummus-recipe-homemade/), which also buys you another few days in a sealed container.

If the fridge window is going to run out, freeze it flat in its liquid at 0 degrees F. Thawed beans lose a little firmness, so they belong in [three bean chili](/hearty-vegetarian-chili-with-three-beans-and-corn/) or a soup rather than in a salad where texture is the whole point.

Beans are cheap enough that nobody's budget collapses over a wasted half can. It's still the easiest 44 cents in the kitchen to stop throwing away, and a covered container and a date on the lid handles it.
